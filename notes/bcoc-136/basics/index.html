Basic Concepts, Casual & Agricultural Income

Critical Asked in 8 of 9 papers · avg 17 marks

Previous year and assessment year, person and assessee, GTI vs total income, casual income, agricultural income and partial integration, exempt incomes.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 1 Basic Concepts-IBlock 1 · Unit 2 Basic Concepts-IIBlock 1 · Unit 4 Exempted Incomes

Key points

  • Income of the previous year (PY) is taxed in the assessment year (AY) that follows it. Exceptions (taxed in the same year): non-resident shipping companies, persons leaving India, bodies formed for a short duration, assets transferred to avoid tax, discontinued business.
  • Person (Sec 2(31)): individual, HUF, company, firm, AOP/BOI, local authority, artificial juridical person. Assessee: person by whom tax is payable; assessee in default fails to pay or deduct tax.
  • Gross total income = total of the five heads after clubbing and set-off. Total income = GTI − deductions under Chapter VI-A.
  • Casual income: lottery, crossword puzzles, races, card games, gambling, TV game shows — taxed at a flat 30% with no deduction of expenses or basic exemption.
  • Agricultural income (Sec 10(1)) is exempt: rent or revenue from agricultural land in India, income from agriculture, farm buildings. Partly agricultural: tea (40% business income), coffee (25%), rubber (35%).
  • Partial integration: when a non-corporate assessee has agricultural income above ₹5,000 and non-agricultural income above the basic exemption limit, agricultural income is used to fix the tax rate.
  • Exempt incomes u/s 10: agricultural income, share of profit from a firm, HUF receipts, gratuity/commuted pension (limits), leave encashment (limits), scholarships, PPF interest, etc.
  • Capital receipts are generally not taxable; revenue receipts are taxable unless exempt.

Worked examples

Example: previous year and assessment year

Income earned from 1 April 2024 to 31 March 2025 (the previous year) is assessed and taxed in assessment year 2025-26. A person leaving India for good in the middle of the year can instead be taxed in the same year.

Example: partly agricultural income and casual income

A company grows and manufactures tea and earns ₹10 lakh. 40% (₹4 lakh) is business income and taxed; 60% (₹6 lakh) is agricultural income and exempt.

A person wins ₹1,00,000 in a TV game show. It is casual income, taxed at a flat 30% with no expenses deducted and no basic exemption.

How to solve

  1. Agricultural income from land: sale proceeds − cultivation expenses (seeds, fertiliser, labour, depreciation, electricity).

Formulas

Total income = GTI − Chapter VI-A deductions
