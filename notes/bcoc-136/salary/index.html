Income from Salaries

Critical Asked in 9 of 9 papers · avg 37 marks

Computation of taxable salary: allowances (HRA, entertainment), perquisites (house, car, servants), gratuity, pension, leave encashment, provident funds.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 5 Salaries-IBlock 2 · Unit 6 Salaries-IIBlock 2 · Unit 7 Salaries-III

Key points

  • Salary includes basic pay, DA, bonus, commission, fees, allowances, perquisites, retirement benefits, advance salary (taxed when received).
  • Allowances: fully taxable (DA, city compensatory, overtime, servant, medical); partly exempt (HRA, children education, hostel); entertainment allowance — deduction u/s 16(ii) only for government employees.
  • HRA exemption (Sec 10(13A)) = least of: actual HRA received; rent paid − 10% of salary; 50% of salary in Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai (40% elsewhere). Salary = basic + DA (forming part of retirement benefits) + commission at a fixed % of turnover.
  • Perquisites: rent-free house (a % of salary depending on city population), car (depends on engine capacity and private/official use), servants (sweeper, gardener, cook — actual cost), free gas, electricity and water.
  • Gratuity (Sec 10(10)) — covered by the Gratuity Act: least of actual, 15/26 × last month's salary × completed years (part over 6 months counts as a year), statutory limit. Not covered: least of actual, ½ × average salary of last 10 months × completed years, limit.
  • Commuted pension (Sec 10(10A)): government employees — fully exempt; others — ⅓ of full commuted value exempt if gratuity also received, ½ if not. Uncommuted pension is fully taxable.
  • Leave encashment on retirement: least of actual, 10 months' average salary, cash equivalent of unavailed leave (max 30 days per year of service), statutory limit.
  • Provident funds: statutory (fully exempt), recognised (employer's contribution exempt up to 12% of salary; interest up to 9.5%), unrecognised, public provident fund.
  • Deductions from gross salary: standard deduction, entertainment allowance (government), professional tax (Sec 16(iii)).

Worked examples

Example: HRA exemption (Mumbai)

Basic ₹30,000 a month and DA ₹10,000 (part of retirement benefits), so salary for HRA = ₹4,80,000 a year. HRA received ₹1,80,000; rent paid ₹2,16,000.

Exempt = least of: actual HRA ₹1,80,000; rent − 10% of salary = 2,16,000 − 48,000 = ₹1,68,000; 50% of salary (metro) = ₹2,40,000 → ₹1,68,000.

Taxable HRA = 1,80,000 − 1,68,000 = ₹12,000.

Example: gratuity and commuted pension on retirement

An employee covered by the Gratuity Act retires after 20 years 7 months; last month's salary (basic + DA) ₹50,000; gratuity received ₹8,00,000 (well below the statutory limit).

Service counts as 21 years (over 6 months rounds up). Exempt = least of 8,00,000 and 15/26 × 50,000 × 21 = ₹6,05,769 → taxable ₹1,94,231.

The same (non-government) employee commutes half the pension for ₹3,00,000, so the full commuted value is ₹6,00,000. With gratuity received, ⅓ of that, ₹2,00,000, is exempt → ₹1,00,000 taxable.

How to solve

  1. List basic, DA, bonus, commission → add taxable allowances (show HRA working) → add perquisites → gross salary.
  2. Less deductions u/s 16 → income under the head Salaries.
  3. For retirement benefits, compute each exemption in a separate working note.

Formulas

HRA exempt = min(HRA, Rent − 10% of salary, 50%/40% of salary)
Gratuity (covered) = min(actual, 15/26 × salary × years, limit)
Commuted pension exempt = ⅓ (with gratuity) or ½ (without) of full commuted value

Common mistakes

  • Using basic pay only for HRA 'salary' — add DA that forms part of retirement benefits and commission on turnover.
  • Not rounding service years (over 6 months = full year) for covered gratuity.
