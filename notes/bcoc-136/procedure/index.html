Return Filing, PAN, Assessment & Authorities

High Asked in 9 of 9 papers · avg 13 marks

e-filing steps and benefits, consequences of late filing, PAN, best judgement assessment, income tax authorities.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 5 · Unit 18 Filing of Return and Tax AuthoritiesBlock 5 · Unit 19 Online Filing of ReturnsBlock 5 · Unit 20 Leading Cases Decided by Supreme Court

Key points

  • e-Filing steps: register/log in on the income tax portal with PAN → choose the correct ITR form (e.g. ITR-1 Sahaj for salary, one house property, other sources up to the limit) → fill or use pre-filled data → compute and pay tax → submit → e-verify (Aadhaar OTP, net banking, DSC) within the time allowed.
  • Benefits: loan and visa proof of income, claim refunds, carry forward losses, avoid penalties, quick processing.
  • Late filing: late fee u/s 234F, interest u/s 234A, some losses cannot be carried forward, belated return u/s 139(4).
  • PAN (Sec 139A): 10-character alphanumeric number; required for filing returns and many financial transactions.
  • Best judgement assessment (Sec 144): when the assessee fails to file a return or comply with notices, the AO assesses to the best of judgement.
  • Income tax authorities: CBDT, Principal Chief Commissioners/Chief Commissioners, Commissioners (incl. Appeals), Joint/Additional Commissioners, Deputy/Assistant Commissioners, Income Tax Officers, Tax Recovery Officers, Inspectors.
  • Marginal relief: surcharge limited so that extra tax does not exceed the extra income above the threshold.
  • Types of assessment in the book: self-assessment, regular assessment (Sec 143(3)), best judgement assessment (Sec 144), re-assessment of escaped income (Sec 147) and protective assessment. Advance tax is paid in instalments by due dates (Sec 211).
  • Leading Supreme Court cases in the book include Bharat V. Patel (2018) — money from redeeming stock appreciation rights before the 1999 amendment was a capital receipt, not a salary perquisite.

Worked examples

Example: filing late

A salaried person with one house and bank interest (total income ₹7 lakh) should use ITR-1. They file after the due date: they pay a late fee u/s 234F (₹5,000; ₹1,000 if total income does not exceed ₹5 lakh) and interest u/s 234A on unpaid tax, and file a belated return u/s 139(4).

Example: marginal relief on surcharge (old-regime slabs, before cess)

Income ₹50.1 lakh — just ₹10,000 above the ₹50 lakh surcharge threshold. Tax on ₹50 lakh = ₹13,12,500. Tax on ₹50.1 lakh = ₹13,15,500, plus 10% surcharge = ₹14,47,050.

Marginal relief caps the tax at 13,12,500 + 10,000 = ₹13,22,500, so the relief is ₹1,24,550 — the extra tax cannot exceed the extra ₹10,000 of income.
