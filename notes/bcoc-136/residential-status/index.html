Residential Status & Incidence of Tax

High Asked in 7 of 9 papers · avg 10 marks

Residential status of individuals, HUF and companies; ordinarily resident vs not ordinarily resident; income deemed to accrue in India.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 3 Residential Status and Tax Liability

Key points

  • Individual is resident if in India for 182 days or more in the PY, or 60 days in the PY and 365 days in the 4 preceding years (the 60-day rule is replaced by 182 for Indian citizens leaving for employment and for visiting NRIs, with conditions).
  • Resident and ordinarily resident (ROR) vs resident but not ordinarily resident (RNOR): RNOR if non-resident in 9 of 10 preceding years or in India 729 days or less in 7 preceding years.
  • HUF is resident if control and management is wholly or partly in India. Company is resident if Indian or its place of effective management is in India.
  • Incidence: ROR — global income; RNOR — Indian income plus foreign income from a business controlled from India; non-resident — only income received or accrued/deemed to accrue in India.
  • Income deemed to accrue in India (Sec 9): business connection, property or asset in India, salary for services in India, interest, royalty, fees for technical services paid by residents.

Worked examples

Example: is a foreign visitor resident?

A foreign citizen stays in India for 70 days in 2024-25, and 390 days in the four years before. They fail the 182-day test, but satisfy 60 days in the year plus 365 days in the four preceding years → resident.

They were non-resident in 9 of the 10 preceding years → resident but not ordinarily resident (RNOR).

Example: what income is taxed in India?

Salary earned in India ₹5 lakh; rent from a London flat received in London ₹2 lakh; profit of a Dubai business controlled from India ₹3 lakh.

ROR: all three = ₹10 lakh. RNOR: Indian income ₹5 lakh + foreign business controlled from India ₹3 lakh = ₹8 lakh. Non-resident: only Indian income = ₹5 lakh.

How to solve

  1. Count days of stay in the PY and preceding years; apply the basic conditions, then the additional conditions.

Common mistakes

  • Counting the day of departure and arrival wrongly — both days count as days in India.
