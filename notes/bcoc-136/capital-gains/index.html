Capital Gains & Exemptions (54, 54B, 54F)

Critical Asked in 9 of 9 papers · avg 15 marks

Capital asset, short- and long-term gains, computation with indexation, exemptions u/s 54, 54B, 54F, Capital Gains Account Scheme.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 4 · Unit 12 Capital Gains

Key points

  • Capital asset: property of any kind held by the assessee — excludes stock-in-trade, personal effects (except jewellery, paintings), rural agricultural land, certain bonds.
  • Short-term vs long-term: depends on holding period (e.g. listed shares 12 months, immovable property 24 months, other assets 36 months — as per the course material).
  • Transfer includes sale, exchange, relinquishment, compulsory acquisition.
  • LTCG = full value of consideration − transfer expenses − indexed cost of acquisition − indexed cost of improvement. For assets bought before 1-4-2001, cost may be taken as FMV on 1-4-2001.
  • Sec 54: LTCG on a residential house exempt if invested in another residential house (bought 1 year before or 2 years after, or built within 3 years).
  • Sec 54B: gains on agricultural land invested in new agricultural land within 2 years.
  • Sec 54F: LTCG on any other asset exempt proportionately if net consideration is invested in a residential house.
  • Capital Gains Account Scheme, 1988: unutilised amount deposited in a bank before the return due date is treated as invested.

Worked examples

Example: LTCG on a house, with Section 54 (rules as in the course material)

A house was bought in 2012-13 for ₹20 lakh (CII 200) and sold in 2023-24 (CII 348) for ₹60 lakh; brokerage ₹1 lakh.

Net consideration = ₹59 lakh. Indexed cost = 20 × 348/200 = ₹34.8 lakh. LTCG = 59 − 34.8 = ₹24.2 lakh.

The seller buys a new house for ₹15 lakh and deposits ₹5 lakh in the Capital Gains Account Scheme before the return due date. Exemption u/s 54 = ₹20 lakh → taxable LTCG = ₹4.2 lakh.

Note: for transfers from 23 July 2024 the law removed indexation for most assets (land and buildings bought before that date by resident individuals and HUFs keep a choice).

Example: proportionate exemption under Section 54F

Unlisted shares sold: LTCG ₹10 lakh, net consideration ₹40 lakh. The seller invests ₹30 lakh in a residential house.

Exempt = 10 × 30/40 = ₹7.5 lakh; taxable LTCG = ₹2.5 lakh. Had all ₹40 lakh been invested, the whole gain would be exempt.

How to solve

  1. Full value of consideration − transfer expenses = net consideration.
  2. Indexed cost = cost × CII of year of transfer ÷ CII of year of acquisition (or 2001-02).
  3. Capital gain = net consideration − indexed cost; then subtract exemption (amount invested + CGAS deposit, up to the gain).

Formulas

Indexed cost = Cost × CII(transfer) / CII(acquisition)

Common mistakes

  • Indexing a short-term asset.
  • Forgetting the CGAS deposit when computing the exemption.
