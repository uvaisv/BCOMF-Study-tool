Amalgamation & External Reconstruction (AS 14)

High Asked in 8 of 9 papers · avg 16 marks

Amalgamation in the nature of merger and purchase, pooling of interests and purchase methods, purchase consideration and its methods, external reconstruction.

In your IGNOU bookUnit 13 Amalgamation of Companies: Basic ConceptsUnit 14 Amalgamation of Companies: Accounting Treatment

Key points

  • Amalgamation: two or more companies combine — either one absorbs the other, or a new company takes over both. External reconstruction: a new company is formed to take over an existing (usually weak) company.
  • Nature of merger (AS 14): all assets and liabilities taken over; holders of at least 90% of shares become shareholders of the transferee; consideration in equity shares (cash only for fractions); business continued; book values kept.
  • Nature of purchase: any amalgamation that fails one of those conditions.
  • Pooling of interests method (merger): assets, liabilities and reserves recorded at book values; difference between consideration and share capital adjusted in reserves.
  • Purchase method: assets and liabilities at fair values; only statutory reserves preserved (through an amalgamation adjustment account); excess of consideration over net assets is goodwill, shortfall is capital reserve.
  • Purchase consideration: amount paid to the transferor's shareholders in shares, securities or cash. Methods: lump sum, net payment (adding up payments), net assets (assets taken over at agreed values − liabilities taken over), intrinsic value (share exchange ratio).
  • Ind AS 103 applies to companies following Ind AS; AS 14 to others.

Worked examples

Example: purchase consideration and the purchase method

The transferee takes over assets at agreed values of ₹12,00,000 and liabilities of ₹3,00,000. Net assets method: purchase consideration = ₹9,00,000, paid as 80,000 equity shares of ₹10 issued at ₹11 (₹8,80,000) plus ₹20,000 in cash.

Under the purchase method, if the fair value of the net assets taken over is ₹8,50,000, the extra ₹50,000 paid is goodwill; had it been ₹9,50,000, the ₹50,000 surplus would go to capital reserve.

Example: merger or purchase?

If all assets and liabilities are taken over, holders of 90% or more of the shares get equity shares of the transferee, the business continues and book values are kept → amalgamation in the nature of merger (pooling of interests). If the transferee pays mostly in cash, it fails a condition → purchase.
