Buyback & Redemption of Preference Shares

High Asked in 6 of 9 papers · avg 14 marks

Redemption of preference shares — conditions, methods and journal entries, capital redemption reserve and bonus issue; buyback of shares — motives, conditions, SEBI rules and journal entries.

In your IGNOU bookUnit 3 Buy Back of SharesUnit 4 Redemption of Preference Shares

Key points

  • Redemption of preference shares (Sec 55): only fully paid shares; out of profits available for dividend or proceeds of a fresh issue made for the purpose; premium on redemption from profits or securities premium; an amount equal to the nominal value redeemed out of profits goes to the capital redemption reserve (CRR); CRR can be used to issue fully paid bonus shares.
  • Methods: out of fresh issue of shares, out of profits, or a combination; also partly paid shares must be made fully paid first.
  • Buyback motives: return surplus cash, raise earnings per share and share price, defend against takeover, increase promoters' holding, signal undervaluation, adjust capital structure.
  • Buyback conditions (Sec 68): authorised by articles; special resolution (board resolution up to 10%); up to 25% of paid-up capital and free reserves; debt–equity ratio not above 2:1 afterwards; fully paid shares; completed within one year; no further issue for 6 months (except bonus); shares extinguished within 7 days.
  • Sources: free reserves, securities premium, proceeds of an issue of a different kind of securities. When bought out of free reserves, the nominal value is transferred to CRR (Sec 69).
  • SEBI (Buy-back of Securities) Regulations, 2018: methods — tender offer, open market (stock exchange or book building); public announcement, escrow account, no buyback from promoters in open market, 12-month gap between buybacks.

Worked examples

Example: redeeming preference shares

10,000 8% preference shares of ₹100 are redeemed at a 10% premium (payable ₹11,00,000). Profit and loss balance available ₹6,00,000; securities premium ₹1,00,000.

Premium on redemption ₹1,00,000 → charged to securities premium.

Fresh issue needed = nominal value 10,00,000 − divisible profits 6,00,000 = ₹4,00,000 (40,000 equity shares of ₹10 at par).

Transfer to capital redemption reserve = 10,00,000 − 4,00,000 = ₹6,00,000 from the profit and loss balance.

Example: buyback out of free reserves

A company buys back 1,00,000 of its ₹10 shares at ₹15 (₹15,00,000) out of free reserves and securities premium. The nominal value of ₹10,00,000 is transferred to the capital redemption reserve (Section 69); the ₹5,00,000 premium is charged to securities premium; the shares are extinguished.

How to solve

  1. Find the amount payable (nominal value + premium).
  2. Decide the fresh issue needed: nominal value to be redeemed − profits available (if the question asks).
  3. Record the fresh issue (application, allotment).
  4. Transfer premium on redemption to securities premium or profit and loss.
  5. Transfer to CRR = nominal value redeemed − face value of fresh issue.
  6. Record amount due to preference (or equity) shareholders and payment.
  7. If asked, issue bonus shares out of CRR.

Formulas

Transfer to CRR = Nominal value of shares redeemed (or bought back) − Proceeds (face value) of fresh issue
Fresh issue needed = Nominal value to be redeemed − Divisible profits available

How to write the answer

Journal entries in order with narrations; show the CRR working.

Common mistakes

  • Transferring the whole redemption amount to CRR — only the part not covered by a fresh issue.
