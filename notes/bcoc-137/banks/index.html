Bank Accounts, Commercial Banks & NBFCs

Critical Asked in 8 of 9 papers · avg 21 marks

Structure and functions of commercial banks, investment norms, the bank balance sheet, treatment of special items in bank accounts, deposits, teller and slip systems, and non-banking financial companies.

In your IGNOU bookUnit 16 Banking and Non-Banking Companies: Basic ConceptsUnit 17 Accounts of Banking Companies: Accounting TreatmentUnit 18 Commercial Bank

Key points

  • Structure: Reserve Bank of India at the top; scheduled commercial banks — public sector, private sector, foreign, regional rural banks, small finance banks, payments banks; cooperative banks; non-scheduled banks.
  • Primary functions: accepting deposits (current, savings, fixed, recurring) and lending (loans, cash credit, overdraft, discounting bills). Secondary: agency services (collecting cheques, paying bills, acting as trustee) and utility services (lockers, remittances, letters of credit, guarantees, cards, ATMs).
  • Investment norms: cash reserve ratio with the RBI and statutory liquidity ratio in approved securities; investments classified and valued under RBI norms (held to maturity at cost; available for sale and trading at market value).
  • Balance Sheet (Form A, Third Schedule, Banking Regulation Act 1949) — capital, reserves and surplus, deposits, borrowings, other liabilities and provisions; cash and balances with RBI, balances with banks and money at call, investments, advances, fixed assets, other assets. Contingent liabilities and bills for collection are shown below the total. Profit and Loss (Form B) — interest earned, other income; interest expended, operating expenses, provisions and contingencies.
  • Treatment of items: rebate on bills discounted (unexpired discount) — a liability, deducted from discount income; bills for collection — shown only as a footnote; interest on doubtful debts — credited to interest suspense, not income; advances — shown net of provisions and classified; unclaimed dividend and provision for tax — other liabilities; borrowing from other banks — borrowings.
  • Teller system: one teller receives and pays cash at a counter for small transactions — faster service, fewer queues; suits retail banking. Slip system: entries posted directly from pay-in slips and cheques to ledgers, then summarised — quick posting and fewer errors.
  • NBFC: a company that lends, invests, leases or hires out but cannot accept demand deposits or issue cheques; regulated by RBI. Forms: asset finance, loan, investment, infrastructure finance, microfinance, housing finance; deposit-taking and non-deposit-taking.
  • Residuary non-banking company: an NBFC whose main business is taking deposits under any scheme (not investment, asset-finance or loan business); RBI makes it keep depositors' money in highly liquid, secure instruments.

Worked examples

Example: rebate on bills discounted

On 1 March a bank discounts a ₹1,00,000 bill due on 30 April, at 12% a year. On 31 March, 30 days of the discount relate to the next year.

Rebate = 1,00,000 × 12% × 30/365 = ₹986. This is deducted from discount income and shown as a liability (other liabilities) in the balance sheet.

Bills sent for collection (₹5 lakh) are not the bank's assets or liabilities, so they appear only as a note below the balance sheet.

Example: where items go in a bank's balance sheet

Savings and fixed deposits → deposits. Money borrowed from another bank → borrowings. Cash kept with the RBI for CRR → cash and balances with RBI. Government securities held for SLR → investments. Loans → advances, net of provisions. Unclaimed dividend and provision for tax → other liabilities and provisions.

How to write the answer

Headed points; for 'treatment' questions give each item's place in Form A or B in one line.
