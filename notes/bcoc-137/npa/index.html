Non-Performing Assets (NPAs)

High Asked in 8 of 9 papers · avg 13 marks

Meaning of NPAs, classification and provisioning, reasons for growing NPAs, their consequences, income recognition, and recovery mechanisms in India.

In your IGNOU bookUnit 19 Non-Performing Assets

Key points

  • NPA: a loan or advance where interest or principal remains overdue for more than 90 days (term loans); an overdraft or cash credit that is 'out of order' for 90 days; bills overdue for 90 days; for agriculture, overdue for two crop seasons (short duration) or one (long duration).
  • Classification: standard assets; sub-standard (NPA for up to 12 months); doubtful (sub-standard for 12 months); loss assets (identified as uncollectable). Provisions rise with each class — loss assets 100%.
  • Income recognition: interest on NPAs is not recognised on accrual — only when actually received; interest charged earlier but unrealised is reversed or kept in an interest suspense account.
  • Reasons for growing NPAs: poor credit appraisal and monitoring, economic slowdown, wilful default and diversion of funds, industrial sickness, over-optimistic lending in boom times, directed and political lending, delays in legal recovery, natural calamities.
  • Consequences of high NPAs: lower profits (no interest, higher provisions), erosion of capital, less credit for others, higher lending rates, liquidity problems, loss of depositor and investor confidence, need for government recapitalisation.
  • Recovery mechanisms: Lok Adalats (small loans), Debt Recovery Tribunals (1993 Act), SARFAESI Act 2002 (banks take possession of security without court; asset reconstruction companies), Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code 2016, one-time settlements and restructuring.

Worked examples

Example: when does a loan become an NPA?

A term-loan instalment due on 31 March is not paid. Once it has stayed overdue for more than 90 days — by the end of June — the account is an NPA.

For its first 12 months as an NPA it is sub-standard. After that it becomes doubtful. If the bank or an auditor finds it uncollectable, it is a loss asset, provided for in full.

Example: income recognition on an NPA

The bank had debited ₹1,00,000 of interest to the borrower's account, but none has been received. Because the account is an NPA, this interest is not taken to income; it is reversed or kept in an interest suspense account. It is taken to income only when actually received.
