Valuation of Goodwill & Shares

Critical Asked in 9 of 9 papers · avg 21 marks

Meaning, features and need for valuing goodwill; factors; average profit, super profit, capitalisation and annuity methods; valuation of shares — net assets, yield and earning capacity methods; minority and majority holdings.

In your IGNOU bookUnit 11 Valuation of GoodwillUnit 12 Valuation of Shares

Key points

  • Goodwill: the value of a firm's reputation and ability to earn more than a normal return; an intangible asset; valued when a business is sold, on amalgamation, admission or retirement of partners, conversion into a company.
  • Factors affecting goodwill: profitability and its trend, location, quality of management, market share and competition, nature of business, brand and customer loyalty, special contracts or licences.
  • Methods: average profit (average maintainable profit × years' purchase); super profit (super profit × years' purchase); capitalisation (of average profit, or of super profit); annuity (present value of super profits).
  • Adjust past profits for non-recurring items, abnormal losses (e.g. fire), expected expenses (insurance) and a fair remuneration to the proprietor; exclude income from non-trading investments.
  • Valuation of shares is needed for: amalgamation or absorption, buying a controlling interest, unquoted shares, loans against shares, buyback, conversion of shares, tax and ESOP valuation.
  • Net assets (intrinsic) method: value of assets less all liabilities and preference capital, divided by the number of equity shares — suited to a controlling (majority) holding.
  • Yield method: based on expected dividend or earnings compared with the normal rate of return — suited to small (minority) holdings, which cannot control the company's assets.
  • Fair value = average of intrinsic value and yield value.

Worked examples

Example: goodwill by four methods

Profits for three years: ₹1,00,000; ₹1,20,000 (after an abnormal fire loss of ₹20,000, so ₹1,40,000 adjusted); ₹1,50,000. Average maintainable profit = 3,90,000 ÷ 3 = ₹1,30,000. Capital employed ₹10,00,000; normal return 10%.

Average profit, 3 years' purchase: 1,30,000 × 3 = ₹3,90,000.

Super profit: normal profit ₹1,00,000, super profit ₹30,000 × 3 = ₹90,000.

Capitalisation: 1,30,000 × 100/10 − 10,00,000 = ₹3,00,000.

Annuity (5 years at 10%, factor 3.7908): 30,000 × 3.7908 = ₹1,13,724.

Example: value of an equity share

Assets at agreed values ₹25,00,000; outside liabilities ₹5,00,000; preference capital ₹4,00,000; 1,00,000 equity shares of ₹10.

Intrinsic value = (25,00,000 − 5,00,000 − 4,00,000) ÷ 1,00,000 = ₹16.

Yield value: expected rate 15%, normal rate 12% → 15/12 × 10 = ₹12.50.

Fair value = (16 + 12.50) ÷ 2 = ₹14.25.

How to solve

  1. Adjust each year's profit for abnormal or non-recurring items and the proprietor's remuneration.
  2. Find the average (or weighted average) maintainable profit.
  3. For super profit: compute normal profit = capital employed × normal rate; super profit = average profit − normal profit.
  4. Apply the method asked: × years' purchase, capitalise, or × annuity factor.
  5. For shares: net assets ÷ number of equity shares; or yield = expected rate ÷ normal rate × paid-up value per share.

Formulas

Goodwill (average profit) = Average profit × Number of years' purchase
Normal profit = Capital employed × Normal rate of return ÷ 100
Super profit = Average profit − Normal profit; Goodwill = Super profit × Years' purchase
Capitalisation: Goodwill = (Average profit × 100 ÷ Normal rate) − Capital employed
Annuity: Goodwill = Super profit × Present value of annuity of ₹1
Intrinsic value per share = (Assets − Liabilities − Preference capital) ÷ Number of equity shares
Yield value per share = (Expected rate of return ÷ Normal rate of return) × Paid-up value per share

How to write the answer

Working notes → adjusted profits → average → super profit or value → final answer with the method named.

Common mistakes

  • Not adjusting past profits for abnormal items before averaging.
