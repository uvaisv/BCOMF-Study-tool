Final Accounts of Companies (Schedule III)

Critical Asked in 8 of 9 papers · avg 21 marks

Preparing the Statement of Profit and Loss and Balance Sheet of a company under Schedule III of the Companies Act, 2013; form and content; provisions for tax and dividend.

In your IGNOU bookUnit 6 Final Accounts-IUnit 7 Final Accounts-II

Key points

  • Sec 129: financial statements must give a true and fair view, follow notified accounting standards and be in the form of Schedule III.
  • Balance Sheet — Equity and Liabilities: shareholders' funds (share capital; reserves and surplus); share application money pending allotment; non-current liabilities (long-term borrowings, deferred tax liabilities, long-term provisions); current liabilities (short-term borrowings, trade payables, other current liabilities, short-term provisions).
  • Balance Sheet — Assets: non-current assets (property, plant and equipment; intangible assets; capital work-in-progress; non-current investments; deferred tax assets; long-term loans and advances); current assets (current investments, inventories, trade receivables, cash and cash equivalents, short-term loans and advances, other current assets).
  • Statement of Profit and Loss: revenue from operations + other income = total income; expenses — cost of materials consumed, purchases of stock-in-trade, changes in inventories, employee benefits, finance costs, depreciation and amortisation, other expenses; profit before tax; tax expense (current and deferred); profit for the year; earnings per share.
  • Notes to accounts carry the details (share capital, reserves, borrowings, fixed assets schedule).
  • Adjustments usually asked: closing stock, depreciation, outstanding and prepaid expenses, provision for doubtful debts, provision for tax, transfer to general reserve, managerial remuneration, writing off preliminary expenses and goodwill.
  • Dividend: since the revised AS 4 (2016), proposed final dividend is not provided in the accounts — it is disclosed in the notes. Dividend distribution tax was abolished from April 2020. Older exam questions still say 'provide for proposed dividend and CDT' — follow the question's instruction.
  • Terms: provision for tax (estimated tax liability, a current liability); interim dividend (paid by the board during the year, from surplus); final dividend (declared at the AGM); general reserve (profits set aside, not for any specific purpose).

Worked examples

Example: Statement of Profit and Loss to surplus

Revenue from operations ₹10,00,000; other income ₹20,000 → total income ₹10,20,000.

Expenses: materials consumed 4,00,000, employee benefits 2,00,000, finance costs 50,000, depreciation 70,000, other expenses 80,000 → ₹8,00,000.

Profit before tax ₹2,20,000; tax at 30% ₹66,000 → profit for the year ₹1,54,000.

Surplus: opening 40,000 + 1,54,000 − transfer to general reserve 20,000 − interim dividend 50,000 = ₹1,24,000, shown under Reserves and Surplus.

Example: where does each item go in the Schedule III Balance Sheet?

10% debentures repayable in 5 years → non-current liabilities, long-term borrowings. Trade creditors → current liabilities, trade payables. Provision for tax → current liabilities, short-term provisions. Goodwill → non-current assets, intangible assets. Stock → current assets, inventories. Calls in arrears → deducted from subscribed capital in the share capital note.

How to solve

  1. Read every adjustment and mark where it goes (P&L, Balance Sheet, or both).
  2. Prepare working notes: depreciation, closing stock, outstanding/prepaid items, provision for doubtful debts, tax.
  3. Draw up the Statement of Profit and Loss in Schedule III order and find profit after tax.
  4. Take profit to Reserves and Surplus (surplus in P&L), less transfers to reserves and interim dividend.
  5. Prepare the Balance Sheet under the Schedule III headings, with notes for share capital and reserves.
  6. Check that the Balance Sheet totals agree.

Formulas

Profit before tax = Total income − Total expenses
Surplus c/f = Opening surplus + Profit after tax − Transfers to reserves − Dividends paid

How to write the answer

Show working notes for each adjustment, then the Statement of Profit and Loss, then the Balance Sheet with note references.

Common mistakes

  • Showing items in the old horizontal format — use the vertical Schedule III format.
  • Forgetting to carry adjustments to both statements.
