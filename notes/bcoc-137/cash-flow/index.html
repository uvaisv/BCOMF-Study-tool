Cash Flow Statement (AS 3)

Critical Asked in 8 of 9 papers · avg 17 marks

Cash flow statement under AS 3: cash and cash equivalents, operating, investing and financing activities, direct and indirect methods, uses, and comparison with fund flow and income statements.

In your IGNOU bookUnit 8 Cash Flow Statement

Key points

  • Cash and cash equivalents: cash in hand, demand deposits with banks, and short-term highly liquid investments maturing within 3 months.
  • Operating activities: main revenue-producing activities (cash from customers, paid to suppliers and employees, taxes). Investing: buying and selling long-term assets and investments, interest and dividend received. Financing: issue and redemption of shares and debentures, borrowings, interest and dividend paid.
  • Indirect method: start from net profit before tax, add back non-cash and non-operating items (depreciation, amortisation, loss on sale of assets), deduct non-operating gains, adjust for changes in working capital, deduct tax paid.
  • Direct method: cash receipts from customers minus cash paid to suppliers, employees and for expenses, minus tax paid.
  • Uses: shows ability to generate cash, liquidity and solvency, helps plan financing, explains why profit differs from cash, allows comparison between firms.
  • Cash flow vs fund flow: cash vs working capital; a cash flow statement is required by AS 3; it is more useful for short-term planning.
  • Cash flow vs income statement: cash basis vs accrual basis; shows actual cash movement rather than profit.

Worked example

Example: cash flow statement (indirect method)

Operating: net profit before tax 1,00,000 + depreciation 20,000 + loss on sale of machinery 5,000 − profit on sale of investments 3,000 = 1,22,000. Debtors up 15,000 (−), stock down 8,000 (+), creditors up 6,000 (+), outstanding expenses down 2,000 (−) → 1,19,000. Tax paid 25,000 → net cash from operating activities ₹94,000.

Investing: machinery bought −80,000, machinery sold +15,000, investments sold +23,000 → −₹42,000.

Financing: shares issued +50,000, loan repaid −30,000, dividend paid −20,000 → nil.

Net increase in cash = ₹52,000; opening cash 18,000 → closing cash ₹70,000.

How to solve

  1. Find net profit before tax (reconstruct from the change in P&L balance, adding back dividend and transfers).
  2. Add non-cash and non-operating items; subtract non-operating gains.
  3. Adjust for changes in current assets (increase −, decrease +) and current liabilities (increase +, decrease −).
  4. Subtract tax paid → net cash from operating activities.
  5. Use ledger accounts for fixed assets and investments to find purchases and sales → investing activities.
  6. Shares, debentures, loans, interest and dividend paid → financing activities.
  7. Net change + opening cash and cash equivalents = closing balance.

Formulas

Cash from operations = Net profit before tax + Non-cash expenses − Non-operating income ± Working capital changes − Tax paid
Net increase in cash = Operating + Investing + Financing cash flows

How to write the answer

Three sections with headings; working notes for asset accounts; reconcile opening + net change = closing cash.

Common mistakes

  • Treating interest paid as operating for a non-financial company — it goes under financing.
  • Missing the add-back of depreciation in the indirect method.
