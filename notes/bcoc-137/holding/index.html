Holding Companies & Consolidation

Critical Asked in 9 of 9 papers · avg 19 marks

Holding and subsidiary companies, their types, advantages and disadvantages, and preparing a consolidated balance sheet — cost of control, minority interest, pre- and post-acquisition profits.

In your IGNOU bookUnit 9 Accounts of Holding Companies-IUnit 10 Accounts of Holding Companies-II

Key points

  • Holding company (Sec 2(46), 2(87)): controls the composition of another company's board or holds more than half of its total voting power (alone or with its subsidiaries). The other company is its subsidiary; wholly owned when it holds all shares.
  • Advantages: control with less investment, separate liability of each company, tax and management flexibility, easier expansion. Disadvantages: possible manipulation of accounts and transfer prices, concentration of economic power, minority shareholders may be exploited, complex structure.
  • Consolidation (AS 21; Ind AS 110 for Ind AS companies): combine the holding and subsidiary balance sheets line by line, eliminating the investment and inter-company items.
  • Cost of control (goodwill) = cost of investment − holding's share of the subsidiary's share capital and pre-acquisition profits; if negative it is a capital reserve.
  • Minority interest = minority's share of share capital + its share of all reserves and profits (pre- and post-acquisition) + share of revaluation.
  • Pre-acquisition profits are capital profits (go to cost of control); the holding's share of post-acquisition profits goes to consolidated reserves.
  • Other adjustments: revaluation of subsidiary's assets (capital), mutual debts and bills, unrealised profit on inter-company stock, bonus shares, preference shares and debentures held, contingent liabilities (note only).

Worked example

Example: cost of control and minority interest

H Ltd buys 80% of S Ltd's ₹1,00,000 share capital for ₹1,50,000. At that date S has a general reserve of ₹40,000 and P&L of ₹20,000 (pre-acquisition = capital profits ₹60,000). Since then S has earned ₹30,000 (post-acquisition = revenue profit).

Minority interest = 20% × (1,00,000 + 60,000 + 30,000) = ₹38,000.

Cost of control = 1,50,000 − (80,000 + 80% × 60,000) = 1,50,000 − 1,28,000 = ₹22,000 goodwill.

H's consolidated reserves include 80% × 30,000 = ₹24,000 of S's post-acquisition profit.

How to solve

  1. Find the holding's and minority's share of the subsidiary's shares.
  2. Analyse the subsidiary's reserves and profit into pre-acquisition (capital) and post-acquisition (revenue); include revaluation profit or loss as capital.
  3. Minority interest = minority share of capital + all reserves.
  4. Cost of control = investment − holding's share of capital and capital profits.
  5. Consolidated reserves = holding's reserves + its share of the subsidiary's revenue profits − unrealised profit.
  6. Add assets and liabilities line by line, cancelling inter-company debts; prepare the consolidated Balance Sheet.

Formulas

Cost of control = Cost of investment − (Share of paid-up capital + Share of capital profits)
Minority interest = Minority % × (Share capital + All reserves and profits)

How to write the answer

Working notes: analysis of profits → minority interest → cost of control → consolidated reserves → consolidated Balance Sheet.

Common mistakes

  • Treating all of the subsidiary's profits as revenue — split them at the date of acquisition.
