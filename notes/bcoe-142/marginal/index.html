Marginal Costing & Decision Making

Critical Asked in 9 of 9 papers · avg 20 marks

Marginal costing and its characteristics, absorption vs marginal income statements, and decisions — make or buy, sales mix, price cuts, special or export orders, differential costing.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 4 · Unit 13 Marginal CostingBlock 5 · Unit 15 Relevant Costs for Decision Making

Key points

  • Marginal costing: only variable costs are charged to products; fixed costs are treated as period costs and written off against contribution.
  • Characteristics: costs split into fixed and variable; stocks valued at variable cost; contribution used to judge products; profit = contribution − fixed cost.
  • Absorption vs marginal profits: the same when production equals sales (no change in stock); different when stock changes — absorption profit is higher when production exceeds sales, because some fixed overhead is carried forward in closing stock.
  • Make or buy: buy if the purchase price is below the variable (marginal) cost of making, considering spare capacity, quality and supply.
  • Sales mix: choose the mix that gives the highest total contribution (or contribution per unit of the limiting factor).
  • Price reduction or special/export order: accept if the price covers marginal cost and adds contribution, if capacity is spare and the home market is not harmed.
  • Differential costing: comparing the change in costs and revenues between alternatives — used for choosing between options.

Worked examples

Example: make or buy

Making a component costs: materials ₹20, labour ₹15, variable overhead ₹5, share of fixed overhead ₹10 = ₹50. A supplier offers it at ₹45.

The fixed overhead continues either way, so compare the variable cost ₹40 with ₹45 → make it; buying would lose ₹5 a unit. (If the spare capacity could earn more elsewhere, that must also be counted.)

Example: limiting factor, and absorption vs marginal profit

Machine hours are scarce. Product A: contribution ₹60, 3 hours → ₹20 an hour. Product B: contribution ₹40, 1 hour → ₹40 an hour. Make B first.

Production 10,000 units, sales 8,000; fixed overhead ₹1,00,000 (₹10 a unit). Under absorption costing, closing stock of 2,000 units carries ₹20,000 of fixed overhead forward, so absorption profit is ₹20,000 higher than marginal-costing profit this year.

How to solve

  1. Separate variable and fixed costs.
  2. For each option, compute contribution (or total cost of making vs buying).
  3. Compare contributions (or the limiting factor contribution) and choose the higher one.
  4. Absorption statement: include fixed overhead in product cost and value stock at full cost; marginal: stock at variable cost, fixed cost in full.

Formulas

Contribution = Sales − Variable cost
Profit = Contribution − Fixed cost

How to write the answer

Marginal cost statement: Sales − Variable cost = Contribution − Fixed cost = Profit, for each option compared.

Common mistakes

  • Including fixed costs that do not change in make-or-buy comparisons.
