Financial Statements & Their Analysis

Critical Asked in 9 of 9 papers · avg 19 marks

Nature, usefulness and limitations of financial statements, tools of analysis, horizontal and vertical balance sheets, profit levels (GP to PAT, profit for equity), reserves and provisions, fund flow statement.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 3 Understanding Financial StatementsBlock 1 · Unit 4 Techniques of Financial Analysis

Key points

  • Financial statements: balance sheet, statement of profit and loss, cash flow statement and notes. Nature: recorded facts, accounting conventions and personal judgement.
  • Limitations: historical costs, ignore qualitative factors and inflation, interim not final, window dressing possible, one-year view.
  • Usefulness for decisions: judge profitability, liquidity and solvency; compare with past and with others; plan and control.
  • Tools of analysis: comparative statements, common-size statements, trend analysis (index numbers from a base year), ratio analysis, fund flow and cash flow statements.
  • Horizontal balance sheet: liabilities and assets side by side (T-form). Vertical: one below the other, as in Schedule III.
  • Profit levels: gross profit = net sales − cost of goods sold; operating profit = gross profit − operating expenses; PBIT = operating profit + non-operating income − non-operating expenses; PBT = PBIT − interest; PAT = PBT − tax; profit for equity = PAT − preference dividend.
  • Reserves are appropriations of profit (revenue, capital, general); provisions are charges against profit for known liabilities or losses. A secret reserve is hidden by undervaluing assets or overvaluing liabilities. Capital reserve comes from capital profits (e.g. premium, profit on reissue of forfeited shares).
  • Fund flow statement: shows sources and uses of working capital — useful for planning finance; limited because it ignores cash and is historical.

Worked examples

Example: levels of profit

Sales 12,00,000 − returns 20,000 = net sales ₹11,80,000. Cost of goods sold 7,00,000 → gross profit ₹4,80,000 (40.7% of net sales).

Less administration 1,00,000 and selling 80,000 → operating profit ₹3,00,000.

Add dividend received 20,000, less loss on sale of an asset 10,000 → PBIT ₹3,10,000.

Less interest 60,000 → PBT ₹2,50,000; tax at 25% → PAT ₹1,87,500; less preference dividend 20,000 → profit for equity shareholders ₹1,67,500.

Example: trend analysis

Sales: 2022 ₹10 lakh, 2023 ₹12 lakh, 2024 ₹15 lakh. With 2022 as base (100), the trend percentages are 100, 120 and 150 — sales grew 50% in two years.

How to solve

  1. Net sales = gross sales − returns inwards.
  2. Gross profit = net sales − cost of goods sold (direct costs).
  3. Operating profit = GP − administrative, selling and distribution expenses.
  4. PBIT = operating profit + non-operating income − non-operating expenses (excluding interest).
  5. PBT = PBIT − interest; PAT = PBT − tax; profit for equity = PAT − preference dividend.

How to write the answer

Meaning → headed points → conclusion; for profit levels, show a vertical statement.
