Management Accounting: Meaning, Scope & Functions

Critical Asked in 7 of 9 papers · avg 13 marks

Meaning, nature, scope, objectives, functions, techniques and role of management accounting, and how it differs from cost and financial accounting.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 1 Management Accounting: An Introduction

Key points

  • Management accounting: presenting accounting information to help management plan, control and make decisions.
  • Nature: forward-looking, decision-oriented, selective, no fixed formats, uses cost and financial data and statistical tools.
  • Scope: financial accounting, cost accounting, budgeting and forecasting, cost control, reporting to management, tax planning, internal audit, statistical analysis.
  • Objectives and functions: planning, controlling, organising, coordinating, motivating, communicating, interpreting data, helping decisions.
  • Techniques: financial planning, analysis of financial statements, standard costing, marginal costing, budgetary control, decision accounting, responsibility accounting, MIS.
  • Role: supplies information, evaluates alternatives, helps fix prices and policies, measures performance.
  • Vs cost accounting: wider scope (all information) vs costs only; forward-looking vs mainly historical and current. Vs financial accounting: internal vs external users, no statutory format vs prescribed format, parts of the business vs whole.

Worked example

Example: management accounting at work

A factory manager asks whether to run a second shift. The management accountant estimates the extra wages, power and supervision (differential costs), the contribution from extra sales, the cash needed each month (cash budget), and the break-even volume for the shift, then recommends starting it for the peak season only.

None of this appears in the published financial statements — it is forward-looking information prepared for one decision.
