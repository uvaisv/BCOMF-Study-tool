Pricing Decisions

Moderate Asked in 6 of 9 papers · avg 9 marks

Internal and external factors influencing pricing decisions, methods of pricing, differentiated pricing and break-even pricing.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 5 · Unit 16 Pricing Decisions

Key points

  • Internal factors: costs, company objectives, stage of product life cycle, marketing mix, product differentiation, organisation.
  • External factors: demand and elasticity, competition, customers and buyer behaviour, government regulation, economic conditions, suppliers, channel members.
  • Methods: cost-plus (full cost + markup), marginal-cost pricing, target-return pricing, going-rate pricing, penetration pricing, skimming, perceived-value pricing, sealed-bid pricing.
  • Differentiated pricing (price discrimination): different prices for customer groups, times, places or product forms.
  • Break-even pricing: setting the price at which revenue covers total cost at the expected volume.

Worked examples

Example: cost-plus vs marginal-cost pricing

Cost-plus: full cost of a table ₹800 + 25% markup → price ₹1,000.

Marginal-cost pricing: a hotel's variable cost per room-night is ₹1,200 and its normal rate ₹4,000. In the off-season it offers rooms to a tour group at ₹2,000. Each room still adds ₹800 of contribution towards fixed costs, which would otherwise earn nothing.

Example: differentiated pricing

A cinema charges less for weekday morning shows and gives student discounts; a railway charges different fares for sleeper and AC classes on the same train — different prices for times, groups and product forms.
