Cost Control, Cost Reduction & Cost Management

High Asked in 9 of 9 papers · avg 13 marks

Cost control and its techniques and advantages, cost reduction and its advantages and limitations, their differences, and techniques of cost management.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 2 Cost Control, Cost Reduction and Cost Management

Key points

  • Cost control: keeping costs within set targets or standards — through budgetary control, standard costing, variance analysis, inventory control, quality control.
  • Advantages of cost control: higher profit, efficient use of resources, cost consciousness, better pricing, competitiveness.
  • Cost reduction: a real and permanent fall in unit cost without lowering quality — through value analysis, work study, standardisation and simplification, better design, automation, TQM.
  • Differences: cost control keeps to targets, cost reduction lowers the targets themselves; control is preventive and short-term, reduction is corrective and continuous; control accepts standards, reduction challenges them.
  • Limitations of cost reduction: can cut quality if careless, needs investment and staff cooperation, gains may be hard to measure.
  • Cost management: using techniques across the business to manage costs — activity-based costing, target costing, kaizen, life-cycle costing, value-chain analysis, JIT, benchmarking.

Worked example

Example: cost control vs cost reduction

Cost control: the canteen's budget is ₹50,000 a month; the manager checks each month's spending against it and acts on overspending.

Cost reduction: value analysis shows that a metal bracket can be replaced by a plastic one doing the same job ₹5 cheaper. At 1,00,000 units a year this cuts cost by ₹5,00,000 permanently, and the standard itself is lowered.
