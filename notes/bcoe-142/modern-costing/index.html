ABC, Target, Kaizen & Other Modern Costing

Moderate Asked in 7 of 9 papers · avg 9 marks

Activity-based costing, drawbacks of traditional costing, target costing, kaizen costing, throughput costing and value-chain analysis.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 5 · Unit 18 Contemporary Issues in Management Accounting-I (18.8–18.9)Block 5 · Unit 19 Contemporary Issues in Management Accounting-II

Key points

  • Activity-based costing (ABC): overheads are traced to activities and then to products using cost drivers. Steps: identify activities → build activity cost pools → choose cost drivers → compute driver rates → assign costs to products.
  • Advantages of ABC: more accurate product costs, better pricing and product-mix decisions, shows non-value-adding activities, suits many products and high overheads.
  • Drawbacks of traditional costing: one volume-based rate (labour or machine hours) distorts costs when products differ in complexity and batch size; ignores non-volume activities.
  • Target costing: target cost = target selling price − target profit. Steps: find market price → set required profit → compute target cost → design and value-engineer to reach it → keep costs there.
  • Kaizen costing: continuous small cost reductions during the production stage; features — employee involvement, small steps, all levels; approaches — asset-based and product-based.
  • Throughput costing: only direct materials treated as variable; throughput = sales − direct materials; focuses on bottlenecks.
  • Value-chain analysis (Porter): primary activities (inbound logistics, operations, outbound logistics, marketing and sales, service) and support activities — used to find cost savings and competitive advantage.
  • Also in the book: life-cycle costing (all costs of a product from design to withdrawal), backflush costing (costs assigned only at the end of production, suited to JIT) and strategic cost management.

Worked examples

Example: ABC corrects a distorted cost

Set-up costs of ₹1,20,000 are shared by products A and B. A needs 10 set-ups and 8,000 labour hours; B needs 50 set-ups and 2,000 labour hours.

Traditional (labour hours): A ₹96,000, B ₹24,000.

ABC (driver = set-ups, ₹2,000 each): A ₹20,000, B ₹1,00,000.

B, made in many small batches, was being undercosted; ABC shows its true cost.

Example: target costing

Customers will pay ₹500 for a mixer-grinder; the company wants 20% profit on price (₹100), so the target cost is ₹400. The present design costs ₹450, so engineers must remove ₹50 — for example a simpler jar lid and a standard motor — before launch.
