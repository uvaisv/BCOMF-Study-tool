Computerised Accounting & Tally

Critical Asked in 11 of 11 papers · avg 22 marks

Usually the last question (Q9), always theory, often 'write notes on any two'. The most predictable 20 marks in the paper.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 6 · Unit 18 Introduction to Computerised AccountingBlock 6 · Unit 19 Creating MastersBlock 6 · Unit 20 Voucher Entries and InvoicingBlock 6 · Unit 21 Preparation of Reports

Key points

  • Computerised accounting is used for speed, ease, security and reliability; the risks are data corruption, power problems, theft of data and over-reliance on computers.
  • The key difference from manual accounting: ledgers are created first, and transactions are then entered through vouchers, instead of journal entries followed by ledger posting.
  • Choose software on factors such as cost, ease of use, flexibility, data security, vendor support and statutory compliance.
  • Step 1 in Tally ERP.9 is company creation (name, address, contact details, with or without inventory, financial year). Features apply only to that company; configurations apply to the whole software.
  • Step 2 is creating masters. Tally has 2 predefined ledgers (Cash, and Profit & Loss) and 28 predefined groups — 15 primary (9 for balance sheet items, 6 for profit and loss) and 13 sub-groups. Inventory masters: stock groups, categories, units, godowns and stock items.
  • Step 3 is recording vouchers: 8 main voucher types (contra, payment, receipt, journal, sales, purchase, credit note, debit note) plus reversing journal, memorandum and post-dated vouchers. Entries can be checked and altered in the Day Book.
  • Invoices can be raised as an Account Invoice (services) or an Item Invoice (with item details).
  • Step 4 is reports, produced automatically: Balance Sheet, Profit & Loss, Stock Summary and Trial Balance on the main screen; Day Book, receivables, payables and cash/bank books under Display.

Worked examples

Example: Sharma Traders starts using Tally

Company creation: name, address and the financial year from 1 April, with inventory switched on.

Masters: ledgers such as Sharma Capital (under Capital Account), SBI (under Bank Accounts), Rent (under Indirect Expenses) and Ravi & Co (under Sundry Creditors); stock items with their units.

Vouchers: rent of ₹10,000 paid by cheque → Payment voucher (Rent Dr, SBI Cr). Goods bought on credit from Ravi & Co → Purchase voucher.

Reports: the Trial Balance, Profit & Loss and Balance Sheet appear on their own — nothing is posted or totalled by hand.

Example: which voucher?

Cash deposited into the bank → Contra.

Salary paid in cash → Payment.

Commission received by cheque → Receipt.

Depreciation charged for the year → Journal.

Goods returned by a customer → Credit Note; goods returned to a supplier → Debit Note.

How to write the answer

Definition (2–3 lines) → 5–6 headed points with one line each → a small comparison table where relevant → one-line conclusion.

Common mistakes

  • Writing only definitions — each 10-mark note needs 5–6 distinct points.
  • Mixing up stock group and stock category.
