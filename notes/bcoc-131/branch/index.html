Branch Accounts

Critical Asked in 11 of 11 papers · avg 19 marks

Either a numerical (Branch Account / Stock & Debtors system) or theory on systems of dependent branches.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 4 · Unit 13 Branch Accounts-IBlock 4 · Unit 14 Branch Accounts-II

Key points

  • Branch accounts show each branch's profit or loss and help control its stock and cash.
  • Dependent branches keep no full books — the head office keeps their accounts. Independent branches keep a full double-entry system and prepare their own trial balance.
  • Systems for dependent branches: Debtors system (a Branch Account works like a Profit and Loss account), Stock and Debtors system, and Final Accounts system.
  • When goods are invoiced to the branch above cost, the loading on goods sent, returns, and opening and closing stock is removed so only real profit is shown.
  • Independent branches: the head office keeps a Branch Account and the branch keeps a Head Office Account; each mirrors the other.
  • Items needing special treatment: goods in transit, cash in transit, head-office expenses charged to the branch, depreciation on branch assets whose accounts are kept at head office, and inter-branch transactions.
  • Incorporating the branch trial balance: detailed incorporation (every item) or abridged incorporation (branch profit or loss plus assets and liabilities). The Branch Account then closes and reopens next year.

Worked examples

Example: debtors system, goods invoiced at cost + 25%

Loading = 25 ÷ 125 = 1/5 of the invoice price.

Data (at invoice price): opening stock ₹20,000, goods sent ₹1,00,000, closing stock ₹25,000. Opening debtors ₹10,000, closing debtors ₹15,000. Cash sent for expenses ₹8,000. Branch remitted cash sales ₹40,000 + collections ₹50,000.

Branch A/c debit: opening stock 20,000 + opening debtors 10,000 + goods sent 1,00,000 + cash for expenses 8,000 + stock reserve on closing stock 5,000 = ₹1,43,000.

Branch A/c credit: cash remitted 90,000 + closing stock 25,000 + closing debtors 15,000 + loading on goods sent 20,000 + loading on opening stock 4,000 = ₹1,54,000.

Branch profit = 1,54,000 − 1,43,000 = ₹11,000.

Check the same answer at cost

Cost of goods sold = opening stock 16,000 + goods sent 80,000 − closing stock 20,000 = ₹76,000.

Sales = cash 40,000 + credit sales (15,000 + 50,000 − 10,000 = 55,000) = ₹95,000.

Profit = 95,000 − 76,000 − expenses 8,000 = ₹11,000 — the same, which shows why all the loading had to be removed.

How to solve

  1. Debtors system: open Branch A/c. Debit: opening stock, opening debtors, petty cash, prepaid expenses, goods sent, cash sent for expenses. Credit: cash remitted by branch (cash sales + collections), goods returned to HO, closing stock, closing debtors, closing petty cash.
  2. Liabilities work the other way: opening outstanding expenses on the credit side, closing outstanding expenses on the debit side.
  3. Credit sales, discounts, bad debts and returns by customers do NOT enter the Branch Account (they are inside the debtors figure).
  4. If goods are sent at invoice price: bring stock and goods sent at invoice price, then remove loading with contra entries (Stock Reserve / Goods sent to Branch).
  5. Balancing figure of Branch A/c = profit (credit shortfall) or loss.

Formulas

Loading = Invoice price × Profit% ÷ (100 + Profit%)
e.g. cost + 25% → loading = 25/125 = 1/5 of invoice price

Common mistakes

  • Putting credit sales in the Branch Account under the debtors system.
  • Forgetting to remove loading on opening and closing stock.
