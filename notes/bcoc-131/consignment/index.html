Consignment

Critical Asked in 10 of 11 papers · avg 19 marks

Numericals on Consignment Account and valuation of unsold stock, theory on invoice price, loading and losses.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 5 · Unit 15 Consignment Accounts-IBlock 5 · Unit 16 Consignment Accounts-II

Key points

  • In a consignment, the consignor (owner) sends goods to a consignee (agent) who sells them for a commission. Their relationship is that of principal and agent.
  • A proforma invoice goes with the goods; after selling, the consignee sends an account sales showing units sold, prices, expenses, commission and the balance due.
  • Commission may be ordinary or special; del credere commission is paid when the consignee bears the risk of bad debts.
  • The consignor's accounts: Consignment Account, Goods Sent on Consignment Account and the Consignee's Account. The consignee's: Commission Account and the Consignor's Account.
  • Unsold stock is valued at cost plus a proportion of non-recurring expenses; selling expenses are not added.
  • Normal loss is not recorded separately — it raises the cost per unit of the remaining goods. Abnormal loss is valued like unsold stock, credited to the Consignment Account, and charged to Profit and Loss after deducting any insurance claim.
  • Invoice price (IP) is set above cost to hide the profit; loading = IP − cost price. Loading affects four items — goods sent, goods returned, opening stock and closing stock — and is removed by adjusting entries in the consignor's books only.

Worked examples

Example: consignment with unsold stock

A of Delhi sends 100 units costing ₹500 each (₹50,000) to B of Mumbai and pays freight ₹2,000. B pays unloading ₹1,000 (non-recurring) and godown rent ₹1,500 (selling), sells 80 units at ₹800 and gets 5% commission.

Closing stock (20 units) = (50,000 + 2,000 + 1,000) × 20 ÷ 100 = ₹10,600 — godown rent is not added.

Consignment A/c debit: goods 50,000 + A's freight 2,000 + B's expenses 2,500 + commission 3,200 = ₹57,700. Credit: sales 64,000 + stock 10,600 = ₹74,600. Profit ₹16,900.

B owes A: sales 64,000 − expenses 2,500 − commission 3,200 = ₹58,300.

Example: normal loss raises the cost per unit

1,000 kg sent at ₹20 per kg with freight ₹2,000 (total ₹22,000). Normal loss in transit 50 kg, so 950 kg arrive; 760 kg are sold.

Closing stock = 22,000 ÷ 950 × 190 kg = ₹4,400. The 50 kg loss is not shown separately — its cost is spread over the good units.

How to solve

  1. Consignment A/c (Dr): goods sent, consignor's expenses, consignee's expenses, commission. (Cr): sales, abnormal loss, closing stock. Balance = profit/loss.
  2. Unsold stock value = (Cost of goods + consignor's expenses + consignee's non-recurring expenses) × unsold units ÷ units received.
  3. Consignee's selling expenses (godown rent, selling) are NOT added to stock value.
  4. Commission: ordinary on total sales; over-riding on surplus above invoice; del credere on total credit sales.

Formulas

Closing stock = (Total cost incl. direct expenses) ÷ Units received × Units unsold
Normal loss: units received = sent − normal loss

Common mistakes

  • Adding selling expenses to closing stock.
  • Treating normal loss as abnormal loss.
