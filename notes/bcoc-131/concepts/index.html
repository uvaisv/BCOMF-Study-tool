Accounting Concepts, Conventions & Income

Moderate Asked in 8 of 11 papers · avg 10 marks

Short-note style theory, 10–20 marks.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 3 Accounting PrinciplesBlock 1 · Unit 4 Accounting Standards (4.10–4.13 Measurement of business income)

Key points

  • Concepts (generally accepted accounting principles) are ground rules for recording and reporting; some apply at the recording stage, others at the reporting stage.
  • Recording stage: business entity (business and owner are separate), money measurement (record in the country's currency), historical record (record only what has happened, date-wise), cost (record the amount paid, not the value) and dual aspect (every transaction has two sides).
  • Reporting stage: going concern (the firm continues, it is not closing down), accounting period, matching (match costs with the revenues of the same period), accrual and realisation.
  • Conservatism (prudence): provide for all losses but take only incomes actually earned.
  • Consistency: follow the same methods from period to period so figures can be compared.
  • Full disclosure: reports must disclose all material information users need.
  • Materiality: facts that are not material may be ignored when measuring income.
  • Measuring business income relies on the accounting period, realisation (revenue recognition), matching and accrual.

Worked example

Examples: which concept applies?

The owner takes ₹5,000 from the business till for household use → recorded as drawings, because the business is separate from its owner (business entity).

Land bought for ₹10 lakh is now worth ₹15 lakh → it stays at ₹10 lakh in the books (cost).

Closing stock cost ₹50,000 but can now be sold for only ₹45,000 → shown at ₹45,000; an expected profit is not recorded (conservatism).

March salary paid on 5 April → an expense of the year ending 31 March (accrual and matching).

A ₹150 stapler is written off as an expense rather than depreciated (materiality).

The firm keeps using the same stock valuation method every year (consistency).

How to write the answer

One-line meaning of each concept + one example each. For 20 marks, cover 8–10 concepts with examples.

Common mistakes

  • Listing names without examples.
