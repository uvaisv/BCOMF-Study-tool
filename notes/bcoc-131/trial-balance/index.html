Trial Balance, Errors & Rectification

High Asked in 7 of 11 papers · avg 13 marks

Usually a 20-mark rectification with Suspense Account.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 7 Trial Balance

Key points

  • A trial balance lists ledger balances to check the arithmetical accuracy of the books and to provide a summary for final accounts.
  • It may disagree because of errors such as wrong totals, wrong posting, posting to the wrong side, omitting one side, or wrong balancing.
  • Errors not disclosed by a trial balance: errors of principle (e.g. a machine bought on credit entered in the Purchases Journal — capital treated as revenue), complete omission, errors of commission posted to the wrong account of the same class, and compensating errors.
  • Advantages: checks arithmetical accuracy, helps locate errors, summarises balances for final accounts. Limitation: agreement does not prove the books are error-free.
  • Rectification: find what was done and what should have been done, then pass the correcting entry.
  • One-sided errors found after the trial balance is drawn go through a Suspense Account, which must close to nil once all errors are corrected.
  • Rectifying entries that involve nominal accounts change the profit already calculated.

Worked example

Example: rectification with a suspense account

The trial balance debit total is ₹750 more than the credit total, so Suspense A/c is opened with a credit of ₹750. Errors found later:

(a) Sales Book undercast by ₹1,000 → Suspense A/c Dr 1,000 To Sales A/c 1,000.

(b) Furniture ₹5,000 bought was debited to Purchases (error of principle — both sides exist, so no suspense) → Furniture A/c Dr 5,000 To Purchases A/c 5,000.

(c) Discount allowed ₹250 not posted to Discount A/c → Discount Allowed A/c Dr 250 To Suspense A/c 250.

Suspense A/c: debit 1,000; credit 750 + 250 = 1,000 → closes to nil.

Effect on profit: +1,000 (sales) + 5,000 (purchases lower) − 250 (discount) = ₹5,750 more profit.

How to solve

  1. For each error, write what was done, what should have been done, then the correcting entry.
  2. If the error affects only one account (e.g. wrong posting, casting error), use Suspense A/c for the other side.
  3. If both sides are wrong (e.g. entered in wrong book), no suspense — correct both accounts.
  4. Post all suspense entries to Suspense A/c; it must close to zero.

Formulas

Undercasting of Sales Book → Suspense A/c Dr To Sales A/c
Overcasting of Purchase Book → Suspense A/c Dr To Purchases A/c

Common mistakes

  • Using suspense for errors that don't affect the trial balance.
  • Reversing the sign of casting errors.
