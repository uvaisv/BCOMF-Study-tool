Subsidiary Books (Cash Book, Purchase Book)

Moderate Asked in 5 of 11 papers · avg 8 marks

Cash book / purchases book numericals and 'why is the journal sub-divided'.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 6 Subsidiary Books

Key points

  • The journal is sub-divided when repetitive transactions are numerous: it saves time, allows division of work and reduces errors.
  • Special journals: Cash Book, Purchases Journal, Sales Journal, Purchases Returns Journal, Sales Returns Journal, and the Journal Proper for everything else.
  • Source documents: inward invoice → Purchases Journal; debit note → Purchases Returns Journal; outward invoice → Sales Journal; credit note → Sales Returns Journal.
  • Only credit purchases and sales of goods go into the Purchases and Sales Journals; cash transactions go into the Cash Book.
  • Cash books: single column; two column (cash + discount — discount allowed on the debit side, discount received on the credit side); three column (cash, bank, discount). The bank column replaces a Bank Account in the ledger.
  • Petty Cash Book records small expenses and saves labour.
  • Banking: a business usually opens a current account and gets a pass book, pay-in-slip book and cheque book. Cheques may be crossed generally or specially, endorsed to a third party, and dishonoured if the bank refuses payment.

Worked examples

Examples: which book?

Goods bought on credit from Ravi → Purchases Book. Goods sold for cash → Cash Book. Goods returned to Ravi (debit note) → Purchases Returns Book. Goods returned by a customer (credit note) → Sales Returns Book. Furniture bought on credit → Journal Proper (it is not goods for resale).

Example: discounts in the cash book

100 units bought at ₹50 each less 10% trade discount → the Purchases Book records ₹4,500; the trade discount is never entered.

Ram pays ₹4,900 in full settlement of ₹5,000 → debit side of the two-column cash book: cash ₹4,900 and discount allowed ₹100.

How to solve

  1. Single column cash book: Dr receipts, Cr payments; balance c/d.
  2. Cash discount appears in the discount column (two-column cash book); trade discount is never recorded.
  3. Purchases Book: date, supplier, invoice no., details (qty × rate − trade discount + taxes/charges), amount.

Common mistakes

  • Recording cash purchases or asset purchases in the Purchases Book.
  • Recording trade discount in the accounts.
