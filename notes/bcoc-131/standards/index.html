Accounting Standards (AS, Ind AS, IFRS)

Moderate Asked in 7 of 11 papers · avg 6 marks

Mostly 10-mark theory: objectives, procedure in India, Ind AS vs IFRS.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 4 Accounting Standards

Key points

  • Accounting standards are written statements of accounting rules and guidelines for preparing uniform and consistent financial statements.
  • Objectives: provide information, harmonise different accounting practices, and bring uniformity, consistency and comparability.
  • Benefits: true and fair view, easy comparison, more valuable information, efficient management, help to accountants and auditors, more credibility and reliability.
  • In India the Accounting Standards Board (ASB) of ICAI frames standards: ASB (with a study group) → exposure draft circulated for comments → ASB revises and submits to the ICAI Council → ICAI issues the standard.
  • Section 133 of the Companies Act, 2013 requires companies to follow the prevailing standards; as on 1 April 2016 there were 32 accounting standards.
  • IFRS: a single global set of standards developed by the International Accounting Standards Board (IASB); used across borders to make statements comparable.
  • India converged its standards with IFRS; the converged standards are called Ind AS. Ind AS 101 covers first-time adoption (moving from Indian GAAP to Ind AS).

Worked examples

Example: why a standard is needed

Two shops buy the same goods at rising prices. One values closing stock by FIFO, the other by a weighted average, so their profits differ even though their business is the same. A standard on inventories (AS 2) sets common rules, so the figures can be compared.

Example: moving to Ind AS

Indian companies above set size limits (the first phase covered companies with net worth of ₹500 crore or more, from 1 April 2016) had to prepare accounts under Ind AS, which are converged with IFRS. The first year's switch from old Indian GAAP is handled under Ind AS 101.
