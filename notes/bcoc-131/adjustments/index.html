Adjustments in Final Accounts

Moderate Asked in 4 of 11 papers · avg 6 marks

20-mark theory question on the adjustments and their two-fold effect.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 3 · Unit 10 Final Accounts-II

Key points

  • Adjustments are needed because some expenses and incomes relate to more than one year or are still outstanding; without them the accounts do not show a true picture.
  • An adjustment given outside the trial balance appears at two places in the final accounts, completing the double entry; one already inside an adjusted trial balance appears only once.
  • Closing stock: credit side of the Trading Account and an asset in the Balance Sheet.
  • Outstanding expenses: added to the expense and shown as a liability. Prepaid expenses: deducted from the expense and shown as an asset.
  • Accrued income: added to the income and shown as an asset. Income received in advance: deducted from the income and shown as a liability.
  • Depreciation, bad debts and provision for doubtful debts: debited to Profit and Loss and deducted from the asset or debtors.
  • Interest on capital is an expense of the business; interest on drawings is an income.

Worked example

Examples: each adjustment appears twice

Rent paid ₹11,000 for 11 months; one month (₹1,000) is outstanding → P&L rent ₹12,000; ₹1,000 shown as a liability.

Insurance ₹12,000 paid on 1 October for one year; books close on 31 March → ₹6,000 is prepaid: P&L ₹6,000; prepaid ₹6,000 shown as an asset.

Debtors ₹50,000; write off further bad debts ₹2,000 and make a 5% provision (no old provision) → provision = 5% of 48,000 = ₹2,400; P&L is charged 2,000 + 2,400; debtors shown at 50,000 − 2,000 − 2,400 = ₹45,600.

Interest on capital ₹3,000 → debited to P&L and added to capital.

How to write the answer

Meaning of adjustment → each adjustment with its two effects (P&L side and Balance Sheet) — a table works well.

Common mistakes

  • Showing only one effect of an adjustment (every adjustment has two).
