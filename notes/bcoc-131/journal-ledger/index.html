Journal & Ledger

Low Asked in 2 of 11 papers · avg 3 marks

Rare as a full question, but journal entries are needed for every numerical topic.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 5 Journal and Ledger

Key points

  • The journal is the book of original entry: transactions are recorded date-wise with a narration.
  • Steps in journalising: identify the accounts, classify them (personal, real, nominal), apply the rules of debit and credit, write the entry with narration.
  • A compound entry has more than one debit or credit; the opening entry brings forward assets and liabilities (Assets A/cs Dr, To Liabilities and Capital A/c).
  • The ledger groups entries by account; posting transfers each journal entry to the accounts concerned.
  • Balancing: a personal account's debit balance means the party owes the business (debtor); a credit balance means the business owes the party (creditor).

Worked example

Example: journalising a few days' transactions

1 April: started business with cash ₹50,000 → Cash A/c Dr To Capital A/c.

3 April: bought goods on credit from Mohan ₹20,000 → Purchases A/c Dr To Mohan A/c.

5 April: sold goods on credit to Sita ₹15,000 → Sita A/c Dr To Sales A/c.

10 April: paid Mohan ₹19,500 in full settlement → Mohan A/c Dr 20,000 To Cash A/c 19,500 and To Discount Received A/c 500 (a compound entry).

Ledger: Mohan's account now balances to nil; Sita's shows a debit balance of ₹15,000, so she is a debtor.

How to solve

  1. Identify accounts → classify (personal/real/nominal) → apply golden rules → write entry with narration.

Common mistakes

  • Missing narrations.
  • Recording trade discount.
