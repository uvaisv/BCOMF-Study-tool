Final Accounts

Moderate Asked in 6 of 11 papers · avg 9 marks

Trading & P&L / Balance Sheet numericals and format questions.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 3 · Unit 9 Final Accounts-I

Key points

  • Final accounts are the Profit and Loss Account (with a Trading section) and the Balance Sheet, prepared from the trial balance at the year end.
  • Trading Account shows gross profit: sales minus cost of goods sold, including direct expenses.
  • Profit and Loss Account shows net profit: gross profit and other incomes minus indirect expenses and losses. Closing entries transfer expenses and incomes to these accounts.
  • Balance Sheet: assets are the debit balances of real and personal accounts (plus deferred revenue expenses not yet written off); liabilities are the credit balances, including capital. The two totals are always equal.
  • Assets may be arranged in order of liquidity or permanence; companies use a vertical presentation.
  • A Manufacturing Account (optional) finds the cost of goods produced, which is carried to the Trading Account.

Worked example

Example: from trial balance to net profit

Opening stock ₹20,000, purchases ₹1,00,000, purchase returns ₹5,000, wages ₹10,000, carriage inwards ₹3,000, closing stock ₹25,000. Sales ₹1,60,000, sales returns ₹4,000.

Cost of goods sold = 20,000 + 1,00,000 − 5,000 + 10,000 + 3,000 − 25,000 = ₹1,03,000.

Gross profit = net sales 1,56,000 − 1,03,000 = ₹53,000 (Trading section).

Indirect items: salaries ₹15,000, rent ₹6,000, carriage outwards ₹2,000; commission received ₹1,000.

Net profit = 53,000 + 1,000 − 23,000 = ₹31,000, added to capital in the Balance Sheet.

How to solve

  1. Classify each item: direct (Trading) vs indirect (P&L) vs balance sheet.
  2. Carriage inwards/wages → Trading; carriage on sales, salaries, rent → P&L.
  3. Apply adjustments before totalling.

Formulas

COGS = Opening stock + Purchases − Purchase returns + Direct expenses − Closing stock

Common mistakes

  • Putting carriage on sales in Trading A/c.
  • Forgetting closing stock on both P&L side (Trading credit) and Balance Sheet.
