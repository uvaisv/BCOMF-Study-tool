Hire Purchase & Instalment

High Asked in 10 of 11 papers · avg 15 marks

Theory on features and hirer's accounting, numericals with default and repossession.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 4 · Unit 11 Hire Purchase Accounts-IBlock 4 · Unit 12 Hire Purchase Accounts-II

Key points

  • Under hire purchase the buyer gets possession at once but ownership passes only with the last instalment; on default the seller can take the goods back.
  • Hire purchase price = cash price + interest; knowing any two gives the third.
  • The buyer (hirer) records the asset at its full cash price or at the cash price actually paid; the main accounts are the Asset Account and the Hire Vendor Account.
  • The vendor keeps the Hire Purchaser's Account and an Interest Account.
  • On default the vendor may repossess all of the goods, or only part by agreement — the repossessed part is revalued and adjusted against the amount due.
  • Instalment payment system: ownership passes when the contract is signed. The buyer is debited with the total interest at the start through an Interest Suspense Account, released as instalments fall due.
  • Goods of small value: the vendor keeps a Hire Purchase Sales Register and works out profit by the Debtors method (Hire Purchase Trading Account) or the Stock and Debtors method.
  • Missing figures in the Hire Purchase Trading Account are found through memorandum accounts: Stock at Shop, Stock with Customers and Instalments Due.

Worked examples

Example: machine on hire purchase, interest 10% on the balance

Cash price ₹50,000; ₹14,000 down, rest in three yearly instalments with interest.

Year 1: interest 10% × 36,000 = 3,600; instalment 15,600 (12,000 principal). Balance 24,000.

Year 2: interest 2,400; instalment 14,400. Balance 12,000.

Year 3: interest 1,200; instalment 13,200. Balance nil.

Hire purchase price = 14,000 + 15,600 + 14,400 + 13,200 = ₹57,200; total interest = 57,200 − 50,000 = ₹7,200.

Hirer's entries: Machinery A/c Dr 50,000 To Vendor A/c. Each year: Interest A/c Dr To Vendor A/c, then Vendor A/c Dr To Bank A/c. Depreciation is on the full ₹50,000.

Example: default and repossession (same machine)

The hirer cannot pay the third instalment and the vendor takes the machine back. Depreciation at 10% a year on cost for 2 years = ₹10,000, so book value = ₹40,000.

Amount due to the vendor = balance 12,000 + year-3 interest 1,200 = ₹13,200 (Interest A/c Dr To Vendor A/c 1,200 first).

Vendor A/c Dr 13,200 To Machinery A/c. The rest of the book value, 40,000 − 13,200 = ₹26,800, is the loss on repossession, transferred to Profit and Loss.

How to solve

  1. Find interest per instalment: interest = rate × outstanding cash price (work backwards when cash price is not given).
  2. Asset A/c Dr (full cash price) To Vendor A/c.
  3. Each instalment: Interest A/c Dr To Vendor A/c; then Vendor A/c Dr To Bank A/c.
  4. Depreciation on full cash price each year; interest and depreciation to P&L.
  5. Default and repossession (hirer): Vendor A/c Dr To Asset A/c (with the amount still due); the rest of the returned goods' book value is a loss on repossession, transferred to P&L.

Formulas

Interest = Opening balance of cash price × Rate
Total interest = Hire purchase price − Cash price

Common mistakes

  • Charging depreciation on the instalments paid instead of full cash price.
  • Deducting interest from the down payment.
