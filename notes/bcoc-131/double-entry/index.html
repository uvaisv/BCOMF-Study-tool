Accounting Process & Double Entry

High Asked in 8 of 11 papers · avg 10 marks

Mostly 10-mark theory: double entry, rules of debit and credit, process, bases of accounting.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 2 Accounting Process and RulesBlock 1 · Unit 3 Accounting Principles (3.4 Systems of book-keeping)

Key points

  • Accounting process: record in the journal → post to the ledger → prepare a trial balance → prepare final accounts → analyse and report.
  • Accounts are personal (dealings with persons), real (assets) or nominal (expenses, losses, incomes and gains).
  • Every transaction has a receiving and a giving aspect; recording both is double entry — for every debit there is an equal credit.
  • Rules — personal: debit the receiver, credit the giver; real: debit what comes in, credit what goes out; nominal: debit expenses and losses, credit incomes and gains.
  • Advantages of double entry: complete record, arithmetical check through the trial balance, profit and financial position can be found, fraud is easier to detect.
  • Single entry is an incomplete record of transactions; double entry is the complete system.
  • Accounting equation: Assets = Liabilities + Capital.

Worked examples

Example: the accounting equation, one step at a time

Ramesh starts with cash ₹1,00,000 → Assets 1,00,000 = Capital 1,00,000.

Buys goods on credit from Gopal ₹30,000 → Assets 1,30,000 = Liabilities 30,000 + Capital 1,00,000.

Sells goods costing ₹10,000 for ₹15,000 cash → cash 1,15,000 + stock 20,000 = 1,35,000 = 30,000 + capital 1,05,000 (profit adds to capital).

Pays rent ₹2,000 → Assets 1,33,000 = 30,000 + 1,03,000. Both sides stay equal every time.

Examples: applying the rules

Rent paid in cash → Rent A/c Dr (nominal: expense) To Cash A/c (real: goes out).

Goods sold to Sita on credit → Sita A/c Dr (personal: receiver) To Sales A/c.

Commission received by cheque → Bank A/c Dr (real: comes in) To Commission A/c (nominal: income).
