Coping with Stress

Critical Asked in 9 of 9 papers · avg 11 marks

Definition, nature and goals of coping; coping styles — problem-focused and emotion-focused, approach and avoidance, adaptive and maladaptive.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 5 Coping with Stress

Key points

  • Coping (Lazarus and Folkman): constantly changing thoughts and actions to manage demands that a person sees as taxing or exceeding their resources.
  • Nature: a process, not a fixed trait; conscious and effortful; changes with the situation.
  • Goals: reduce harmful conditions, tolerate or adjust to negative events, keep a positive self-image, stay emotionally balanced, maintain relationships.
  • Problem-focused coping: act on the cause — plan, gather information, solve the problem, seek practical help. Best when the situation can be changed.
  • Emotion-focused coping: manage the feelings — reappraisal, acceptance, seeking emotional support, relaxation. Best when the situation cannot be changed.
  • Other styles: approach vs avoidance coping; adaptive (exercise, problem solving, seeking support, humour) vs maladaptive (denial, substance use, withdrawal, aggression).

Worked example

Example: problem-focused vs emotion-focused coping

A student is falling behind in accounts. Problem-focused: makes a study timetable, joins a doubt-clearing class and asks a friend for notes — acting on the cause.

Her grandmother is seriously ill and nothing can be done quickly. Emotion-focused: she talks to a friend, practises breathing exercises and accepts what she cannot change — managing the feeling.
