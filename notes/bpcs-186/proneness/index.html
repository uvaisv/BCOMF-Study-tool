Stress Proneness & Moderators

Critical Asked in 9 of 9 papers · avg 10 marks

Factors that make a person prone to stress — Type A personality, perfectionism, hostility, learned helplessness and pessimism — and moderators of stress.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 3 Factors Contributing to Stress Proneness

Key points

  • Stress proneness: a tendency to experience stress more often or more intensely because of personality and thinking patterns.
  • Type A personality (Friedman and Rosenman): time urgency, impatience, competitiveness, hostility, doing many things at once; the hostility part is linked to heart disease. Type B is relaxed and less stress-prone.
  • Perfectionism: unrealistically high standards, fear of mistakes, harsh self-criticism — every small failure becomes a stressor.
  • Hostility: cynical mistrust and anger towards others; keeps the body in a state of arousal.
  • Learned helplessness (Seligman): after repeated events one cannot control, a person stops trying even when control is possible. Learned pessimism: explaining bad events as permanent, pervasive and personal.
  • Other factors: low self-esteem, external locus of control, negative thinking, poor coping skills.
  • Moderators — factors that reduce (or increase) the effect of stress: social support, hardiness (Kobasa: commitment, control, challenge), internal locus of control, self-efficacy, optimism, good coping resources, health and lifestyle.

Worked example

Example: two students, the same exam

Riya (Type A) studies with a timer, gets angry at small delays and cannot relax even after preparing well; a single wrong answer in a mock test feels like disaster (perfectionism). Arjun failed twice before and now says 'whatever I do, I'll fail' and stops studying — learned helplessness.

The exam is the same; their personality and thinking make them more prone to stress.

How to write the answer

Meaning → each factor with a one-line example → conclusion on managing it.
