Stress: Concept, Types, Sources & Measurement

Critical Asked in 9 of 9 papers · avg 10 marks

Meaning and nature of stress, types (eustress and distress, acute and chronic), sources, symptoms, and ways to measure stress.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 1 Stress: An Introduction

Key points

  • Stress (Hans Selye): the body's non-specific response to any demand made on it. A stressor is the demand; stress is the response.
  • Nature: individual (depends on how a person sees the demand), can be positive or negative, involves body, mind and behaviour.
  • Types: eustress (positive, motivating) and distress (harmful); acute, episodic acute and chronic stress; hypostress (too little challenge) and hyperstress (overload).
  • Sources: environmental (noise, pollution, crowding), social (relationships, life events), work or academic (workload, deadlines, role conflict, job insecurity), personal (expectations, personality), physical (illness, poor sleep), daily hassles, frustration and conflict.
  • Symptoms: physical (headache, fatigue, high BP), emotional (anxiety, irritability), cognitive (poor concentration, worry), behavioural (sleep and appetite changes, withdrawal, substance use).
  • Measurement: self-report scales — Holmes and Rahe Social Readjustment Rating Scale (life change units), Perceived Stress Scale; physiological measures — heart rate, blood pressure, cortisol, skin conductance; interviews and behavioural observation.
  • The book's measures: physiological (blood pressure, pulse and breathing rate, polygraph; stress hormones such as epinephrine, norepinephrine and cortisol) — useful but costly, and the changes may have other causes — and psychological tests (standardised questionnaires and scales).

Worked example

Example: eustress vs distress

Being chosen to lead a college fest is a demand that excites and energises a student — eustress. The same student facing three assignments, a part-time job and a family illness in one week feels overwhelmed, sleeps badly and gets headaches — distress.

Symptoms here are physical (headache), emotional (irritability), cognitive (poor concentration) and behavioural (skipping meals).
