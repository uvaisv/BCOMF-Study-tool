Models of Stress

Critical Asked in 8 of 9 papers · avg 9 marks

Fight-or-flight response, General Adaptation Syndrome, the cognitive appraisal model and other models of stress.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 2 Models of Stress

Key points

  • Fight-or-flight response (Walter Cannon): a threat activates the sympathetic nervous system and adrenaline — faster heart rate and breathing, energy released — preparing the body to fight or flee.
  • General Adaptation Syndrome (Hans Selye): three stages — alarm (body mobilises), resistance (body adapts, resources used), exhaustion (resources run out, illness follows).
  • Cognitive appraisal model (Lazarus and Folkman): stress depends on how a person appraises an event — primary appraisal (is it harmful, threatening or a challenge?) and secondary appraisal (can I cope?); reappraisal follows.
  • GAS vs cognitive appraisal: GAS is physiological and says all stressors produce the same response; the cognitive model is psychological and says the response depends on the person's interpretation.
  • Life events model (Holmes and Rahe): major life changes add up and raise the risk of illness. Person–environment fit: stress comes from a mismatch between a person's abilities and the demands of the setting.

Worked example

Example: Selye's GAS and Lazarus's appraisal

A shop owner's business is hit by a new competitor. Alarm: shock, poor sleep. Resistance: works 14-hour days and copes for months. Exhaustion: falls ill when reserves run out.

Lazarus: another owner faces the same competitor, sees it as a challenge (primary appraisal) and believes she can respond with home delivery (secondary appraisal) — so she feels far less stress.

Holmes and Rahe: marriage (50) + a new line of work (36) + moving house (20) + a holiday (13) in one year score 119 — low risk; scores above 300 are linked to a high risk of illness.

How to write the answer

Name the model and its author → stages or components as a diagram → one example → conclusion.
