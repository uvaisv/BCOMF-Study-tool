Promoter, Formation & Pre-incorporation Contracts

Moderate Asked in 6 of 9 papers · avg 9 marks

Who a promoter is, functions, legal position, duties and liabilities; formation and incorporation of a company; pre-incorporation contracts.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 3 PromoterBlock 1 · Unit 4 Formation of a Company

Key points

  • Promoter (Sec 2(69)): named as such in the prospectus or annual return, or who controls the company's affairs, or on whose directions the board acts. Professionals acting in a professional capacity are not promoters.
  • Functions: conceive the idea, investigate feasibility, assemble people and resources, choose name and directors, prepare memorandum and articles, register the company, arrange capital.
  • Legal position: neither agent nor trustee of the company (it does not yet exist), but in a fiduciary position.
  • Duties: no secret profit, disclose any interest, act in good faith, make full disclosure in the prospectus.
  • Liabilities: account for secret profits; compensation for misstatement in the prospectus (Sec 35) and criminal liability (Sec 34); personal liability on pre-incorporation contracts; fraud (Sec 447).
  • Formation and incorporation: promotion → name reservation and filing (SPICe+ form with memorandum, articles and declarations) → certificate of incorporation, which is conclusive evidence of registration (Sec 7) → declaration for commencement of business within 180 days (Sec 10A).
  • Pre-incorporation contracts: made before incorporation; the company cannot ratify them (Kelner v Baxter) and the promoter is personally liable. Under the Specific Relief Act, 1963 (Sec 15(h), 19(e)) they can be enforced by or against the company if made for its purposes and accepted by it after incorporation.

Worked examples

Example: pre-incorporation contract (Kelner v Baxter)

Promoters bought wine 'on behalf of' a hotel company that was still being formed. The company was later formed, 'ratified' the purchase, used the wine and then failed. The promoters were held personally liable: a company cannot ratify a contract made before it existed.

Example: secret profit

A promoter buys land for ₹50 lakh and, without disclosing it, sells it to the new company for ₹80 lakh. They must account to the company for the ₹30 lakh secret profit, or the company may rescind the purchase.

How to write the answer

Definition → functions or duties as headings → legal position with a case → conclusion.
