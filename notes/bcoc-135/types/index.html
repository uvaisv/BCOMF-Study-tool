Types of Companies; Public vs Private

High Asked in 7 of 9 papers · avg 12 marks

Types of companies by incorporation, liability, control and ownership; public vs private companies, privileges of a private company, conversion; one-person company; company limited by guarantee.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 2 Public and Private CompaniesBlock 1 · Unit 1 Nature and Types of Companies (1.9–1.11 Kinds of companies)

Key points

  • By incorporation: chartered, statutory (special Act, e.g. LIC) and registered companies. By liability: limited by shares, limited by guarantee (members promise to contribute a set amount on winding up — used by clubs, charities), unlimited. By control: holding and subsidiary, associate. By ownership: government company (51% or more held by government), foreign company.
  • Private company (Sec 2(68)): restricts transfer of shares, up to 200 members (excluding employees), cannot invite the public to subscribe; minimum 2 members and 2 directors; name ends in 'Private Limited'.
  • Public company: not private; minimum 7 members and 3 directors; shares freely transferable; can raise money from the public by prospectus.
  • Privileges of a private company: fewer members and directors, no prospectus, quorum of 2, no ceiling on managerial remuneration (Sec 197), relaxed rules on loans to directors and related parties, no need for independent directors in most cases.
  • Converting private to public: special resolution altering articles to remove the restrictions; raise members to 7 and directors to 3; change the name; file with the Registrar; issue a prospectus if raising money from the public.
  • One-person company (Sec 2(62)): one member, a natural person resident in India, with a nominee; '(OPC)' in the name; lighter compliance.

Worked examples

Examples: classify the company

LIC, created by its own Act of Parliament → statutory company. A sports club whose members promise ₹1,000 each if it is wound up → company limited by guarantee. A Ltd holds 60% of the voting power of B Ltd → B is A's subsidiary. A company in which the government holds 51% of the paid-up capital → government company.

Example: private company to public company

A family-owned private company with 5 members and 2 directors wants to raise money from the public. It passes a special resolution to remove the restrictions in its articles, increases its members to at least 7 and directors to at least 3, drops 'Private' from its name, and files with the Registrar before issuing a prospectus.

How to write the answer

Classify in a chart → explain each type in a line → table for public vs private → conclusion.
