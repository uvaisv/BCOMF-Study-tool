Prospectus

Moderate Asked in 7 of 9 papers · avg 8 marks

Prospectus and when it is not required, deemed, shelf and red herring prospectus, misstatement, minimum subscription, book building and GDRs.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 8 Prospectus

Key points

  • Prospectus (Sec 2(70)): a document inviting the public to subscribe to a company's securities, with the details they need to decide.
  • Not required: rights issues to existing members, bonus issues, private placement (Sec 42), shares not offered to the public, issues to underwriters or in certain schemes.
  • Deemed prospectus (Sec 25): when a company allots securities to an issuing house with a view to their being offered to the public, the offer document is deemed a prospectus; this is presumed if the offer is made within 6 months of allotment or the company has not received the whole consideration.
  • Shelf prospectus (Sec 31): valid for one year for several issues, with an information memorandum for each. Red herring prospectus (Sec 32): issued before the price and quantity are fixed in book building.
  • Misstatement: civil liability to compensate investors (Sec 35) and criminal liability (Sec 34, punishable as fraud); defences include withdrawal of consent and reasonable belief in the statement.
  • Minimum subscription (Sec 39): the minimum amount stated must be received within 30 days, otherwise the money is refunded.
  • Book building: the price is discovered from bids within a price band; advantages — a fair market price, visible demand, lower cost and time.
  • Global depository receipts: receipts issued abroad representing shares of an Indian company, letting it raise foreign capital.

Worked examples

Example: minimum subscription

A company's prospectus states a minimum subscription of ₹90 lakh for a ₹1 crore issue. Thirty days after the issue opens it has received only ₹80 lakh. It cannot allot any shares and must refund the application money.

Example: deemed prospectus and book building

A company allots its whole issue to an issuing house, which offers the shares to the public two months later. That offer document is deemed to be the company's prospectus, with the same liability for misstatements.

Book building: the price band is ₹100–₹110; after bids come in, the price is fixed at ₹108, where demand covers the issue.
