Winding Up, Dissolution & NCLAT

Low Asked in 8 of 9 papers · avg 6 marks

Winding up vs dissolution, winding up by the Tribunal and its procedure, compulsory winding up, and the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 5 · Unit 20 Winding UpBlock 1 · Unit 5 Authorities under Companies Act, 2013

Key points

  • Winding up: ending a company's life by realising its assets, paying creditors and distributing any surplus to members. Dissolution: the final end — the company ceases to exist and its name is struck off.
  • Under the 2013 Act (as amended with the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016): winding up by the Tribunal (Sec 271); voluntary liquidation and insolvency cases go under the IBC.
  • Grounds for winding up by the Tribunal (compulsory winding up): special resolution of the company; acts against sovereignty, security or public order; fraudulent or unlawful conduct; default in filing financial statements or returns for 5 years; just and equitable grounds.
  • Procedure: petition (Sec 272) → admission and provisional liquidator → statement of affairs → liquidator takes custody → assets realised and claims settled in order of priority → final report → dissolution order (Sec 302).
  • NCLAT (Sec 410): hears appeals against orders of the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), and against IBBI and CCI orders; appeal within 45 days; further appeal to the Supreme Court on a question of law within 60 days.

Worked example

Example: winding up by the Tribunal, and appeals

A company has filed no financial statements or annual returns for five consecutive years. The Registrar petitions the Tribunal (NCLT), which orders winding up and appoints a liquidator to take custody of the assets, realise them and pay creditors in order of priority.

A party aggrieved by the NCLT's order may appeal to the NCLAT within 45 days, and then to the Supreme Court on a question of law within 60 days of the NCLAT's order.

How to write the answer

Meaning → table for winding up vs dissolution → grounds or procedure as steps → conclusion.
