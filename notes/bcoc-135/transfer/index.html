Transfer & Transmission of Shares

Moderate Asked in 5 of 9 papers · avg 8 marks

Procedure for transfer, refusal to register, transfer vs transmission, blank transfer and forged transfer.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 3 · Unit 11 Transfer and Transmission of Shares

Key points

  • Transfer: a voluntary act by the holder. Procedure (Sec 56): execute the instrument of transfer (Form SH-4), stamp it, lodge it with the company within 60 days with the share certificate; the board approves and enters it in the register of members; new certificate within one month. Demat shares move through the depository.
  • Refusal (Sec 58): a private company may refuse under its articles; a public company only for sufficient cause; notice of refusal within 30 days; appeal to the Tribunal.
  • Transmission: by operation of law on death, insolvency or lunacy; no instrument or stamp duty; proof such as a succession certificate or probate. Transfer vs transmission: voluntary vs by law; instrument and stamp duty vs none; transferor's liability ends vs estate stays liable.
  • Blank transfer: a signed transfer form with the transferee's name left blank, handed over with the certificate, often as security for a loan. Evils: stamp duty and tax evasion, speculation, the company does not know its real owners.
  • Forged transfer: a nullity — the true owner stays a member and must be restored to the register; the company may claim compensation from the person who lodged it; a person who bought on a certificate issued on a forged transfer may claim damages from the company (estoppel).

Worked examples

Example: transfer vs transmission

Transfer: Ravi sells 500 physical shares to Anu. They sign and stamp the transfer form (SH-4), and it is lodged with the company within 60 days with the share certificate. The board registers it and issues a new certificate within a month.

Transmission: Ravi dies and his son produces the succession certificate. The shares pass to the son by operation of law — no transfer form and no stamp duty.

Example: a forged transfer

X's signature is forged on a transfer of his shares to Y, and the company registers Y. The transfer is a nullity: X must be restored to the register. If Y had sold to Z on a certificate the company issued, Z may claim damages from the company, which in turn can recover from whoever lodged the forged transfer.
