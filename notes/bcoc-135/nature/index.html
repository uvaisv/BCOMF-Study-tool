Company: Meaning, Features & Corporate Veil

High Asked in 7 of 9 papers · avg 12 marks

Meaning and characteristics of a company, the company as an artificial person, lifting the corporate veil, illegal associations and company vs LLP.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 1 Nature and Types of Companies

Key points

  • Company (Sec 2(20)): a company incorporated under the Companies Act, 2013 or an earlier companies law.
  • Characteristics: incorporated association; separate legal entity (Salomon v Salomon, 1897); artificial person that acts through directors; perpetual succession; limited liability; transferable shares; separate property; can sue and be sued; common seal now optional.
  • Corporate veil: the separation between the company and its members. It is lifted — members are looked at — by statute (misstatement in prospectus, fraudulent conduct of business, carrying on business with fewer than the minimum members under Sec 3A) and by courts (fraud or sham to evade obligations — Gilford Motor v Horne; tax evasion; enemy character — Daimler v Continental Tyre; agency; public interest).
  • Illegal association (Sec 464): an association or partnership of more than the prescribed number (up to 100; now 50 by rules) formed for gain without registering as a company. Consequences: it cannot sue or make valid contracts, members are personally liable, and members are fined.
  • Company vs LLP: a company is governed by the Companies Act 2013, has shareholders and directors, and heavier compliance; a limited liability partnership is governed by the LLP Act 2008 and its LLP agreement, has partners and designated partners, and lighter compliance — both are separate legal entities with limited liability.

Worked examples

Example: separate legal entity (Salomon v Salomon, 1897)

Mr Salomon sold his boot business to a company in which he held almost all the shares, and was also given secured debentures. When the company failed, the unsecured creditors said the company was really Salomon. The House of Lords held the company was a separate person; Salomon, as a secured creditor, was paid before them.

Example: lifting the corporate veil (Gilford Motor v Horne)

A former managing director had promised not to solicit his old employer's customers. He set up a company, in his wife's name, to do exactly that. The court treated the company as a sham to evade his promise and granted an injunction against both him and the company.

How to write the answer

Definition → features as headings with a case → conclusion. For 'comment' questions, agree and explain with Salomon's case.
