Directors & Corporate Governance

Critical Asked in 9 of 9 papers · avg 20 marks

Appointment, qualifications and disqualifications, legal position, duties and liabilities of directors, removal, managerial remuneration, audit committee, insider trading and whistle blowing.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 4 · Unit 13 DirectorsBlock 4 · Unit 14 Managerial Remuneration

Key points

  • Director (Sec 2(34)): a person appointed to the board. Only an individual with a Director Identification Number (DIN) who has consented in writing can be appointed. Minimum: 3 in a public company, 2 in a private company, 1 in a one-person company; maximum 15 (more by special resolution); at least one resident director.
  • Disqualifications (Sec 164): unsound mind; undischarged insolvent; convicted and sentenced to 6 months or more (for 5 years after release; 7 years or more — never); disqualified by court order; calls unpaid for 6 months; convicted of a related-party offence; no DIN; director of a company that failed to file financial statements or annual returns for 3 years.
  • Legal position: agents of the company, trustees of its money and property, officers, and in some cases employees — best described as fiduciaries.
  • Duties (Sec 166): act according to the articles; act in good faith for members, employees, community and environment; use due care, skill and independent judgement; avoid conflict of interest; make no undue gain; not assign the office.
  • Liabilities — to the company: acts beyond powers, breach of duty, negligence, secret profits, misfeasance. To third parties: misstatement in prospectus, personal guarantees, acting beyond authority. Criminal: fraud (Sec 447), false statements (Sec 448), other offences under the Act.
  • Removal (Sec 169): ordinary resolution with special notice; the director must be given a hearing.
  • Managerial remuneration (Sec 197, public companies): total up to 11% of net profits; one managing or whole-time director up to 5%, all of them up to 10%; non-executive directors up to 1% (3% if there is no executive director). More needs a special resolution. Sitting fees (up to ₹1 lakh a meeting) and reimbursement of expenses are not counted.
  • Audit committee (Sec 177): in listed and certain public companies; at least 3 directors, majority independent, majority financially literate. Role: recommend auditors, review financial statements and audit, approve related-party transactions, run the vigil mechanism.
  • Insider trading: dealing in securities using unpublished price-sensitive information — illegal under SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015 because it cheats other investors and destroys market confidence. Whistle blowing: a vigil mechanism (Sec 177(9)) lets directors and employees report genuine concerns without victimisation.

Worked examples

Example: managerial remuneration limits (Section 197)

A public company's net profit is ₹10 crore.

Total managerial remuneration: up to 11% = ₹1.1 crore.

One managing director: up to 5% = ₹50 lakh. All managing and whole-time directors together: up to 10% = ₹1 crore.

Non-executive directors: up to 1% = ₹10 lakh (3% if there is no executive director). Sitting fees are outside these limits. Paying more needs a special resolution.

Example: disqualification

A director is convicted and sentenced to one year's imprisonment. They cannot be appointed a director for 5 years after release. Had the sentence been 7 years or more, they could never be appointed.

Common mistakes

  • Quoting the old 1956 Act section numbers — use the Companies Act, 2013.
