Share Capital, Shares & Debentures

Critical Asked in 9 of 9 papers · avg 28 marks

Types of shares, preference shares and their redemption, DVR shares, sweat equity, premium, rights and bonus issues, allotment, buyback, forfeiture, share vs debenture and stock, debentures.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 3 · Unit 9 Share and Loan CapitalBlock 3 · Unit 10 Issue and Allotment of Shares

Key points

  • Share (Sec 2(84)): a share in the capital of a company. Types (Sec 43): equity shares — with voting rights, or with differential rights as to dividend or voting (DVR) — and preference shares.
  • Preference shares carry a preferential right to dividend and repayment of capital. Types: cumulative/non-cumulative, participating/non-participating, convertible/non-convertible, redeemable. Irredeemable preference shares cannot be issued; redemption within 20 years (30 for infrastructure).
  • Redemption (Sec 55): only fully paid shares; out of profits available for dividend (transferring an equal amount to a capital redemption reserve) or the proceeds of a fresh issue; premium on redemption from profits or securities premium.
  • Non-voting shares: a company cannot issue equity shares with no vote, but may issue DVR shares with lower voting rights under the rules; preference shares vote only on matters affecting them or when dividend is unpaid.
  • Sweat equity (Sec 54): shares issued to directors or employees at a discount or for know-how, intellectual property or value addition — needs a special resolution, a 3-year lock-in and is capped yearly.
  • Premium (Sec 52): excess over face value goes to a securities premium account; used for fully paid bonus shares, writing off preliminary and issue expenses, premium on redemption, buyback.
  • Rights issue (Sec 62): new shares offered to existing shareholders in proportion, for payment, renounceable. Bonus issue (Sec 63): free fully paid shares from reserves.
  • Allotment: acceptance of an application. General rules — by proper authority (the board), within reasonable time, communicated, absolute and unconditional. Statutory rules (Sec 39) — minimum subscription received within 30 days, application money at least 5% of face value, refund if not received, return of allotment filed; share certificates within 2 months (Sec 56).
  • Buyback (Sec 68): authorised by articles; special resolution (board resolution for up to 10%); up to 25% of paid-up capital and free reserves; from free reserves, securities premium or a different kind of issue; debt–equity ratio no more than 2:1 afterwards; fully paid shares; completed within a year; bought shares extinguished.
  • Forfeiture: cancelling shares for non-payment of calls, if the articles allow; 14 days' notice, board resolution; the member loses the shares and membership but stays liable for unpaid calls; the company may reissue them.
  • Share vs debenture: owner vs creditor; dividend (only from profits) vs fixed interest; voting vs no voting; repaid last vs first; debentures can be secured by a charge. Share vs stock: stock is consolidated fully paid shares, transferable in fractions, without distinctive numbers (Sec 61).
  • Debentures (Sec 2(30)): instruments acknowledging a debt; types — secured/unsecured, redeemable, convertible/non-convertible, registered/bearer; a debenture trustee is needed for a public issue.

Worked examples

Example: how much can be bought back?

Paid-up capital ₹10 crore; free reserves ₹30 crore; total ₹40 crore.

With a special resolution: up to 25% = ₹10 crore. With only a board resolution: up to 10% = ₹4 crore.

After the buyback, debt may not exceed twice the capital and free reserves (2 : 1), and the bought-back shares must be extinguished.

Example: premium, rights and bonus

A company issues 1,00,000 shares of ₹10 at ₹15: ₹10 lakh goes to share capital and ₹5 lakh to the securities premium account, which it may later use to issue fully paid bonus shares.

Rights issue '1 for 4': a member holding 400 shares may buy 100 new shares at the offer price, or renounce the right to someone else. A bonus issue would give the same member free shares from reserves.

How to write the answer

Meaning → rules or types as headings with the section number → a table for any 'distinguish' part → conclusion.

Common mistakes

  • Saying preference shares can be irredeemable — Sec 55 forbids it.
