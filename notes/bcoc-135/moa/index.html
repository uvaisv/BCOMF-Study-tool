Memorandum of Association & Ultra Vires

High Asked in 9 of 9 papers · avg 16 marks

Meaning and purpose of the memorandum, its clauses, alteration of name and object clauses, binding effect, constructive notice and the doctrine of ultra vires.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 6 Memorandum of Association

Key points

  • Memorandum of Association: the company's charter — defines its name, domicile, objects, liability and capital, and its relations with outsiders.
  • Clauses (Sec 4): name (ending 'Limited' or 'Private Limited'); registered office (the state); objects; liability; capital; association or subscription; nominee clause for a one-person company.
  • Name change (Sec 13): special resolution and Central Government (Registrar) approval, except when only 'Private' is added or deleted; new certificate of incorporation. Effects: the company stays the same legal person — rights, obligations and legal proceedings continue.
  • Object clause change: special resolution; if money raised by prospectus is unused, dissenting shareholders get an exit and the change is advertised; filed with the Registrar.
  • Binding effect (Sec 10): memorandum and articles bind the company and members as if signed by each member; they do not bind outsiders.
  • Constructive notice: the memorandum and articles are public documents, so outsiders are deemed to know their contents.
  • Doctrine of ultra vires: an act beyond the objects in the memorandum is void and cannot be ratified even by all members (Ashbury Railway Carriage v Riche, 1875). Consequences: the act is void; members can get an injunction; directors are personally liable; the company cannot sue or be sued on it. Example: a railway-carriage company financing a railway line.

Worked examples

Example: ultra vires (Ashbury Railway Carriage v Riche, 1875)

A company's objects were to make and sell railway carriages and related business. Its directors agreed to finance the building of a railway line in Belgium. The House of Lords held the contract ultra vires and void — outside the objects — and even a later approval by all the shareholders could not make it valid.

Example: changing the name

'Sunrise Textiles Limited' wants to become 'Sunrise Fabrics Limited'. It passes a special resolution and gets the Registrar's approval (for the Central Government), and a fresh certificate of incorporation is issued. Its contracts, loans and pending court cases continue unaffected — it is still the same company.

How to write the answer

Meaning → clauses as a numbered list → alteration or doctrine with a case → conclusion.
