Membership of a Company

High Asked in 6 of 9 papers · avg 10 marks

Who is a member, member vs shareholder, who can become a member, modes of becoming a member, rights of members and cessation of membership.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 3 · Unit 12 Membership of a Company

Key points

  • Member (Sec 2(55)): subscribers to the memorandum; persons who agree in writing and are entered in the register of members; beneficial owners in a depository.
  • Member vs shareholder: usually the same, but a subscriber is a member before shares are allotted; a guarantee company without share capital has members but no shareholders; the holder of a share warrant (old law) was a shareholder but not a member.
  • Who can be a member: any person competent to contract; a minor only through a guardian and for fully paid shares; a company; a Hindu undivided family through its karta; trustees; foreign nationals subject to FEMA; a partnership firm only in the names of partners.
  • Modes: subscribing to the memorandum, application and allotment, transfer, transmission, holding out (estoppel), taking qualification shares, beneficial ownership in a depository.
  • Rights: vote; receive dividend; receive notices and attend meetings; inspect registers and minutes; get copies of financial statements; transfer shares; rights shares; apply to the Tribunal for oppression and mismanagement (Sec 241); share surplus on winding up.
  • Cessation: transfer, transmission on death or insolvency, forfeiture, surrender, buyback, rescission of allotment, winding up.

Worked examples

Examples: how people become members

A signs the memorandum when the company is formed → a member from registration, even before shares are allotted.

B buys shares on the stock exchange → becomes a member when shown as the beneficial owner in the depository's records.

C inherits shares on her father's death → becomes a member by transmission.

A minor can hold only fully paid shares, through a guardian.

Example: using a member's rights

Minority shareholders find the majority transferring company property to themselves at low prices. They can apply to the Tribunal for relief against oppression and mismanagement (Section 241).
