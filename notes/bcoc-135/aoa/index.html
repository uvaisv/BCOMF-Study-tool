Articles of Association & Indoor Management

High Asked in 6 of 9 papers · avg 11 marks

Meaning, purpose and contents of the articles, their alteration and its limits, and the doctrine of indoor management with its exceptions.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 7 Articles of Association

Key points

  • Articles of Association (Sec 5): the internal rules for managing the company — the relationship between the company and its members and among members. Model articles are in Table F.
  • Contents: share capital and rights, calls, transfer and transmission, forfeiture, meetings and voting, directors' powers and appointment, dividends, accounts, borrowing, winding up; entrenchment provisions if any.
  • Alteration (Sec 14): by special resolution. Limits: must not conflict with the Act or memorandum; must be legal and for the company's benefit (bona fide); cannot increase a member's liability without written consent; conversion of a public into a private company needs Tribunal approval.
  • Doctrine of indoor management (Royal British Bank v Turquand, 1856): outsiders dealing in good faith may assume the company's internal procedures have been followed.
  • Exceptions: the outsider knew of the irregularity; negligence or suspicious circumstances; forgery (Ruben v Great Fingall); act clearly beyond the officer's authority; the outsider is an insider (e.g. a director).
  • Indoor management protects outsiders; constructive notice protects the company.

Worked examples

Example: indoor management (Royal British Bank v Turquand, 1856)

A company's articles let the directors borrow on bonds if a general meeting passed a resolution allowing it. The directors issued a bond to the bank without such a resolution. The company was still bound: the bank could assume that this internal step had been completed.

Example: an exception — forgery (Ruben v Great Fingall)

A company secretary forged directors' signatures on a share certificate and gave it as security for a loan. The company was not bound: the doctrine of indoor management does not cover forgery, because a forged document is a nullity.

How to write the answer

Meaning → purpose/contents → doctrine with the Turquand case → exceptions as a list → conclusion.
