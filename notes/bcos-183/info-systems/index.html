Business Information Systems (MIS, DSS, ERP)

High Asked in 8 of 9 papers · avg 12 marks

Business and information systems, MIS vs DSS, ERP, data vs information, business ownership forms.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 5 Business Information System (5.2–5.3, 5.5–5.7)

Key points

  • Data are raw facts; information is data processed into a meaningful form for decisions.
  • A business information system supports effective decision-making; key aspects are the business plan, market analysis, sales and funding, organisation management and financial projections.
  • Information systems divide into operation support systems (e.g. transaction processing) and management support systems (MIS, DSS).
  • MIS: a computerised database that stores the firm's (mainly financial) information and gives regular reports — e.g. bank, railway and inventory management systems.
  • DSS: analyses data and presents it so users can make decisions; works on 'decide – act – review'. Types: communication-, data-, document-, knowledge- and model-driven.
  • MIS vs DSS: primary vs highest decision level; reports vs analysis of choices; operational efficiency vs effective decisions; large volume of transaction data vs smaller filtered data; summary reports vs decision analysis.
  • ERP: integrates information between customer, vendor and manufacturer over networks; it evolved from MRP and MRP II.
  • Forms of business by ownership: sole proprietorship, partnership, company, cooperative.

Worked example

Example: information systems in a supermarket chain

Every bill at the counter is recorded — a transaction processing (operation support) system.

The MIS turns these into a weekly report: sales by store and product, and stock below the reorder level.

The DSS lets the manager ask 'what if we give 10% off on biscuits next month?' and see the likely effect on sales and profit.

ERP links it all — sales, purchase, warehouse, suppliers and accounts — so a sale at one store updates stock and triggers a purchase order automatically.
