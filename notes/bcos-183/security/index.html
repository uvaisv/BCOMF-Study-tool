Cyber Security, Authentication & Data Protection

Critical Asked in 9 of 9 papers · avg 25 marks

CIA principles, authentication vs authorisation, 2FA and OTP, CAPTCHA, identity theft, firewall, GDPR vs DPD.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 6 IT Security Measures in BusinessBlock 2 · Unit 5 Business Information System (5.8 GDPR)

Key points

  • IT security protects confidentiality (only authorised access), integrity (data not altered) and availability (usable when needed).
  • The book's key principles: identification (user states who they are), authentication (identity checked against stored data) and authorisation (what the user may access).
  • Security actions for a business include a firewall, antivirus software, complex passwords, regular backup, penetration testing and limiting access to critical data.
  • Cryptography protects data; an electronic (digital) signature confirms who sent a message and that it is genuine.
  • Identity theft: stealing personal details (card, Aadhaar, passwords) to commit fraud.
  • CAPTCHA ('Completely Automated Public Turing test to tell Computers and Humans Apart', 2003) keeps bots out of forms and logins. Types: text, image, audio, mathematical, logic and gamification.
  • OTP (one-time password): a code valid for one login or transaction, sent to the registered mobile or made by a token; with the normal password it gives two-factor authentication.
  • GDPR replaced the EU Data Protection Directive (DPD, 95/46/EC). It widens 'personal data' (biometric, genetic, economic identity), gives the right to access data and to be forgotten, makes data processors as well as controllers responsible, and requires 'privacy by design' with stiff penalties.

Worked examples

Example: identification, authentication and authorisation at a bank website

A customer types their customer ID (identification), enters the password and then an OTP sent to their registered mobile (authentication — two factors: something they know and something they have). Once inside, they can see only their own accounts and transfer up to their set limit (authorisation).

Example: identity theft by phishing

An SMS says 'Your KYC will expire — update now' with a link to a fake bank page. The victim enters card details and the OTP, and money is withdrawn. Safeguards: never share OTPs or PINs, check the website address, and use the bank's own app.

How to write the answer

Definition → principles with one example each → a comparison table where asked → conclusion.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing up authentication and authorisation.
