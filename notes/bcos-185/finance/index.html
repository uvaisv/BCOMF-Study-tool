Financial & Non-financial Resources

Critical Asked in 8 of 9 papers · avg 18 marks

Sources of finance (long and short term), factors in choosing a source, non-financial resources (human, physical, technology, information, networks, mentoring).

In your IGNOU bookBlock 3 · Unit 11 Mobilising Financial ResourcesBlock 3 · Unit 12 Mobilising Non-Financial Resources

Key points

  • Financial resources — owned: own savings, family and friends, equity; borrowed: term loans from banks and financial institutions, debentures, public deposits.
  • Start-up finance: angel investors, venture capital, private equity, crowdfunding, government schemes (MUDRA, Stand-Up India, SIDBI), bootstrapping.
  • Short-term sources: trade credit, bank overdraft and cash credit, bill discounting, factoring, customer advances, commercial paper, invoice discounting (TReDS) — they fund working capital, keep operations running and cost less for short gaps.
  • Factors in choosing a source: cost, amount needed, period, risk, control (equity dilutes ownership), flexibility, legal form, stage of business, collateral available, market conditions.
  • Non-financial resources: human resources (skills, team), physical resources (land, building, machinery), technology, information and market knowledge, networks and relationships, mentoring and incubation support, legal and institutional support.
  • Good mentor: experienced, available, a good listener, honest in feedback, shares networks, motivates and has no conflicting interest — e.g. an incubator mentor guiding a first-time founder.
  • Entrepreneur vs intrapreneur: the entrepreneur owns the business, bears the risk and raises the capital; the intrapreneur innovates inside an existing organisation with its resources (e.g. Gmail was built inside Google).
  • PMEGP (Prime Minister's Employment Generation Programme): credit-linked subsidy for new micro enterprises, run through KVIC.

Worked examples

Example: funding a home bakery that is growing

Start: own savings ₹2 lakh (bootstrapping) plus a MUDRA loan of ₹5 lakh for ovens.

Working capital: 30 days' credit from the flour supplier (trade credit) and a bank overdraft for festival months.

Expansion to 10 outlets: an angel investor puts in ₹50 lakh for 15% equity, bringing mentoring and contacts; later a venture-capital fund invests more for faster growth. Each round dilutes the founder's control, which is the price of equity finance.

Example: entrepreneur vs intrapreneur

A woman who quits her job to start a cloud kitchen with her own money is an entrepreneur — she bears the risk. An employee who builds a new online ordering service inside a large hotel chain, using the company's money and brand, is an intrapreneur.

How to write the answer

Classify sources (owned/borrowed, long/short term) in a table → explain each in 2–3 lines → factors for choice → conclusion.
