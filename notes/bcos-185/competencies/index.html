Entrepreneurial Competencies

High Asked in 5 of 9 papers · avg 10 marks

Who an entrepreneur is, competencies for success, what makes an entrepreneur successful, whether entrepreneurs are made or born.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 4 Entrepreneurs Competencies

Key points

  • Entrepreneur: a person who spots an opportunity, organises resources and bears risk to start and run a venture for profit and growth.
  • Competencies (McClelland): initiative, seeing and acting on opportunities, persistence, information seeking, concern for quality, commitment, efficiency orientation, systematic planning, problem solving, self-confidence, assertiveness, persuasion, use of influence strategies, monitoring, concern for employees' welfare.
  • Other success factors: risk-taking, creativity and innovation, leadership, decision-making, networking, financial literacy, adaptability.
  • Made, not born: most competencies can be learnt through education, training (EDPs), experience, mentoring and exposure — traits like risk appetite may be inborn but are shaped by environment; give an example (Dhirubhai Ambani, first-generation founders of start-ups).
  • Answer with a clear view — agree that entrepreneurs are mostly made, and give reasons.

Worked example

Example: McClelland's competencies in a real story

A vegetable vendor notices that restaurants struggle to get fresh vegetables early (seeing an opportunity). She asks restaurant owners what they need (information seeking), borrows a small van (initiative), plans routes and delivery times (systematic planning), keeps delivering on time through the monsoon (persistence), and replaces any bad stock free (concern for quality).

Within two years she supplies 40 restaurants — the skills were learnt on the way, which supports the view that entrepreneurs are mostly made.

How to write the answer

Meaning → list the competencies with one example each → conclusion. For 'made not born', state your view, give 5–6 reasons, then a balanced conclusion.
