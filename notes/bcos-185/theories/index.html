Theories & Models of Entrepreneurship

High Asked in 5 of 9 papers · avg 9 marks

Economic, psychological, sociological, anthropological and management theories; models of entrepreneurship development.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 1 An Introduction to Entrepreneurship (1.6–1.7)

Key points

  • Economic theories: Schumpeter's innovation theory (the entrepreneur as innovator — new product, method, market, source of supply, organisation); Knight's risk and uncertainty theory; Cantillon's risk-bearing; Leibenstein's X-efficiency gap-filling.
  • Psychological theories: McClelland's need for achievement (nAch); Hagen's withdrawal of status respect; Kunkel's behavioural theory.
  • Sociological theories: Max Weber (the Protestant ethic, religious beliefs); Cochran's role expectations and social sanctions; Hoselitz's marginal groups.
  • Anthropological theory: culture and social context shape entrepreneurship.
  • Management/opportunity theory: Peter Drucker — the entrepreneur searches for change and exploits it as an opportunity; resource-based theory (access to finance, human and social capital).
  • Models of entrepreneurship: Timmons model (the founder balances opportunity, resources and team); Shapero's entrepreneurial event model (a trigger event plus perceived desirability and feasibility leads to a new venture); Gartner's framework (individual, organisation, environment, process). In India, entrepreneurship development programmes (EDPs) grew from McClelland's achievement-motivation training in the Kakinada experiment (1964) and the Gujarat model of the 1970s (identify, train and support new entrepreneurs).
  • For India: argue for an integrated view — McClelland's achievement training (used in EDPs) combined with government support and innovation.
  • The book groups theories as psychological (McClelland's need for achievement, Rotter's locus of control, Frese's action regulation), economic (classical, Cantillon's risk-bearing, Schumpeter's innovation), opportunity-based, sociological and anthropological (culture and community beliefs), plus network and resource-based theories.
  • Models of entrepreneurship in the book: ambitious innovators (radical, high-risk ventures like tech start-ups), adaptive innovators, solo self-employed, managerial employers and social entrepreneurs.

Worked examples

Example: Schumpeter's five kinds of innovation

A new product — an electric scooter. A new method — making it on a robotic assembly line. A new market — exporting it to African cities. A new source of supply — sodium-ion batteries instead of lithium. A new organisation — a subscription model instead of outright sale. Each counts as entrepreneurial innovation.

Example: McClelland in India and Shapero's trigger event

In the Kakinada experiment (1964), local businessmen were given achievement-motivation training; those trained afterwards started or expanded more businesses — the basis of India's EDPs.

Shapero: a trigger event, such as losing a job, together with a sense that starting a business is both desirable and feasible, leads a person to start one.

How to write the answer

Group theories under Economic / Psychological / Sociological / Management headings → give each theorist and one line → your view for India → conclusion.
