Family Business

Critical Asked in 6 of 9 papers · avg 17 marks

Concept, types, challenges, conflicts and their management, the systems theory of family business.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 4 · Unit 14 Family Businesses in India

Key points

  • Family business: owned and managed by members of one or more families, with the intention of passing it to the next generation (e.g. Tata, Birla, Godrej, Bajaj).
  • Types: family-owned and family-managed; family-owned but professionally managed; joint family businesses; partnership among family members; family-controlled listed companies.
  • Challenges: succession planning, conflicts among members, resistance to change, nepotism over merit, informal decision-making, limited capital, keeping family and business apart, retaining professional managers.
  • Kinds of conflict: between generations, among siblings and cousins, between family and non-family managers, over ownership and money, over roles and succession, values and lifestyle.
  • Managing conflict: a family constitution, a family council, clear roles and succession plan, professional managers and an independent board, open communication, mediation, a fair compensation policy.
  • Systems theory (three-circle model — Tagiuri and Davis): family, ownership and business are overlapping systems; a person's position in the overlaps explains their interests and conflicts.
  • Other theories of family business in the book: agency, stewardship, resource-based, socio-emotional wealth and stakeholder theory.

Worked examples

Example: the three-circle model

The founder owns shares, works in the business and is family → the centre of all three circles.

His daughter owns shares and is family but works elsewhere → family + ownership: she wants dividends.

A nephew who works as manager but owns no shares → family + business: he wants a good salary and promotion.

A professional CFO with no shares → business only.

Their different positions explain why they disagree over dividends versus reinvestment.

Example: managing conflict

Two brothers disagree over who will lead after their father retires. The family writes a constitution: family members must work five years outside before joining, the board picks the CEO on merit, and a family council meets every quarter to settle disputes.
