Rural, Social, Agri & Techno Entrepreneurship

Moderate Asked in 8 of 9 papers · avg 6 marks

Rural entrepreneurship and its challenges, social entrepreneurship, agri-entrepreneurship, techno-entrepreneurship and its factors.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 3 Dimensions of Entrepreneurship

Key points

  • Rural entrepreneurship: enterprises in rural areas using local resources (agro-processing, handicrafts, dairy) — creates rural jobs and checks migration.
  • Challenges in rural areas: finance, poor infrastructure (roads, power, internet), lack of skills and training, limited market access, middlemen, low risk-taking, weak institutional support.
  • Social entrepreneurship: ventures that solve social problems with a sustainable business model — the aim is social impact, not just profit (Amul, SELCO, Goonj, Grameen Bank).
  • Agri-entrepreneurship: agriculture-based businesses — organic farming, food processing, agri-inputs, dairy, poultry, agri-tech (DeHaat, Ninjacart); supported by FPOs and schemes.
  • Techno-entrepreneurship: ventures built on technology or innovation (software, biotech, fintech). Factors: R&D and innovation, technical skills, venture capital, incubators and university links, IP protection, government policy, market demand.
  • The book's dimensions of entrepreneurship also include women entrepreneurship, ecopreneurship (green ventures), cultural, heritage and tourism, and international entrepreneurship.

Worked example

Examples: four kinds of entrepreneurship

Rural: a women's self-help group makes and sells pickles and papad from local produce.

Social: an enterprise sells solar home lights to low-income households on easy instalments — the aim is energy access, earned through a sustainable business.

Agri: farmers form a farmer producer organisation (FPO) to sort, grade and sell vegetables directly to supermarkets.

Techno: engineers incubated at a technology institute build drones for crop spraying, backed by venture capital and a patent.
