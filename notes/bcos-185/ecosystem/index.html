Entrepreneurial Ecosystem

Moderate Asked in 7 of 9 papers · avg 8 marks

Meaning of an entrepreneurial ecosystem, its components, factors influencing it, India's ecosystem.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 2 Entrepreneurial Eco-system

Key points

  • Entrepreneurial ecosystem: the interconnected set of people, institutions, resources and policies in a region that supports the birth and growth of new ventures.
  • Components (Isenberg's six domains): policy (government, regulation, incentives), finance (banks, angel investors, VC), culture (attitude to risk and failure, success stories), supports (incubators, accelerators, infrastructure, professional services), human capital (education, skilled workforce), markets (customers, networks).
  • Factors influencing the ecosystem: government policy, access to capital, education and skills, infrastructure, culture, universities and research, large companies, market size, networks and mentors.
  • India's ecosystem: third-largest start-up ecosystem in the world, over 100 unicorns; Start-up India, Atal Innovation Mission, incubators at IITs/IIMs, hubs in Bengaluru, Delhi-NCR, Mumbai, Hyderabad; UPI and India Stack as digital public infrastructure.
  • Determinants of entrepreneurship: economic, social, cultural, psychological and political factors (see Entrepreneurship & Economic Development).

Worked example

Example: Isenberg's six domains in Bengaluru

Policy: state start-up policy and Start-up India recognition. Finance: many angel networks and venture-capital firms. Culture: failure is accepted and founders' success stories inspire others. Supports: incubators, co-working spaces, lawyers and accountants who serve start-ups. Human capital: engineering colleges and experienced tech workers. Markets: large companies and a big customer base to sell to.

A founder there finds all six within reach, which is why ecosystems cluster in a few cities.

How to write the answer

Meaning → a diagram of the six domains → each domain in 2–3 lines → India example → conclusion.
