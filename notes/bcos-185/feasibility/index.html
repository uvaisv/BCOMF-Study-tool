Feasibility & Market Research

Moderate Asked in 7 of 9 papers · avg 9 marks

Feasibility analysis of a business proposal (market, technical, financial, organisational), market research process, critical factors for a new venture.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 8 Business Plan FeasibilityBlock 2 · Unit 6 Market Research

Key points

  • Feasibility analysis: checking whether a business idea is viable before investing.
  • Components: market feasibility (demand, customers, competition, price), technical feasibility (technology, raw materials, location, capacity), financial feasibility (cost, funding, profitability), organisational/managerial feasibility (skills, team), legal and environmental feasibility.
  • Financial analysis of a proposal: project cost, means of finance, projected income statement and balance sheet, cash-flow projection, break-even analysis, ratios (DSCR, return on investment), payback period, NPV and IRR, sensitivity analysis.
  • Market research: systematic collection and analysis of information about customers, competitors and market. Process: define the problem → research design → collect data (primary and secondary) → analyse → report and act. Significance: reduces risk, identifies opportunities, sets price and product, supports the business plan.
  • Critical factors for a new venture: a viable idea, market demand, adequate finance, the right team, location, technology, legal compliance, timing, competition, risk tolerance.

Worked example

Example: feasibility of a cloud kitchen

Market: a survey of 200 office workers nearby shows demand for healthy lunches at ₹150; three competitors exist, none healthy-focused.

Technical: a 400 sq ft kitchen, two cooks and delivery through apps are available.

Financial: investment ₹10 lakh; expected net cash inflow ₹2.5 lakh a year → payback in 4 years; break-even at about 120 meals a day.

Legal: an FSSAI licence and a trade licence are needed.

Only if all four look sound does the founder go ahead.

How to write the answer

Meaning → components as headings with one line each → conclusion.
