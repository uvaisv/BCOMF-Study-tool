Report Writing

Moderate Asked in 6 of 12 papers · avg 5 marks

Stages of preparing a report, long and short reports, and guidelines for preparing reports.

In your IGNOU bookUnit 11 Reporting

Key points

  • Stages: define the purpose and readers → collect information → organise and analyse → outline → write the draft → revise and edit → present.
  • Long report: formal, detailed, for complex matters — title page, contents, executive summary, introduction, methods, findings, conclusions, recommendations, appendices.
  • Short report: brief and often in letter or memo form — progress, field visit or event reports.
  • Guidelines: clear purpose, accurate facts, logical structure with headings, simple language, objectivity, tables and charts where useful, brevity, correct references.

Worked example

Example: a short field-visit report

To: Programme Manager · From: Field Officer · Date: 12 March

Purpose: visit to three reading centres in Block A.

Findings: two centres run regularly with about 25 children each; the third has had no volunteer for two weeks.

Recommendation: recruit and train a replacement volunteer by 31 March; meanwhile the Block coordinator to cover it twice a week.

How to write the answer

Meaning → stages as a numbered list → an NGO report example → conclusion.
