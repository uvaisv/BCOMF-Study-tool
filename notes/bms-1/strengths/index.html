Assessing Strengths & Weaknesses of an NGO

Critical Asked in 9 of 12 papers · avg 10 marks

Formats, criteria and checklists for assessing an NGO's strengths and weaknesses, critical success factors, efficiency measures, organisational audit, impact analysis, core competencies and capacity building.

In your IGNOU bookUnit 6 SWOT AnalysisUnit 7 Process of Management (7.6 Core competency and capacity building)

Key points

  • Strengths and weaknesses are internal factors; they are assessed in functional areas — programmes and operations, finance, human resources, marketing and fund-raising, governance and management.
  • Two dimensions of evaluation: effectiveness (is the NGO achieving its mission and outcomes?) and efficiency (are resources used well?).
  • Formats and criteria: functional checklists, comparison with past performance, comparison with similar NGOs (benchmarking), stakeholder feedback, critical success factors.
  • Checklist examples: finance — diversity and stability of funds, cost control, audited accounts; HR — skills, turnover, training, volunteer base; marketing — donor base, visibility, communication; operations — coverage, quality, timeliness.
  • Critical success factors: the few areas an NGO must do well to succeed (e.g. community trust, funding, skilled staff). Efficiency measures: cost per beneficiary, admin cost ratio, output per worker.
  • Organisational audit: a systematic review of the NGO's structure, systems, finances and performance against its mission.
  • Impact analysis: measuring the change the NGO's work brings to beneficiaries — shows donors results, improves programmes, builds credibility.
  • Core competencies: what the NGO does best (e.g. community mobilisation). Capacity building: strengthening people, systems and resources so the NGO can grow and sustain its work.

Worked example

Example: measuring an NGO's efficiency

An NGO spent ₹20 lakh last year and reached 4,000 children → cost per beneficiary ₹500. Of that, ₹3 lakh went on administration → admin cost ratio 15%.

A checklist review finds strengths in community trust and field staff, but weaknesses in fund-raising (90% of money from one donor) and record-keeping. Its core competence is mobilising mothers' groups; capacity building should focus on diversifying funds and training staff in documentation.

How to write the answer

Meaning → headed points with an NGO example → conclusion.
