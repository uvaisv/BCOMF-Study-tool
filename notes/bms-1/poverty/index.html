Poverty, Development & Exploitation

High Asked in 6 of 12 papers · avg 6 marks

The relationship between poverty and development, exploitation and vulnerability, development indicators, and the UN's approach to poverty.

In your IGNOU bookUnit 3 Issues in NGO Management

Key points

  • Poverty: lack of income and of access to basic needs, services and opportunities. The idea has moved from income alone to multidimensional poverty (health, education, living standards) and capabilities.
  • Poverty and development: development means improved well-being for all; poverty persists when growth is unequal. NGOs link the two by reaching the poor directly.
  • Exploitation: the poor are exploited through low wages, debt bondage, land grabbing and middlemen — which keeps them poor.
  • Vulnerability: exposure to shocks (illness, disaster, job loss) and inability to cope. Exploitation is done to people by others; vulnerability is their exposure to risk.
  • Development indicators: per-capita income, Human Development Index, literacy, life expectancy, infant mortality, poverty ratio, gender indices.
  • The UN tackles poverty through the Sustainable Development Goals (Goal 1: end poverty), UNDP programmes and agencies like ILO, UNICEF and WFP.
  • Poverty is also powerlessness: the poor have little control over material, human, intellectual and financial resources. The World Bank's study for the World Development Report 2000/01 (60,000 poor people in 60 countries) found their defining experience was limited choice and no voice.
  • The book traces NGO thinking from aid to development, and from dependency to sustainability — helping communities stand on their own.

Worked example

Example: exploitation vs vulnerability

A brick-kiln worker borrowed ₹20,000 from the kiln owner and must work for low wages until the debt is 'repaid' — exploitation (debt bondage), done to him by another.

A small farmer with no savings or insurance loses his crop to a flood and has to sell his land — vulnerability: exposure to a shock he cannot cope with. An NGO might help the first through legal aid and the second through crop insurance and a savings group.
