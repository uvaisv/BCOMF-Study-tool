Measures of Central Tendency & Position

Critical Asked in 7 of 8 papers · avg 17 marks

Mean (arithmetic, weighted, geometric, harmonic), median and mode, their relationship in symmetrical and skewed data, which to choose, and measures of position — z scores, percentiles, quartiles and deciles.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 2 Statistical Concepts (2.4 and 2.6)

Key points

  • Central tendency is the tendency of data to cluster around a central value; the usual measures are the arithmetic mean, median and mode (also weighted, geometric and harmonic means).
  • Mean: uses every value, varies least from sample to sample, allows algebra (combined mean) — but is pulled by outliers and cannot be found for open-ended classes.
  • Median: the middle value; best for skewed data and open-ended classes, little affected by extreme values. Mode: the most frequent value; the only average for nominal data (e.g. most-preferred brand or colour), may not exist or may not be unique.
  • Geometric mean suits rates of growth and ratios (average growth over several years); harmonic mean suits rates and speeds, e.g. average price when equal amounts of money are spent on each good.
  • Symmetrical distribution: mean = median = mode. Moderately skewed: Mode = 3 Median − 2 Mean (Karl Pearson). Positive skew: mean > median > mode; negative skew: mean < median < mode.
  • Measurement scale decides the average: nominal → mode; ordinal → median; interval/ratio → mean (median if skewed).
  • Measures of position: z score = (X − mean) ÷ SD, which compares positions in different distributions; percentiles divide data into 100 parts, quartiles into 4, deciles into 10. The median is the 50th percentile and Q2.
  • Percentile rank of a value = (number of values below it + 0.5) ÷ n × 100. A percentile is a position, not a percentage of marks.

Worked examples

Example: harmonic mean (June 2024, Q1(b))

Marks X: 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 with frequencies 2, 4, 8, 3, 3 (N = 20).

Σ(f/X) = 2/10 + 4/20 + 8/30 + 3/40 + 3/50 = 0.2 + 0.2 + 0.2667 + 0.075 + 0.06 = 0.8017.

H.M. = N ÷ Σ(f/X) = 20 ÷ 0.8017 = 24.95.

Example: which average? (Dec 2023 Q2(b), June 2024 Q2(b), Dec 2024 Q2(b))

Average price: goods cost ₹10/kg and ₹5/kg and ₹20 is spent, taking ₹10 on each (equal spending). That buys 1 kg + 2 kg = 3 kg, so the average price is 20 ÷ 3 = ₹6.67/kg — the harmonic mean of 10 and 5, not the arithmetic mean of ₹7.50.

Percentile rank: scores 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 15, 18, 20. Seven scores are below 15, so its percentile rank = (7 + 0.5) ÷ 10 × 100 = 75th percentile.

Growth rates 10%, 20%, 18%, −9%, 11%: use the geometric mean of the growth factors: (1.10 × 1.20 × 1.18 × 0.91 × 1.11)^(1/5) = 1.0949, i.e. 9.49% a year (the arithmetic mean, 10%, overstates it).

How to solve

  1. Ungrouped: mean = ΣX/n; median = middle value after arranging; mode = most frequent value.
  2. Grouped: mean = Σfm/N; median = L + (N/2 − cf)/f × h; mode = L + (f₁ − f₀)/(2f₁ − f₀ − f₂) × h.
  3. Harmonic mean = n ÷ Σ(1/X) (weighted: Σf ÷ Σ(f/X)); geometric mean = (X₁ × X₂ × … × Xn)^(1/n).
  4. Percentile rank: arrange the data, count values below X, apply (below + 0.5)/n × 100.

Formulas

Mode = 3 Median − 2 Mean
H.M. = n ÷ Σ(1/X)
G.M. = (X₁X₂…Xn)^(1/n)
z = (X − X̄) ÷ s
Percentile rank = (values below + 0.5) ÷ n × 100

How to write the answer

Definition → each measure with formula, merits and limits → relationship (symmetrical vs skewed) → when to use which → short worked example.

Common mistakes

  • Using the arithmetic mean for averaging growth rates (use the geometric mean) or for rates with equal spending (use the harmonic mean).
  • Confusing percentile with percentage.
  • Writing that the mean is the best average for nominal data — only the mode works there.
