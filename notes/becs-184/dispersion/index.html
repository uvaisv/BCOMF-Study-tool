Measures of Dispersion

Moderate Asked in 5 of 8 papers · avg 9 marks

Meaning of dispersion, absolute and relative measures — range, quartile deviation, mean deviation, standard deviation and variance, coefficient of variation — and which measure is most reliable.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 2 Statistical Concepts (2.5)

Key points

  • Dispersion is the spread of values around the centre; a small dispersion means the data are uniform, a large one means they vary a lot.
  • A good measure is rigidly defined, based on all items, easy to understand and compute, little affected by extreme items and capable of algebraic treatment.
  • Absolute measures (in the units of the data): range, quartile deviation, mean deviation, standard deviation. Relative measures (unit-free, for comparison): coefficients of range, quartile deviation, mean deviation, and the coefficient of variation.
  • Range = largest − smallest; simplest but depends only on two extreme values.
  • Mean deviation = average of absolute deviations from the mean (or median); ignores signs, so it is less suited to algebra.
  • Standard deviation (Karl Pearson, 1893) = square root of the mean of squared deviations from the mean; variance = SD². For a sample, divide by n − 1 to get an unbiased estimate.
  • Standard deviation is the most reliable measure: it uses all values, is rigidly defined, is used in further analysis (correlation, tests) and varies least between samples.
  • Coefficient of variation = SD ÷ mean × 100. It is better than SD for comparing series with different units or very different means; the lower CV is the more consistent series.

Worked examples

Example: mean and SD of a sample (June 2023, Q2(a))

Heights of 7 plants (cm): 52, 35, 25, 45, 55, 20, 13. Mean = 245 ÷ 7 = 35 cm.

Deviations: 17, 0, −10, 10, 20, −15, −22; squares: 289, 0, 100, 100, 400, 225, 484 → Σd² = 1,598.

Sample SD s = √(1,598 ÷ 6) = √266.33 = 16.32 cm (divide by n − 1 because it is a sample; dividing by n would give 15.11).

Example: why CV is better for comparing

Factory A: mean wage ₹500, SD ₹50 → CV = 10%. Factory B: mean wage ₹200, SD ₹30 → CV = 15%.

A has the larger SD but the smaller CV, so wages in A are more uniform. The SD alone misleads because the means differ.

How to solve

  1. Find the mean.
  2. Take deviations d = X − mean and square them.
  3. Sample variance s² = Σd² ÷ (n − 1); s = √s² (population: divide by N).
  4. CV = s ÷ mean × 100; compare CVs to judge consistency.

Formulas

s = √[Σ(X − X̄)² ÷ (n − 1)]
σ = √[Σ(X − μ)² ÷ N]
CV = (SD ÷ Mean) × 100
Coefficient of range = (L − S) ÷ (L + S)

How to write the answer

Meaning → table of absolute vs relative measures → each with formula and one merit/limit → most reliable measure with reasons → small example.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing the SDs of two series with different means or units instead of their CVs.
  • Dividing by n instead of n − 1 when the question asks for the sample SD of a sample.
  • Forgetting the units of the SD (same as the data) and that CV is a percentage.
