Composite Index (PQLI, HDI, GDI)

Critical Asked in 7 of 8 papers · avg 16 marks

Meaning of a composite index, the ten steps in building one, handling missing values and outliers, the four methods (simple ranking, indices, mean standardisation, range equalisation), PQLI, HDI and GDI, merits and limitations.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 4 · Unit 10 Composite Index Numbers

Key points

  • A composite index combines several indicators of a many-sided idea (development, deprivation, food security) into one score, so regions or years can be ranked and compared.
  • All indicators must point the same way (unidirectional): a negative indicator such as the share of agricultural labourers is converted, e.g. 100 − value, before combining.
  • Steps in construction: theoretical framework → selection of indicators → imputation of missing data → multivariate analysis of the indicators → normalisation → weighting and aggregation → uncertainty and sensitivity analysis → going back to the detail → links to other indicators → visualisation of results.
  • Missing values: drop the cases only if few are missing and dropping does not bias the result (missing data are often commoner among poorer households); otherwise impute (e.g. a group average) and record how it was done. Extreme values (outliers) must be checked because they can become unintended benchmarks.
  • Simple ranking method: rank every unit on each indicator (1 = best), add or average the ranks; the unit with the best average rank is the most developed.
  • Indices method: express each unit's value as an index with the group average = 100, then average the indices. Mean standardisation: divide each value by the mean of that indicator (Malkangiri 73 ÷ 59 = 1.237) and average the results.
  • Range equalisation (max–min, used by UNDP for the HDI): index = (actual − minimum) ÷ (maximum − minimum), giving 0 to 1; the minimum and maximum are 'goalposts', taken from the data or set by judgement.
  • PQLI (Morris D. Morris, 1979): average of three indicators — life expectancy, infant mortality and literacy — each scaled 0–100 (worst to best) with equal weights; e.g. (75 + 40 + 65) ÷ 3 = 60. It was not a UNDP index, although one past paper calls it so.
  • HDI (UNDP): health (life expectancy at birth, goalposts 20–85 years), education (expected and mean years of schooling) and standard of living (per-capita income, PPP $, in logs); dimension indices by the max–min formula, HDI = their geometric mean. GDI = female HDI ÷ male HDI.
  • Merits: sums up many indicators in one number for policy, eases comparison across regions and over time, easy to communicate. Limitations: can mislead if badly built, choice of weights and goalposts involves value judgement, results change with the method chosen.

Worked examples

Example: simple ranking method for three districts

Indicators (all positive): literacy %, households with safe water %, villages with paved roads %.

District A: 70, 80, 40 → ranks 1, 2, 2. District B: 60, 85, 50 → ranks 2, 1, 1. District C: 50, 60, 30 → ranks 3, 3, 3.

Average rank: A = 5/3 = 1.67, B = 4/3 = 1.33, C = 3. B is the most developed and C the most backward.

Had one indicator been negative (e.g. % agricultural labourers), it would first be converted (100 − value) so that all point the same way.

Example: range equalisation and PQLI

Female literacy in a district is 54% with goalposts 10% (minimum) and 70% (maximum): index = (54 − 10) ÷ (70 − 10) = 0.733. Doing this for every indicator and averaging gives the district's composite index (for the HDI, a geometric mean of the dimension indices is used).

PQLI: if a country rates 75 on life expectancy, 40 on infant mortality and 65 on literacy (each on a 0–100 scale), PQLI = (75 + 40 + 65) ÷ 3 = 60.

How to solve

  1. Make every indicator point the same way (positive or negative).
  2. Ranking: rank each indicator, average the ranks. Indices: value ÷ group average × 100, then average. Mean standardisation: value ÷ mean, then average.
  3. Range equalisation: (X − min) ÷ (max − min) for each indicator, then average (or geometric mean for HDI).
  4. Arrange units by the final score and interpret (most and least developed).

Formulas

Range equalisation index = (X − Min) ÷ (Max − Min)
Mean standardisation = X ÷ X̄
PQLI = (Life expectancy index + Infant mortality index + Literacy index) ÷ 3
HDI = (I_health × I_education × I_income)^(1/3)
GDI = HDI_female ÷ HDI_male

How to write the answer

Definition → steps in order (or the method asked, with a small worked table) → an example index (HDI/PQLI) → merits and limitations → conclusion.

Common mistakes

  • Combining indicators that point in opposite directions without converting them first.
  • Writing that PQLI was made by UNDP — it was Morris D. Morris's index; the HDI is UNDP's.
  • Using an arithmetic mean for the HDI — it is the geometric mean of the three dimension indices.
