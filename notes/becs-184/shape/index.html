Skewness & Kurtosis

Moderate Asked in 4 of 8 papers · avg 6 marks

Shape of a distribution: symmetry and skewness (Karl Pearson and Bowley's coefficients), kurtosis (Pearson's β₂ and the three types of curves) and how they describe a distribution.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 2 Statistical Concepts (2.5.5)

Key points

  • Skewness means lack of symmetry. In a symmetrical distribution mean = median = mode and the two tails are equal.
  • Positive (right) skew: a long tail to the right; mean > median > mode (e.g. incomes). Negative (left) skew: a long tail to the left; mean < median < mode (e.g. marks in an easy test). Note: the study material's sentence that the mode is highest in both cases is wrong for positive skew.
  • Karl Pearson's coefficient of skewness = (Mean − Mode) ÷ SD, or 3(Mean − Median) ÷ SD when the mode is ill-defined.
  • Bowley's coefficient = (Q₃ + Q₁ − 2 Median) ÷ (Q₃ − Q₁); it uses quartiles, so it works for open-ended distributions; it lies between −1 and +1.
  • Kurtosis measures peakedness (or flatness) compared with the normal curve. Karl Pearson's measure: β₂ = μ₄ ÷ μ₂² and γ₂ = β₂ − 3.
  • Three types: mesokurtic (normal, β₂ = 3, γ₂ = 0), leptokurtic (more peaked, β₂ > 3), platykurtic (flatter, β₂ < 3).
  • Skewness tells the direction and size of asymmetry (the relative size of the two tails); kurtosis tells how peaked or heavy-tailed it is. Together with the mean and SD they describe the shape fully.

Worked examples

Example: measuring skewness

Incomes in a town: mean ₹40,000, median ₹36,000, mode ₹28,000 (note 3 × 36,000 − 2 × 40,000 = 28,000), SD ₹16,000.

Karl Pearson's Sk = (40,000 − 28,000) ÷ 16,000 = +0.75; using the median, 3(40,000 − 36,000) ÷ 16,000 = +0.75 too. Positive: a long right tail (a few very high incomes), with mean > median > mode.

Bowley: Q1 = 24,000, median 36,000, Q3 = 56,000 → (56,000 + 24,000 − 72,000) ÷ 32,000 = +0.25.

Example: kurtosis

If β₂ = μ₄ ÷ μ₂² = 4.2, then γ₂ = 1.2 > 0 → leptokurtic (more peaked, heavier tails than normal). If β₂ = 2.1 → platykurtic (flatter). If β₂ = 3 → mesokurtic (normal).
