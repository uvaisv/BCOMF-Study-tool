E-Payments & E-Banking

Critical Asked in 9 of 9 papers · avg 22 marks

Types of electronic payment, EFT/NEFT/RTGS/IMPS/UPI, payment gateways, e-wallets, e-banking, online fund transfer, e-payment vs traditional payment.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 5 E-PaymentsBlock 2 · Unit 6 E-Banking (6.2–6.5, 6.7)

Key points

  • Electronic payment systems: cards (credit, debit, prepaid), net banking, e-wallets (Paytm), UPI, EFT systems (NEFT, RTGS, IMPS), mobile banking, e-cheques.
  • EFT: electronic transfer from one account to another within or across banks.
  • NEFT: batch-wise settlement, no minimum, 24×7. RTGS: real-time gross settlement, one by one, minimum ₹2 lakh. IMPS: instant, 24×7, via mobile/net banking. UPI: instant real-time payments using a virtual payment address (VPA), linked to bank accounts.
  • Payment gateway: bridge between customer, merchant and banks that authorises online payments. Hosted (customer redirected to the gateway page — PayPal) vs self-hosted/integrated (payment on the merchant's site — more control, more security responsibility).
  • Online fund transfer requirements: bank account with net/mobile banking, beneficiary details (name, account number, IFSC), registered mobile for OTP, internet access; tools: net banking, mobile app, UPI app.
  • E-banking: banking over the internet — 24×7, lower costs, convenience; risks: fraud, phishing.
  • E-payment vs traditional payment: instant, trackable, no cash handling vs cash/cheque, slower; limits — cyber risk and need for connectivity vs theft, forgery.
  • Credit card (bank credit, pay later) vs e-wallet (prepaid balance stored in an app).
  • Other banking technology in the book: Automated Clearing House (bulk, recurring payments such as salaries and EMIs) and ATMs, POS terminals and mobile banking.

Worked examples

Example: what happens when you click 'Pay'

On a shopping site the customer clicks Pay and is taken to the payment gateway's page (hosted gateway). They enter card details; the gateway sends them to the card network and the issuing bank, which asks for an OTP. Once the bank authorises the payment, the gateway tells the merchant, and the order is confirmed. Money reaches the merchant's account later, at settlement.

Example: picking the transfer method

Rent of ₹15,000 at midnight → IMPS or UPI (instant, 24×7). ₹10 lakh to a supplier → RTGS (₹2 lakh minimum, settled one by one in real time). Monthly salaries of 500 staff → a bulk file through NEFT or ACH. UPI works with just a VPA such as name@bank, with no account number or IFSC needed.

How to write the answer

Meaning → types as headed points → comparison table (NEFT/RTGS/IMPS/UPI) → conclusion.
