Encryption & Digital Signatures

Moderate Asked in 8 of 9 papers · avg 6 marks

Encryption, symmetric vs asymmetric, digital signature, electronic signature, digital signature certificates.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 4 · Unit 12 IT Act 2000 (12.5 Digital signature & encryption)Block 4 · Unit 11 Cyber Security Measures (11.7 Securing network transactions)

Key points

  • Encryption: converting plaintext into ciphertext so only authorised parties can read it; decryption reverses it.
  • Symmetric encryption: one shared key (fast; key-sharing problem — AES). Asymmetric: public key encrypts, private key decrypts (slower, secure key exchange — RSA).
  • Digital signature: created with the signer's private key and a hash of the document; verified with the public key — ensures authenticity, integrity and non-repudiation.
  • Electronic signature: any electronic means of signing (typed name, scanned signature, OTP-based e-Sign) — digital signature is a secure type of electronic signature.
  • Digital Signature Certificate (DSC): issued by a Certifying Authority under the IT Act; classes by level of verification; used for e-filing, MCA and GST filings, e-tenders.

Worked examples

Example: public-key encryption and a digital signature

Anu wants to send Ravi a confidential file. She encrypts it with Ravi's public key; only Ravi's private key can decrypt it.

To prove the file is from her and unchanged, Anu signs it: a hash of the file is encrypted with her private key. Ravi verifies it with Anu's public key. If even one character was altered, the hash will not match — authenticity, integrity and non-repudiation.

Example: symmetric encryption and DSCs

A password-protected ZIP file uses one shared key — fast, but the password must reach the receiver safely.

A company director uses a Digital Signature Certificate from a Certifying Authority to sign the company's annual filings on the MCA portal.

How to write the answer

Meaning → process diagram (sender signs with private key → receiver verifies with public key) → comparison table → conclusion.
