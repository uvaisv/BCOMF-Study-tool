IT Act, Cyber Crime & Cyber Forensics

Critical Asked in 9 of 9 papers · avg 19 marks

IT Act 2000 and the 2008 amendment, offences and punishments, penalties and adjudication, Appellate Tribunal, cyber crimes, cyber forensics, cyber terrorism.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 4 · Unit 12 IT Act 2000Block 4 · Unit 11 Cyber Security Measures (11.5 Cyber forensics)

Key points

  • IT Act, 2000: legal recognition of electronic records and digital signatures, e-governance, certifying authorities, offences and penalties; based on the UNCITRAL Model Law.
  • IT (Amendment) Act, 2008: technology-neutral 'electronic signature', data protection (Sec 43A — compensation for failing to protect sensitive data), new offences (Sec 66B–66F: receiving stolen computer resources, identity theft, cheating by personation, violation of privacy, cyber terrorism; Sec 66A on offensive messages was struck down by the Supreme Court in 2015), child pornography (67B), intermediary liability (79), CERT-In's role.
  • Offences: tampering with source code (Sec 65), computer-related offences/hacking (66), identity theft (66C), cheating by personation (66D), privacy violation (66E), cyber terrorism (66F — up to life imprisonment), publishing obscene material (67).
  • Penalties and adjudication: compensation for damage to computer systems (Sec 43), adjudicating officers for claims; appeals to the Appellate Tribunal (now TDSAT), then the High Court.
  • Cyber crime: crime using a computer or network — hacking, phishing, identity theft, cyber stalking, cyber bullying, online fraud, ransomware, child pornography, cyber terrorism.
  • Cyber forensics: identifying, preserving, analysing and presenting digital evidence (hard disks, logs, mobiles) for use in court; electronic evidence may not be visible to an ordinary user.

Worked examples

Examples: which section applies?

Someone logs in to a colleague's e-mail with a stolen password and sends messages in their name → identity theft (Sec 66C).

A caller pretends to be a bank officer and tricks a customer into sharing an OTP → cheating by personation using a computer resource (Sec 66D).

A sacked employee deletes the company's source code → tampering with source code (Sec 65) and a computer-related offence (Sec 66).

A company leaks customers' card data through careless security → it must compensate them (Sec 43A).

Example: cyber forensics in a fraud case

Police seize the suspect's laptop and phone. Investigators make an exact copy (image) of the disk and record its hash value, so they can prove the evidence was not changed. They analyse the copy — deleted chats, browser history, transaction logs — and present a report the court can rely on.

How to write the answer

Meaning → sections as headed points (section number + offence + punishment) → conclusion.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing up IT Act 2000 and 2008 — list what the amendment added.
