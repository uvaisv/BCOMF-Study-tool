FinTech, DLT & Digital Currencies

Moderate Asked in 4 of 9 papers · avg 6 marks

FinTech and its technologies, distributed ledger technology, digital vs virtual vs crypto currencies.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 5 · Unit 14 E-Services (14.4 FinTech)Block 2 · Unit 6 E-Banking (6.6 Virtual currency)

Key points

  • FinTech: innovative use of technology to deliver financial services (Paytm, Zerodha, PhonePe).
  • Technologies: AI and machine learning, blockchain/DLT, cloud computing, big-data analytics, APIs/open banking, biometrics, robotic process automation.
  • Distributed ledger technology: a shared, replicated record across many nodes. Advantages: transparency, security, no single point of failure, faster settlement, lower costs.
  • Digital currency: any money in electronic form (includes central bank digital currency — the e-Rupee). Virtual currency: digital value issued by private developers, used in a specific community (game tokens). Cryptocurrency: decentralised virtual currency secured by cryptography (Bitcoin).

Worked examples

Example: three kinds of 'digital money'

The RBI's e-Rupee is digital currency issued by the central bank, with the same value as cash. Gold coins earned inside a mobile game are virtual currency — usable only in that game. Bitcoin is a cryptocurrency — decentralised, with no issuer, and secured by cryptography on a blockchain.

Example: FinTech in daily life

A shopkeeper gets a small loan approved in minutes by an app that uses AI to study their UPI sales (big data and machine learning). An investor buys shares on a discount-broker app. Banks use a shared ledger (DLT) for trade documents, so each party sees the same record and fraud is harder.
