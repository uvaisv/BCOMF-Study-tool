Biodiversity & Its Conservation

Critical Asked in 11 of 11 papers · avg 8 questions

Levels and values of biodiversity, India's biogeographic zones and hotspots, threats to biodiversity, and in-situ and ex-situ conservation.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 6 Biodiversity: Values and ServicesBlock 3 · Unit 8 Biodiversity: Threats and Conservation

Key points

  • 'Biological diversity' was coined by Thomas Lovejoy (1980) and 'biodiversity' by E.O. Wilson. Levels: genetic diversity (the 'fundamental currency of diversity', e.g. varieties of mango or rice), species diversity (the most visible) and ecosystem diversity.
  • About 12.5 million species are estimated and 1.7 million described. In sheer numbers, insects and micro-organisms are the most abundant; insects are the largest described group (about 960,000 species).
  • India's 10 biogeographic zones: Trans-Himalaya (e.g. Ladakh), Himalaya, Indian Desert, Semi-arid, Western Ghats, Deccan Peninsula (the largest), Gangetic Plain, North-East, Islands and Coasts.
  • Zone facts: the Thar's wildlife is known for extraordinary adaptations (desert fox, desert cat, Houbara bustard); the Western Ghats hold about two-thirds of India's endemic plants (lion-tailed macaque, Nilgiri tahr, Malabar grey hornbill, spiny dormouse); the rhinoceros is found only in the Brahmaputra valley.
  • Hotspots: areas extremely rich in species, with high endemism and under constant threat (Myers, 1988). There are 34 worldwide; 4 are in India and its neighbours. Tropical rain forests and coral reefs hold the most species.
  • India is mega-diverse: 4 hotspots, 26 endemism centres, 2 realms, 3 biomes, 10 biogeographic zones, about 33% of recorded flora endemic, and one of 12 centres of origin of cultivated plants.
  • Values: direct use (food, timber); indirect use (non-consumptive, aesthetic, cultural and religious, ethical); non-use (option, bequest, existence).
  • Threats: habitat loss (the greatest), invasive species, over-hunting, pollution and climate change. Fragile habitats: coral reefs, oceanic islands, mountain tops and habitat islands.
  • Examples: the passenger pigeon was wiped out by selective destruction for food; rinderpest from cattle held back the Great Indian Rhinoceros; DDT and other chlorinated pesticides cut bird numbers such as the bald eagle; commercial hunting targets tiger, snow leopard and cheetah skins, elephant ivory, rhino horn and whales.
  • Invasive species: water hyacinth (harms fish and rice), Parthenium or Congress weed (skin allergies), Prosopis juliflora or mesquite (semi-arid areas), golden apple snail. Climate change is likely to worsen the problem.
  • In-situ conservation: national parks, sanctuaries, biosphere reserves, wetlands, tiger and elephant reserves. Ex-situ: zoos, botanical gardens, seed banks ('genetic insurance'), tissue culture, captive breeding, artificial insemination (elephants, cheetahs, black-footed ferret) and fostering (whooping crane, peregrine falcon).
  • Biosphere reserves come from UNESCO's MAB (Man and the Biosphere) programme; each has a core, a buffer (regulated use such as grazing and farming, no hunting) and a transition zone. India's first was Nilgiri. Mangroves act as a natural bulwark against sea erosion; Chilika and Bharatpur were India's first Ramsar wetlands.

Worked examples

Example: the three levels of biodiversity

Genetic: India has grown thousands of rice varieties, each with different traits such as drought or flood tolerance.

Species: the Western Ghats hold endemic species like the lion-tailed macaque and the Nilgiri tahr.

Ecosystem: the Sundarbans mangroves, the Thar desert and the Himalayan forests are very different ecosystems within one country.

Example: in-situ vs ex-situ, and an invasive species

In-situ: the one-horned rhinoceros is protected in its own habitat in Kaziranga National Park.

Ex-situ: seeds of crop varieties are stored in seed banks as 'genetic insurance', and endangered animals are bred in zoos for release.

Invasive: water hyacinth spreads over lakes and backwaters, blocking sunlight and boats and harming fish.
