Natural Resources: Water, Land & Energy

Critical Asked in 11 of 11 papers · avg 7 questions

Water and its cycle, soils and land conservation, forests and deforestation, and conventional and non-conventional energy.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 4 Land and Water ResourcesBlock 2 · Unit 5 Forest ResourcesBlock 2 · Unit 7 Energy Resources

Key points

  • Oceans hold 97% of the earth's water; less than 3% is fresh. Of fresh water about 80% is frozen and about 20% liquid (mostly groundwater). Only about 1% of all water is readily usable; agriculture takes about 70% of what is used.
  • Water cycle terms: runoff is the main source of water for lakes and rivers; sublimation is ice turning straight into vapour; transpiration is water vapour lost from plants. An aquifer is a layer of rock that water percolates into and is held in.
  • Traditional water systems: johads, kuls (channels carrying glacier water to villages in Spiti, Himachal Pradesh) and bamboo drip irrigation (North-East). Sprinkler irrigation is modern. Too much irrigation causes waterlogging, salinity or alkalinity and lower yields.
  • Floods are the most common calamity; Bangladesh and India together account for over two-thirds of flood deaths. Economic impacts of drought: loss of crops, dairy, livestock and fish.
  • Soils as the IGNOU book describes them: red — potatoes, bananas, pineapples, rubber; black (Deccan and Malwa plateaux) — cotton, sugarcane, groundnut, soyabean; desert — low organic matter and fertility; Himalayan — low fertility; alluvial — Indo-Gangetic plains.
  • Soil care: gypsum treats alkaline soil; manure improves soil cohesion, water retention and structure (not salinity). Erosion is controlled by plant cover, drainage, check dams, stone walls along coasts and tree barriers; ravines (e.g. Chambal) come from gully erosion. Finer particles in sewage settle with alum and caustic soda.
  • Forest functions: economic (timber, food, medicines, products used as insecticides and pesticides), ecological (moderating climate as a carbon sink, protecting biodiversity, checking erosion, landslides, cyclones and floods, cleaning air, protecting watersheds) and socio-cultural. India needs 33% of its area under forest.
  • Four major causes of deforestation: population explosion, forest fires, grazing and pest attacks. Crown fires occur in dense forests. NTFPs are items like medicinal herbs, edible flowers, leaves and fruits (timber and firewood are separate).
  • Joint Forest Management began informally in 1972 in Midnapore, West Bengal. The Forest Rights Act, 2006 gives rights to tribal and other forest dwellers.
  • Energy sources — conventional: coal, oil, natural gas, hydropower, atomic energy; non-conventional: solar, wind, biomass, geothermal, fuel cells (best fuel: hydrogen), co-generation, wave and tidal.
  • India's energy: coal meets over half of energy needs and 63% of commercial energy; natural gas about 7%; biomass is the most used in rural India. China is the largest coal producer; Bombay High produces the most oil; the first wave energy plant was at Vizhinjam, Kerala; geothermal potential is in the North-West Himalaya.
  • Sludge gas, like biogas, is mostly methane; a biomass gasifier turns solid fuel into gas; photovoltaic cells turn sunlight into electricity. With development, growth in per-capita energy demand slows in the industrial sector but keeps rising in transport, residential and commercial sectors.

Worked examples

Example: how little water we can use

Imagine all the earth's water as 100 litres. About 97 litres are sea water; under 3 litres are fresh. Of that fresh water, about 80% is locked in ice, and most of the rest is underground. Less than a litre is easy to use — which is why saving and recharging water matter.

Example: a village biogas plant

Cow dung and kitchen waste are fed into a digester; bacteria break them down without air to produce biogas, which is mostly methane, for cooking. The leftover slurry is a good manure. The village cuts firewood use (saving forests) and smoke in kitchens — a non-conventional, renewable source of energy.

Common mistakes

  • IGNOU's book lists rubber under red soil — laterite soil is not described in it.
