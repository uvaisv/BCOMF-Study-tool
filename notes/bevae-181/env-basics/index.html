Environment, Population & Sustainable Development

High Asked in 11 of 11 papers · avg 3 questions

What the environment is, its three types, how thinkers see the human–environment link, sustainable development and the SDGs, and human population growth.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 1 Our EnvironmentBlock 4 · Unit 13 Human Communities and Environment (13.2 Human population growth)Block 2 · Unit 7 Energy Resources (7.3 Carrying capacity)

Key points

  • Environment: the sum total of living (biotic) and non-living (abiotic) components, influences and events around an organism.
  • Three types in the IGNOU book — natural: oceans, rivers, lakes, forests, grasslands, deserts; human-modified: orchards, plantations, sanctuaries, parks; human-made: cities, towns, industries, crop fields, dams, artificial lakes.
  • Determinism (nature controls humans): developed by Friedrich Ratzel and expanded by Ellsworth Huntington. Possibilism (humans choose among what nature allows): the term was coined by Lucien Febvre.
  • Sustainable development: meeting today's needs without harming the ability of future generations to meet theirs — defined by the Brundtland Commission (WCED, chaired by Gro Harlem Brundtland) in 'Our Common Future'. Herman Daly called the term an 'oxymoron'.
  • Transitions a sustainable society needs include a demographic transition (growing → stable population), a resource transition and a technological transition (pollution-intensive → environment-friendly production).
  • Priority areas for sustainable development: slow population growth; reduce poverty, inequality and Third World debt; make agriculture sustainable; protect forests and habitats; use water and energy sustainably; reduce waste. 'Zero pollution' is not one of them.
  • SDGs: 17 goals for 2016–2030 — e.g. no poverty, zero hunger, good health and well-being, quality education, gender equality, clean water and sanitation, affordable and clean energy, climate action, life below water, life on land. Past-paper distractors that are not SDGs: tourism, clean air, life on air, quality health care, social equity.
  • Population history: about 5 million people at the dawn of agriculture (about 8000 BC); growth up to about 1800 AD came with agriculture; in the 14th century the bubonic plague killed more than half the population of Europe and Asia.
  • Projected world population: 8.6 billion (mid-2030s), 9.8 billion (mid-2050s) and 11.2 billion by 2100.
  • Carrying capacity: biophysical = the maximum population the planet's resources can support at a given level of technology; social = the sustainable number within a society's patterns of consumption and trade, always lower. It varies with resource availability, culture and level of economic development.

Worked examples

Example: three types of environment in one district

Natural: a forest and a river. Human-modified: a rubber plantation and a wildlife sanctuary. Human-made: the town, a dam and the industrial estate.

Example: sustainable development in practice

A fishing community agrees not to fish during the breeding season and to use nets with larger holes so young fish escape. Catches may be smaller now, but fish stocks remain for their children — meeting present needs without harming future generations (the Brundtland idea). Clean water, clean energy and life below water are among the SDGs this supports.

Common mistakes

  • Crop fields and dams are human-made in the IGNOU book, not human-modified; orchards, plantations and sanctuaries are human-modified.
