Pollution & Waste Management

Critical Asked in 11 of 11 papers · avg 7 questions

Types of pollutants, air, water, soil and noise pollution with their standards and tests, and how hazardous and solid waste is minimised, treated and disposed of.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 3 · Unit 9 Environmental Pollution and HazardsBlock 3 · Unit 10 Waste Management

Key points

  • Primary pollutants are emitted directly (sulphur dioxide, nitrogen oxides, carbon monoxide, carbon dioxide, hydrocarbons, particulates); secondary pollutants form in the air (ozone, PAN, nitric acid, sulphuric acid, sulphur trioxide, smog). Smog is not a natural pollutant; pollen and volcanic gases are.
  • Point sources discharge at one place (chimneys, pipes); non-point sources are spread out (runoff from farms). Biodegradable pollutants (paper) break down; persistent ones (pesticides, heavy metals, plastics, rubber) do not.
  • Bioaccumulation is a pollutant entering and building up in an organism; biomagnification is its concentration rising from one link of the food chain to the next.
  • Air: particles smaller than 10 µm (PM10) are respirable suspended particulate matter (RSPM), with a standard of 60 µg per cubic metre; PM2.5 goes deep into the lungs. Radon (from building materials) and formaldehyde are indoor pollutants.
  • Water pollutants are biological (pathogens), chemical (inorganic, organic and radioactive substances) or physical (suspended and settled solids, heat). Phosphates and nitrates cause eutrophication; sewage uses up oxygen in water.
  • Water tests: MPN detects coliforms such as E. coli; BOD is the oxygen bacteria use to break down organic matter (high BOD means low dissolved oxygen); TDS is total dissolved solids.
  • Noise is measured in decibels; dB(A) accounts for both pressure and pitch. The prescribed night-time limit for residential areas is 45 dB(A). The Bhopal gas tragedy released methyl isocyanate.
  • Hazardous waste strategy: minimise → recycle → treat → dispose. Minimisation methods: process modification, waste concentration, waste segregation (recycling is a separate step).
  • Treatment — physical: phase separation (lagooning, tank storage, sludge drying beds); chemical: oxidation, reduction, neutralisation, heavy-metal precipitation, oil–water separation; sludge processing (biological): conditioning, digestion, composting, thickening or dewatering, solidification.
  • Disposal — landfill (sanitary landfills are lined with clay or plastic); incineration, used where land is short and excellent for minimising and detoxifying waste but very costly if the heat is not reused (hospital waste is incinerated); dumping at sea; underground disposal, the option for radioactive nuclear waste.

Worked examples

Example: biomagnification of DDT

In a classic study, DDT was about 0.000003 ppm in the water, 0.04 ppm in zooplankton, 0.5 ppm in small fish, 2 ppm in large fish and about 25 ppm in fish-eating birds. The concentration rises at each step of the food chain, so top predators suffer most — birds laid thin-shelled eggs that broke.

Examples: primary vs secondary pollutants, and BOD

Car exhaust releases nitrogen oxides and hydrocarbons (primary). In sunlight they react to form ozone and PAN (secondary) — the brown photochemical smog seen over busy cities.

When untreated sewage enters a river, bacteria use up oxygen to break it down. BOD rises, dissolved oxygen falls, and fish die.

Common mistakes

  • Lagooning is a physical treatment (phase separation); conditioning is part of sludge processing — past papers test this both ways.
