Forest, Grassland, Desert & Aquatic Ecosystems

High Asked in 11 of 11 papers · avg 4 questions

The main forest types and their plants and animals, grasslands, deserts and their adaptations, and freshwater, marine and estuarine ecosystems.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 3 Major Ecosystems

Key points

  • Coniferous (boreal, taiga) forest: spruce, fir and pine; cold, long winters; soil acidic and poor in minerals; the lowest productivity of any ecosystem.
  • Temperate deciduous forest: broad-leaved trees that shed leaves in winter; trees 40–50 m tall with thin, broad leaves — maple, beech, oak, hickory; rainfall 75–150 cm.
  • Temperate evergreen forest (Mediterranean-type climate): low evergreen trees such as hemlock, yew and maple; shrubs up to 3–4 m. Temperate rain forest: the coldest rainforest, where fog can matter more than rain.
  • Tropical rain forest: near the equator, over 200 cm rain a year, the most diverse forest — under 7% of the land but over 50% of known species (about 62% of known plants). Animals include chameleons, agamids, geckos, ant-eaters, giant flying squirrels and sloths.
  • Tropical seasonal (monsoon) forest: teak and bamboo, as in central India. Subtropical rain forest: epiphytes, vines and strangler fig.
  • Grasslands get about 25–75 cm of rain; in Indian grasslands biomass peaks around September–October. Deserts get under 25 cm.
  • Desert adaptations: some nocturnal rodents pass very concentrated urine and never need to drink. In the Thar, the lizards Calotes and Uromastyx prey on the desert locust, and the main predatory birds are vultures (e.g. white-rumped vulture, Gyps bengalensis).
  • Freshwater ecosystems are lentic (standing — lakes, ponds, impoundments, wetlands) or lotic (flowing — rivers, streams). Rivers are open (heterotrophic) systems; lakes are closed.
  • Aquatic organisms: plankton drift; neuston live unattached at the air–water surface; periphyton are attached to stems and leaves of rooted plants; nekton swim; benthos live on the bottom.
  • Ocean zones: littoral (shore — the most variable in temperature, light, salinity and moisture), neritic (shallow coastal — rich in species and highly productive), pelagic (open sea, 90% of the ocean surface), benthic (floor). Sea water averages about 3.5% salt.
  • Estuary: the transition zone between river and sea, with unique ecological features and biotic communities. Estuaries are brackish, the most productive ecosystems and nurseries for fish (e.g. Kerala coast, Sundarbans). Brackish ecosystems include estuaries, salt marshes and mangroves.

Worked examples

Examples: aquatic ecosystems

Lentic (standing water): a lake or pond. Lotic (flowing water): a river or stream.

Estuary: where a river meets the sea, as in the Sundarbans — brackish, very productive, and a nursery for fish and prawns.

Nekton (fish that swim), plankton (drifting algae and tiny animals) and benthos (crabs and worms on the bottom) live at different levels of the same water body.

Example: living in a desert

Desert animals save water: many rodents stay in burrows by day and come out at night, and some produce very concentrated urine and hardly ever drink. Desert plants have thick, waxy stems and spines instead of leaves to reduce water loss.

Common mistakes

  • Hemlock, yew and maple are listed under temperate evergreen forest; maple, beech and oak under temperate deciduous.
