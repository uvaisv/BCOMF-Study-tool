Environmental Laws, Institutions & Constitution

Moderate Asked in 11 of 11 papers · avg 3 questions

The Constitution's environment clauses, Indian environmental Acts with their years, the NGT and NBA, international conventions and ENVIS.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 4 · Unit 12 Environmental LegislationBlock 4 · Unit 14 Environmental Ethics (14.9 ENVIS)

Key points

  • The 42nd Amendment (1976), made after the 1972 Stockholm conference, added Article 48A (the State shall protect and improve the environment and safeguard forests and wildlife) and Article 51A(g) (a fundamental duty of every citizen).
  • The Department of Environment began in 1980, became the Ministry of Environment and Forests in 1985, and was renamed the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change in 2014.
  • Acts: Indian Forest Act 1927; Wild Life (Protection) Act 1972 (Schedules I–V); Water Act 1974 (amended 1988); Water Cess Act 1977; Forest (Conservation) Act 1980; Air Act 1981; Environment (Protection) Act 1986 — the 'umbrella' law passed after Bhopal.
  • The 1987 amendment of the Air Act strengthened enforcement, introduced stiffer penalties and citizen suits, and brought in noise pollution.
  • The Biodiversity Bill (introduced 2000, passed 2002) set up the National Biodiversity Authority (NBA), which has the powers of a civil court and deals with access by foreigners.
  • The National Green Tribunal (NGT Act 2010, set up October 2010) exists for the effective and speedy disposal of environmental cases, aiming to decide them within 6 months. Principal bench: New Delhi; zonal benches: Bhopal, Pune, Kolkata, Chennai.
  • Species projects: Hangul (1970), Lion (1972), Tiger (1973), Crocodile (1974), Brown-antlered deer (1981), Elephant (1991–92). There is no camel project.
  • International milestones: Stockholm 1972 (gave rise to UNEP); CITES 1973; Vienna 1985 (ozone); Montreal Protocol 1987; Basel 1989 (movement of hazardous waste across borders); Rio Earth Summit 1992 (Agenda 21, UNFCCC, CBD); Kyoto 1997; Rotterdam 1998 (hazardous chemicals); Stockholm 2001 (persistent organic pollutants); Johannesburg 2002 (Rio+10); Ramsar 1971 (wetlands).
  • ENVIS is the environmental information system of the Environment Ministry, with its main centre in Delhi. Centres include CPCB (pollution control), ITRC Lucknow (toxic chemicals), TERI (renewable energy and environment), NIOH Ahmedabad (occupational health) and CTS, IISc Bengaluru (Western Ghats ecology). TIFR and AIIMS are not ENVIS centres.
  • Laws are hard to enforce because of a lack of expertise, shortage of funds and a lack of seriousness among implementing authorities.

Worked example

Examples: which law or body applies?

A factory releases untreated effluent into a river → Water Act 1974, enforced by the pollution control boards.

Someone kills a tiger → Wild Life (Protection) Act 1972 (the tiger is in Schedule I, the highest protection).

A company wants to clear forest land for a mine → Forest (Conservation) Act 1980: it needs the Central Government's approval.

Residents want speedy relief against a polluting unit → they can approach the National Green Tribunal.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing up Act years — make a one-line list and revise it on exam day.
