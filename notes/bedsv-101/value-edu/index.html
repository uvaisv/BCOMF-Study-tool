Value Education: Meaning, Need & Methods

Critical Asked in 4 of 4 papers · avg 14 marks

Education and value education, need and importance, moral vs religious education, ways to develop values, malaise of society.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 1 Social Malaise and the Need for Value EducationBlock 1 · Unit 2 Concept of Morality and Moral Education (Moral vs religious education)

Key points

  • Value education develops moral, social, cultural and spiritual values along with knowledge — education that is value-oriented, not just informative.
  • Malaise of Indian society in the book: the race for wealth, power and status at the cost of humanness; poverty, unemployment, broken families, violence against women, children and the aged, child neglect and malnutrition, alcoholism and drugs, corruption and crime.
  • There is a gap between the lofty ideals we preach and what we practise — a value crisis and a 'spiritual vacuum' to be filled with love, care, compassion and justice.
  • The book blames partly the education system and teachers: teaching has become just a livelihood. The National Policy on Education (1986) asked for value-oriented education.
  • The Delors Commission ('Education for the Twenty-first Century') made 'learning to live together' one of the pillars of education.
  • Teachers should set an example — simplicity, economy in using school resources, politeness and care for every child.
  • Ways to develop values in school: build them into every subject, stories and discussions, activities and case studies, and above all the teacher's own example.
  • Moral vs religious education: religious education is authoritarian (belief taught on authority); moral education is based on reason and helps the child think for themselves.

Worked example

Example: building values into an ordinary lesson

A commerce teacher teaching profit calculation adds a short case: a shopkeeper who could earn more by using false weights but chooses not to. Students discuss what they would do and why. Honesty is taught inside the subject, through discussion rather than a lecture — and the teacher's own fairness in marking is the strongest lesson of all.

How to write the answer

Meaning → need or methods as headed points → conclusion.
