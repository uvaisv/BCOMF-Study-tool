Interaction, Adjustment & Conflict

Critical Asked in 3 of 4 papers · avg 17 marks

Verbal and non-verbal interaction, adjustment and its push/pull factors, adjustment disorder, conflict.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 4 · Unit 2 Social Interaction and Human ValuesBlock 4 · Unit 3 Values: Conflict and ResolutionBlock 4 · Unit 4 Process of Human AdjustmentBlock 4 · Unit 1 Process of Socialization

Key points

  • Socialisation is how a person learns society's values and ways; its agents are family, peers, school and mass media. It can be value-loaded or value-less, planned or unplanned, positive or negative.
  • Interaction is of two kinds: verbal (through language, spoken or written) and non-verbal (gestures, expressions, posture, eye contact, tone) — non-verbal cues can support or contradict the words.
  • Conflict (disagreement or clash) leads to stress. Strategies: win–lose (one side forced to give in), lose–lose (compromise) and win–win (both satisfied — the creative, best outcome).
  • Adjustment is a continuous process (Ruth Strang) of changing to live in harmony with one's environment. Signs of good adjustment: responsibility, sense of security, integration, ego development, curiosity and spontaneity.
  • In the book, pull factors pull us away from adjusting — e.g. aggression and anger. Push factors are the good values that help us adjust — empathy, sympathy, humour, helping, guilt and obedience.
  • Adjustment disorder: an unhealthy emotional or behavioural reaction to a stressful change (moving house, parents' divorce, a loss), starting within three months and usually lasting under six months — anxiety, depression, conduct problems or isolation.
  • Helping maladjusted children: daily small talk, chances to play with peers, a kind 'buddy', discussing (not only punishing) misbehaviour, and reporting home problems where needed.

Worked examples

Example: conflict resolved three ways

Two roommates clash over study time and music.

Win–lose: one forces the other to stay silent. Lose–lose: they agree no one plays music at all, and both are unhappy. Win–win: one uses headphones and the other studies in the library in the evening — both needs are met.

Example: helping a maladjusted child

After his parents' separation, a 10-year-old becomes withdrawn and aggressive in class (an adjustment disorder within three months of the change). The teacher talks with him daily, pairs him with a kind 'buddy', discusses rather than punishes his outbursts, and informs the counsellor and parents.

How to write the answer

Meaning → causes or factors as headed points → remedies → conclusion.
