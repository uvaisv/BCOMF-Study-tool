Planning

High Asked in 7 of 11 papers · avg 12 marks

Meaning, nature, importance, principles, process; plans vs policies; long vs short range.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 3 · Unit 9 Planning and Decision Making (9.2–9.9)

Key points

  • Planning: setting future objectives and deciding in advance how to achieve them. It comes before all other management functions (primacy) and runs through every level (pervasive).
  • Nature: future-oriented yet based on past and present information, purposeful, intellectual yet action-oriented, involves decision-making, rests on assumptions and is continuous and dynamic.
  • Importance: gives direction, lets managers compare alternatives, cuts impulsive and ad hoc decisions, is the base of other functions, allocates scarce resources, helps adapt to change and spot threats and opportunities early.
  • Limitations: forecasts may be wrong, information may be unreliable or late, the environment changes beyond control, planning delays action and can make managers rigid.
  • Process (book): planning to plan → appraise internal conditions → appraise external environment → define key areas and issues → develop and choose alternative plans → form medium- and short-range plans → arrange implementation.
  • Types: by comprehensiveness — strategic (organisation-wide moves against competition) vs tactical (specific functional sub-plans); by time — long-range vs short-range (one year or less, more detailed).
  • Plans are single-use (for unique, non-repetitive situations, e.g. programmes, budgets) or standing (for repeated situations, e.g. policies, procedures, rules, methods).
  • Plan vs policy: a plan is a course of action to reach a goal; a policy is a standing plan — a general guide to thinking and decisions (e.g. 'promote from within').
  • Principles: top management interest, long-range view, contribution to objectives, primacy, flexibility, navigational change, commitment and the limiting factor.

Worked example

Example: a bakery plans for Diwali

Objective: sell 2,000 sweet boxes in the festival week. Looking at last year's sales and this year's orders, the owner compares alternatives (hire temporary staff or run a second shift), chooses the second shift, and makes a short-range plan: buy ingredients by a set date, fix the shift roster, book delivery vans.

Standing plan: a policy of 'no credit sales above ₹5,000'. Single-use plan: the budget for this one festival week.

How to write the answer

Definition (quote Koontz: 'deciding in advance what to do, how to do it, when to do it and who is to do it') → headed points → a flow diagram of the process → conclusion.

Common mistakes

  • Listing process steps without one line of explanation each.
