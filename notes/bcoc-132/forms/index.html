Choosing a Form of Organisation

Low Asked in 3 of 11 papers · avg 5 marks

Overview of forms and the features of an ideal form of business organisation.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 6 Forms of Business Organisation-II

Key points

  • Features of an ideal form: easy formation, limited liability, enough capital, secrecy, flexibility, stability, little government control, low tax burden, efficient management and owner interest.
  • No form has all the ideal features. Sole proprietorship and partnership score on easy formation, less control, owner interest, secrecy and flexibility; company and cooperative score on limited liability, capital, managerial efficiency and stability.
  • Factors in choosing a form: nature of business, volume of business, area of operation, capital needed, control desired, expected life of the business and level of regulation accepted.
  • Book's conclusion: sole proprietorship for small business, partnership for somewhat larger business, private company for medium business, public company for large business, cooperative to protect a particular group's interest.
  • At expansion the owner can keep the existing form or move to a new one.

Worked example

Example: matching the form to the business

A tea stall → sole proprietorship (small, easy to start). Three friends starting a CA firm → partnership (skills pooled, moderate capital). A family firm making auto parts for car makers → private company (medium size, limited liability). A steel plant needing ₹500 crore from thousands of investors → public company. Weavers who want to escape middlemen → cooperative society.

How to write the answer

List forms → table comparing them on the features above → conclude with a reasoned choice.
