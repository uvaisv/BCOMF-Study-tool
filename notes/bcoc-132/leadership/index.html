Leadership & Morale

Moderate Asked in 6 of 11 papers · avg 10 marks

Meaning, styles, qualities of a leader, manager as leader, and morale.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 4 · Unit 16 Leadership

Key points

  • Leadership: influencing, guiding and directing people to secure their willing cooperation towards goals. A manager who also leads gets both task results and human satisfaction.
  • Theories: trait (success depends on personality traits), behavioural (leaders known by how they behave with followers) and situational (depends on leader–follower relations, task structure and position power).
  • Styles: autocratic (power centralised — low morale over time), democratic (decisions with group participation — better morale and growth) and laissez-faire (group left to decide — risk of chaos).
  • Functions: build teamwork, represent the group and link it to top management, advise and counsel, manage work schedules, use power properly.
  • Managerial grid: styles judged by concern for people and concern for work. More effective: executive, developer, benevolent autocrat, bureaucrat. Less effective: compromiser, missionary, autocrat, deserter.
  • Qualities of an effective leader: physical and mental health, empathy, self-confidence, self-awareness, objectivity, knowledge, decisiveness, ability to communicate.

Worked example

Example: three styles, one decision

A factory must change its shift timings.

Autocratic: the manager announces the new timings and expects compliance — quick, but workers resent it.

Democratic: the manager discusses options with worker representatives and they agree on a timing — slower, but morale and acceptance are higher.

Laissez-faire: the manager tells workers to sort it out themselves — confusion if the group cannot agree.

How to write the answer

Definition → styles with one example each (table: style / features / suitable when) → conclusion.
