Organisation Structure

Critical Asked in 9 of 11 papers · avg 16 marks

Types of structure (line, functional, line & staff), formal vs informal, departmentation, span of control, charts and manuals.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 3 · Unit 10 OrganisingBlock 3 · Unit 11 Departmentation and Forms of Authority Relationships

Key points

  • Organising: identifying and grouping activities and fixing authority–responsibility relationships so people can work together towards goals; its result is the organisation.
  • An organisation is a system: inputs (people, materials, information) → processor (throughput) → outputs (goods and services).
  • Steps: set objectives → identify and group activities → allot duties → establish relationships.
  • Structures by arrangement of activities: functional, divisional and adaptive (project and matrix organisation).
  • Principles include unity of objectives, specialisation, chain of command, unity of command, span of supervision, exception, balance, flexibility and continuity.
  • Span of control: how many people a manager can supervise well; depends on the nature of work, abilities of the manager and subordinates, and staff help.
  • Organisation chart: diagram of positions and lines of authority. Organisation manual: written record of policies, procedures and job descriptions.
  • Formal organisation is the officially planned structure; informal organisation grows from people's social and psychological needs.
  • Departmentation: grouping activities by function, product, territory, customer, process or project; it brings specialisation, control, clear responsibility and manager development.
  • Forms of authority: line (scalar/military — direct chain from top to bottom), line and staff (line commands, staff advise; the two may clash) and functional (the book advises limiting it to the top levels).

Worked example

Example: a growing garment firm organises itself

Activities are grouped into departments — purchase, production, sales, accounts (functional departmentation). Each department head reports to the general manager (chain of command) and each worker reports to one supervisor (unity of command).

When the firm starts selling in three states it adds regional sales managers for North, South and West — territorial departmentation within the sales department.

A line-and-staff touch: a legal adviser advises the general manager but gives no orders to the workers.
