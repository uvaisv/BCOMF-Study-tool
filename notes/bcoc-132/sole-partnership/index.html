Sole Proprietorship & Partnership

Moderate Asked in 6 of 11 papers · avg 8 marks

Sole trader features, partnership deed, kinds of partners, sole vs partnership.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 5 Forms of Business Organisation-I (5.2–5.3)

Key points

  • Sole trader: owned, financed and controlled by one person; no legal difference between owner and business; best for small business.
  • Sole trader — merits: full control, secrecy, easy and cheap to form and close, little regulation. Demerits: unlimited liability, limited capital and management skill, unstable life, hard to attract good staff.
  • Partnership (Indian Partnership Act, 1932): 'the relation between persons who have agreed to share the profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all.' Minimum 2 partners; the book gives a maximum of 50 under the Companies Act, 2013.
  • Features: two or more persons, agreement (oral, written or implied), profit sharing, a lawful business, mutual agency (each partner is an agent of the firm and the others), unlimited liability, good faith, no transfer of share without everyone's consent.
  • Kinds of partners — by participation: active (working) and sleeping (dormant); by profit sharing: nominal (lends only the name) and partner in profits (shares profits, not losses — e.g. a minor); by conduct: partner by estoppel and by holding out; by liability: limited and general.
  • Partnership — merits: more capital and skills, more stable than a sole trader, incentive to key staff. Demerits: unlimited liability, hard to transfer shares, conflicts among partners, short life.
  • Partnership deed: the written agreement, stamped and registered. It covers the firm's name and business, partners' names, place of business, capital of each partner, profit-sharing ratio, loans, drawings and interest, interest on capital, duties and pay of partners, accounts and audit, goodwill and revaluation on admission, retirement or death, and settlement on dissolution.

Worked example

Example: sole trader to partnership

Ravi runs a mobile repair shop alone — full control and secrecy, but if the shop owes ₹2 lakh and its assets are only ₹50,000, Ravi's personal savings can be taken (unlimited liability).

To open a second shop he takes Meena as a partner who brings ₹3 lakh, and Arun as a sleeping partner who only invests. Their deed fixes capital, profit-sharing ratio, interest on capital and what happens if one retires. Each partner can bind the firm (mutual agency).
