CSR & Business Ethics

High Asked in 8 of 11 papers · avg 11 marks

Corporate social responsibility, its paradigm shift and theories, and components of business ethics.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 3 Social Responsibility and Ethics

Key points

  • Social responsibility of business (CSR) is the role business plays in society — acting for the good of society, not only for profit.
  • The book's three CSR theories: CSR theory (voluntary contributions to community welfare), stakeholder theory (stakeholders' interests must shape decisions) and the triple bottom line (profit, people, planet).
  • Business ethics is applied ethics — moral principles in business decisions. It is wrong to treat ethics as only a personal trait and CSR as only charity; both come from moral philosophy.
  • Corporate responsibility = CSR + corporate governance + environmental accountability.
  • Paradigm shift: NGOs, media reach, scandals (Enron, Satyam), socially responsible investors and aware consumers pushed business to go beyond philanthropy. First shift — business is a social institution, not just property; second shift — responsibility towards society.
  • India's framework: the CSR Voluntary Guidelines (2009) with six components — care for all stakeholders, ethical working, workers' rights and welfare, human rights, environment, social and inclusive development; then the National Voluntary Guidelines (2011).
  • Mandatory rule: Section 135 and Schedule VII of the Companies Act, 2013 (from 1 April 2014) — a company with net worth ₹500 crore, turnover ₹1,000 crore or net profit ₹5 crore must spend at least 2% of its average net profit of the last three years on CSR.
  • Not counted as CSR: activities in the normal course of business, contributions to political parties, and activities outside India.

Worked example

Example: does the CSR rule apply, and how much?

A company's net profit for the last three years is ₹9 crore, ₹12 crore and ₹15 crore. Net profit above ₹5 crore brings it under Section 135.

Average net profit = (9 + 12 + 15) ÷ 3 = ₹12 crore; minimum CSR spend = 2% × 12 = ₹0.24 crore (₹24 lakh).

Funding a village school's library counts; a donation to a political party, or spending on its own employees' routine welfare as part of normal business, does not.
