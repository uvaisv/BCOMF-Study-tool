Motivation & Incentives

Moderate Asked in 6 of 11 papers · avg 10 marks

Meaning, process, Maslow and Herzberg theories, financial vs non-financial incentives.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 4 · Unit 15 Motivation

Key points

  • Motivation: the forces that make a person willing to use their full ability to achieve objectives. Motives (needs) start the process.
  • Nature of motives: invisible, one motive can cause many behaviours and one behaviour many motives, they can conflict and change over time.
  • Elements of the process: behaviour, motives, goals and feedback.
  • McGregor: Theory X assumes people dislike work and must be pushed; Theory Y assumes people like work and can direct and control themselves.
  • Maslow: needs arise in order — physiological → security → social → esteem → self-fulfilment.
  • Herzberg: hygiene (maintenance) factors like pay, security and working conditions only prevent dissatisfaction; motivators like recognition and achievement motivate. Job enrichment (vertical loading — more responsibility and challenge) differs from job enlargement (horizontal loading — more tasks of the same kind).
  • Types: positive (rewards) vs negative (fear, demotion, lay-off); extrinsic (off the job, financial) vs intrinsic (on the job, self-actualisation); financial (wages, bonus, benefits) vs non-financial (praise, competition, participation, growth).

Worked example

Example: Maslow and Herzberg at a call centre

Pay on time and a safe office meet physiological and security needs — they are hygiene factors: without them staff are dissatisfied, but more of them does not motivate much.

A 'best agent of the month' award (esteem) and a chance to lead a small team (self-fulfilment, job enrichment) are motivators.

Job enlargement would be simply giving an agent more calls of the same kind; job enrichment gives them the authority to settle complaints themselves.

How to write the answer
