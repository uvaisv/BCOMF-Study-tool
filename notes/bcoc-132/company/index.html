Joint Stock Company & Public Enterprises

High Asked in 7 of 11 papers · avg 10 marks

Company features and types, private vs public, OPC, government companies, Maharatna/Navratna/Miniratna.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 5 Forms of Business Organisation-I (5.4)Block 2 · Unit 7 Public Enterprises

Key points

  • Company: an artificial person created by law, with a separate legal entity, common seal and perpetual succession; ownership (shareholders) is separate from management (directors).
  • Members: public company minimum 7, no maximum; private company minimum 2, maximum 200. The Companies Act, 2013 also allows a One Person Company and a Small Company.
  • Other features: limited liability, free transfer of shares (restricted in a private company), rigid objects (the object clause of the Memorandum) and heavy statutory regulation.
  • Classification — by incorporation: statutory, registered, chartered; by liability: unlimited, limited by guarantee, limited by shares; by ownership: private, public, government company; by jurisdiction: national, multinational.
  • Company — merits: limited liability, transferable shares, stability, easy to raise capital, expert management. Demerits: costly and hard to form, heavy regulation, little secrecy, slow decisions.
  • Public enterprise: a business owned and managed by central, state or local government whose output is sold, run for social welfare and accountable to the legislature.
  • Three forms of public enterprise: departmental undertaking (run like a government department, financed by the budget, staff are civil servants), public (statutory) corporation (created by a special Act, financially autonomous, staff are not civil servants) and government company.
  • Government company: at least 51% of paid-up capital held by the central and/or state governments; registered under the Companies Act. When private investors also hold shares it is a mixed-ownership company.
  • Ratna schemes give the boards of profit-making central public enterprises more powers: Maharatna (2010, for mega enterprises to become global giants — e.g. Coal India, BHEL, IOC, NTPC, ONGC, SAIL), Navratna (1997, e.g. BEL, HAL) and Miniratna (1997, Category-I and II). The lists change over time.

Worked examples

Example: private or public company?

Five family members form 'Kumar Foods Private Limited' — allowed, since a private company needs at least 2 members (up to 200) and restricts transfer of shares.

To raise money from the public for a big factory it later becomes a public company: it needs at least 7 members and its shares can be freely transferred on a stock exchange.

If the government holds 51% or more of the paid-up capital of a company, it is a government company.

Example: limited liability

A shareholder holds 1,000 shares of ₹10, fully paid. If the company fails with large debts, the shareholder loses at most ₹10,000 — the amount invested; creditors cannot claim personal property.
