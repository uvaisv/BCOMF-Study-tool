Delegation of Authority

Moderate Asked in 5 of 11 papers · avg 6 marks

Meaning, principles, and why managers and subordinates avoid delegation.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 3 · Unit 12 Delegation of Authority and Decentralisation

Key points

  • Delegation: giving a subordinate formal authority and responsibility for certain work. Elements: entrusting duties (responsibility), granting authority and creating accountability.
  • Benefits: better use of resources, lighter load on top executives, better decisions, initiative and self-development of subordinates.
  • Why managers don't delegate: no confidence in subordinates, fear of losing power, lack of self-confidence.
  • Why subordinates avoid it: prefer to avoid responsibility, fear of criticism, lack of resources, lack of motivation.
  • Making it work: climate of trust, clear objectives, clearly defined authority and responsibility, motivation, good communication and training.
  • Centralisation keeps authority with top managers; decentralisation is systematic delegation to all levels of the organisation. Delegation leads to decentralisation.
  • Degree of decentralisation depends on size, rate of growth, nature of the organisation, management philosophy and environment; neither extreme is desirable — the book calls for a 'golden mean'.

Worked example

Example: a principal delegates

The principal of a college entrusts the annual sports day to a senior teacher (responsibility), lets them spend up to ₹50,000 and pick volunteers (authority), and asks for a report afterwards (accountability).

The principal remains finally accountable to the management — authority can be delegated, ultimate responsibility cannot. When such delegation happens in every department systematically, the college becomes decentralised.

How to write the answer

Definition → the three elements → principles → barriers and how to overcome them.
