Controlling

Moderate Asked in 4 of 11 papers · avg 5 marks

Meaning, nature, principles, advantages and limitations of control; control techniques.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 3 · Unit 13 Control

Key points

  • Control: checking whether actions go as planned and correcting them so they conform to the plan.
  • Characteristics: pervasive, continuous, based on planning, action is its essence, forward-looking, delegation is key, helps cope with uncertainty.
  • Importance: adjusts operations, fixes managerial responsibility, has a psychological effect, aids coordination, improves efficiency and effectiveness.
  • Process: set standards → measure performance → compare with standards and find causes of deviation → correct deviations.
  • Requisites of effective control include clear objectives, suitability, flexibility, self-control, strategic-point control, timely and forward-looking action, attention to the human factor and economy.
  • Types: physical and financial; over actual and anticipated performance; by area of operation.
  • Techniques — traditional: budgetary control, standard costing. Modern: break-even analysis, PERT, CPM, statistical quality control, management audit.

Worked example

Example: control in a biscuit factory

Standard: 10,000 packets per shift with at most 1% rejects.

Measure: today's shift made 9,200 packets with 3% rejects.

Compare and find causes: a worn-out oven belt caused the stoppage and uneven baking.

Correct: replace the belt and schedule preventive maintenance (a forward-looking step so it does not happen again).
