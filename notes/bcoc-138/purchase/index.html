Purchasing & Material Control

Critical Asked in 8 of 8 papers · avg 14 marks

Steps in purchasing materials, purchase requisition and order, centralised and decentralised purchasing, and requirements and advantages of material control.

In your IGNOU bookUnit 3 Procurement, Storage and Issue

Key points

  • Purchase procedure: purchase requisition from stores or department → finding suppliers (enquiries, quotations, tenders) → selecting the supplier → purchase order → follow-up → receiving and inspection (goods received note) → checking the invoice against order and GRN → passing for payment → records.
  • Purchase requisition: a request to the purchase department to buy materials — item, quantity, date needed, signed by an authorised person. Purchase order: the written order to the supplier — item, quantity, price, terms, delivery.
  • Centralised purchasing: one department buys for all. Advantages — bulk discounts, specialist buyers, uniform policy, better control; disadvantages — delays, higher overhead, may not suit local needs.
  • Decentralised purchasing: each unit buys for itself. Advantages — quick, flexible, local suppliers; disadvantages — no bulk discounts, duplication, weaker control.
  • Material control: making sure the right materials are available at the right time at minimum cost. Requirements: proper purchase procedure, receiving and inspection, good storage, issues against requisitions, perpetual inventory, stock levels, classification and codes, ABC analysis, internal checks.
  • Advantages: no stock-outs or overstocking, less waste and theft, lower investment in stock, accurate material costs.

Worked examples

Example: buying steel sheets, step by step

The storekeeper sees stock at the reorder level and sends a purchase requisition. The purchase department invites quotations from three suppliers, compares price, quality and delivery, and places a purchase order with the best one.

When the sheets arrive, stores inspect them and prepare a goods received note. The accounts department checks the supplier's invoice against the order and the note before passing it for payment.

Example: centralised or decentralised?

A company with five factories buys all its steel centrally and gets a bulk discount of 4%. But one factory needing an urgent spare part waits a week for head office — so small urgent items are left to local purchase.
