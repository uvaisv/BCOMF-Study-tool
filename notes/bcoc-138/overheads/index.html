Overheads: Classification, Apportionment & Absorption

High Asked in 7 of 8 papers · avg 13 marks

Concept and classification of overheads, principles and bases of apportionment, primary and secondary distribution, methods of absorption, and under- or over-absorption.

In your IGNOU bookUnit 8 Classification and Distribution of OverheadsUnit 9 Absorption of Factory OverheadsUnit 11 Treatment of Other Overheads and Activity Based Cost Allocation

Key points

  • Overheads: indirect material, indirect labour and indirect expenses — costs that cannot be traced to a single cost unit.
  • Classification: by function (factory, office and administration, selling, distribution), by behaviour (fixed, variable, semi-variable), by element, by controllability.
  • Principles of apportionment: service or use, survey, ability to pay, efficiency. Bases: rent and building costs — floor area; power — horsepower × machine hours; lighting — light points; depreciation and insurance of machines — machine value; canteen and welfare — number of workers; supervision — workers or labour hours.
  • Primary distribution: spread overheads over production and service departments. Secondary distribution: re-apportion service departments to production departments — direct, step ladder, repeated distribution or simultaneous equations.
  • Absorption: charging overheads to units — % of direct materials, of direct wages or of prime cost (prime cost method); labour hour rate; machine hour rate; rate per unit.
  • Under- or over-absorption: causes — wrong estimates, change in capacity used, seasonal effects, method changes, abnormal events. Disposal — supplementary rate, write off to costing P&L, or carry forward if due to a seasonal pattern.
  • Activity-based costing (ABC): instead of one volume-based rate, costs are traced to the activities that cause them (set-ups, inspections, orders) and charged to products by how much of each activity they use — more accurate where many overheads do not vary with volume.

Worked example

Example: apportionment and absorption

Rent ₹12,000 is apportioned on floor area — P1 2,000 sq ft, P2 1,500, service department S 500 → ₹6,000, ₹4,500, ₹1,500.

After primary distribution: P1 ₹30,000, P2 ₹20,000, S ₹10,000. S serves P1 and P2 in the ratio 60 : 40 → P1 ₹36,000, P2 ₹24,000.

Absorption: P1 is machine-based with 3,000 machine hours → ₹12 an hour; P2 is labour-based with 4,000 labour hours → ₹6 an hour.

In the year P1's actual overhead is ₹38,000 and it works 3,200 hours, absorbing 3,200 × 12 = ₹38,400 → over-absorbed by ₹400.

How to solve

  1. List each overhead with the basis of apportionment.
  2. Apportion to all departments in the ratio of the basis (primary distribution).
  3. Total each department.
  4. Re-apportion service departments to production departments in the given ratios (secondary distribution).
  5. Divide production department totals by hours or units to get absorption rates if asked.

Formulas

Overhead absorption rate = Budgeted overheads ÷ Budgeted base (hours, units or % of prime cost)

How to write the answer

Overhead distribution summary: columns for each department; one row per overhead with its basis.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving service department totals unapportioned in the final summary.
