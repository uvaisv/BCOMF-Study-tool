Reconciliation of Cost & Financial Accounts

High Asked in 5 of 8 papers · avg 9 marks

Why profit in cost accounts differs from financial accounts, the reconciliation statement, and integral accounting.

In your IGNOU bookUnit 19 Reconciliation of Cost and Financial Accounts

Key points

  • Items only in financial accounts: interest and dividends received, profit or loss on sale of assets, rent received, discount on issue of shares, fines, donations, income tax, provisions and appropriations.
  • Items only in cost accounts: notional charges such as interest on capital or rent on owned premises.
  • Other causes: under- or over-absorption of overheads, different stock valuations, different depreciation methods, abnormal gains and losses treated differently.
  • Reconciliation statement: start from profit as per cost accounts; add items that make financial profit higher (over-absorbed overheads, incomes only in financial books, overvaluation of opening stock in cost books…); deduct items that make it lower; the result is profit as per financial accounts.
  • Integral (integrated) accounting: one set of books for both cost and financial records, so no reconciliation is needed; saves effort and gives quicker, consistent figures.

Worked example

Example: reconciliation statement

Profit as per cost accounts ₹1,50,000.

Deduct: factory overheads under-absorbed ₹5,000; income tax (only in financial books) ₹20,000; closing stock valued ₹4,000 lower in financial books.

Add: administration overheads over-absorbed ₹3,000; dividend received ₹10,000; notional interest on capital charged only in cost accounts ₹8,000.

Profit as per financial accounts = 1,50,000 − 29,000 + 21,000 = ₹1,42,000.

How to solve

  1. Start from the profit in cost accounts (or financial accounts, as asked).
  2. For each item, decide whether it makes financial profit higher or lower.
  3. Add items that increase financial profit; deduct those that reduce it.
  4. Under-absorbed overheads: deduct; over-absorbed: add (starting from cost profit).
  5. Stock: closing stock valued higher in financial books → add; opening stock higher in financial books → deduct.

Formulas

Profit as per financial accounts = Profit as per cost accounts + Additions − Deductions

How to write the answer

Reconciliation statement: Profit as per cost accounts → Add → Less → Profit as per financial accounts.

Common mistakes

  • Getting the direction wrong — ask: does this item make financial profit higher or lower than cost profit?
