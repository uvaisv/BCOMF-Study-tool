Cost Accounting: Meaning, Objectives & Installation

Critical Asked in 8 of 8 papers · avg 14 marks

Meaning, need, objectives and advantages of cost accounting; cost vs financial accounting; limitations of financial accounting; role of the cost accountant; installing a costing system.

In your IGNOU bookUnit 1 Nature and Scope of Cost Accounting

Key points

  • Cost accounting: classifying, recording and allocating expenses to find the cost of products and services, and presenting the data to management for control and decisions.
  • Objectives: find the cost of each product, job or process; control and reduce costs; help fix selling prices; value inventory; give information for decisions; measure efficiency.
  • Advantages: shows profitable and unprofitable products, controls waste, fixes prices, supports budgeting and planning, helps tenders and quotations, compares performance.
  • Cost vs financial accounting: internal vs external users; product-wise vs overall results; includes estimates and standards vs only actual transactions; optional (mostly) vs compulsory; frequent reports vs annual.
  • Limitations of financial accounting: historical and overall results only, no product or department costs, no cost control or efficiency measure, no basis for pricing or quotations.
  • Role of the cost accountant: set up and run the costing system, collect and analyse costs, prepare cost reports and budgets, suggest cost reduction, advise on pricing and decisions, reconcile with financial accounts.
  • Installing a costing system — points to consider: nature and size of the business, products and processes, organisation, management's needs, a simple and economical system, the right method of costing, forms and records, cooperation of staff, reconciliation with financial accounts. Difficulties: staff resistance, lack of management support, cost of the system, shortage of trained staff — overcome by explaining its benefits, training and starting simply.

Worked example

Example: what cost accounts reveal

A firm makes three products and its financial accounts show an overall profit of ₹2 lakh. Its cost accounts show products A and B earning ₹2.5 lakh while product C loses ₹50,000, largely from wasted material. Management can now correct C's process, raise its price or drop it — decisions the overall profit figure could never support.

How to write the answer

Definition → objectives or points as headed list → conclusion.
