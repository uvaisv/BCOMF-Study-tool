Labour Cost: Time Keeping, Wage Plans & Turnover

Critical Asked in 8 of 8 papers · avg 14 marks

Time keeping and time booking, idle time and overtime, labour turnover — causes, measurement and control — and wage plans (time rate, Halsey, Rowan, Taylor).

In your IGNOU bookUnit 6 Labour: Basic ConceptsUnit 7 Accounting for Labour

Key points

  • Time keeping: recording attendance (arrival and departure) — attendance register, disc/token method, time-recording clock, biometric systems. It is the basis for paying wages.
  • Time booking: recording time spent on each job or process — job cards, daily or weekly time sheets. Purposes: charge labour cost to jobs, find idle time, check efficiency, overtime control.
  • Idle time: time paid for but not worked. Normal idle time (tea breaks, change of shift) is included in labour cost or overheads; abnormal idle time (power failure, strikes, machine breakdown) goes to costing profit and loss.
  • Overtime: work beyond normal hours; the premium is charged to the job if the customer asked for it, to overheads if normal, to costing P&L if abnormal.
  • Labour turnover: rate of change in the workforce. Causes — avoidable (low wages, poor conditions, bad relations, no promotion), unavoidable (retirement, death, illness, marriage, moving). Control — fair pay, good conditions, proper selection and training, promotion policy, grievance handling, exit interviews.
  • Wage plans: time rate; piece rate; Taylor's differential piece rate (a low rate below standard output, a high rate at or above it); Halsey premium plan (time wages + 50% of time saved × rate); Rowan plan (time wages + time saved ÷ time allowed × time taken × rate).

Worked examples

Example: Halsey vs Rowan

Time allowed 10 hours; time taken 8 hours; rate ₹50 an hour; time saved 2 hours.

Halsey: 8 × 50 + 50% × 2 × 50 = 400 + 50 = ₹450.

Rowan: 8 × 50 + (2 ÷ 10) × 8 × 50 = 400 + 80 = ₹480.

For small time savings Rowan pays more; if the worker saved more than half the time allowed, Halsey would pay more.

Example: Taylor's differential piece rate and labour turnover

Standard 20 units an hour (160 in an 8-hour day); normal piece rate ₹5; low rate 80% (₹4), high rate 120% (₹6).

Worker A makes 150 units (below standard) → 150 × 4 = ₹600. Worker B makes 170 (above standard) → 170 × 6 = ₹1,020.

Labour turnover: 30 workers left in the year; workers at the start 480 and at the end 520 → average 500 → 30 ÷ 500 × 100 = 6%.

How to solve

  1. Standard output for the period = standard per hour × hours worked.
  2. Taylor: compare each worker's output with standard and apply the low or high piece rate.
  3. Halsey/Rowan: find time allowed, time taken and time saved, then apply the bonus formula.
  4. Labour turnover: separations ÷ average number of workers × 100 (or replacements, or flux method).

Formulas

Halsey earnings = Time taken × Rate + 50% × Time saved × Rate
Rowan earnings = Time taken × Rate + (Time saved ÷ Time allowed) × Time taken × Rate
Labour turnover (separation) = Separations ÷ Average workers × 100

How to write the answer

Meaning → methods or causes as headings → conclusion; for wage plans, show the calculation for each worker.
