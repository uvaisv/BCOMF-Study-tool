Contract & Job Costing

Critical Asked in 5 of 8 papers · avg 12 marks

Contract accounts, notional profit, profit on incomplete contracts, work-in-progress, contractee's account, and job vs contract costing.

In your IGNOU bookUnit 13 Job CostingUnit 14 Contract Costing

Key points

  • Contract costing: used for large, long-duration work at a site (buildings, bridges); each contract is a cost unit. Job costing: for small, short jobs done to order in the factory (printing, repairs). Differences: size, duration, place, cost unit, payment in instalments against certificates.
  • Contract account — debit: materials, wages (including outstanding), direct expenses, plant (or depreciation), overheads; credit: materials returned and in hand, plant at site at closing value, work in progress (certified + uncertified).
  • Notional profit = value of work done (certified + uncertified) − cost of work done.
  • Profit on incomplete contracts: less than 25% complete — no profit; 25–50% — 1/3 × notional profit × cash received ÷ work certified; over 50% — 2/3 × notional profit × cash received ÷ work certified; nearly complete — estimated total profit × work certified ÷ contract price. The rest is a reserve. (AS 7 now uses the percentage-of-completion method.)
  • Work in progress in the Balance Sheet = work certified + uncertified work − profit reserve − cash received.
  • Contractee's account: debit contract account with work certified; credit cash received — the balance is due.

Worked example

Example: profit on an incomplete contract

Contract price ₹20,00,000. Costs to date: materials 5,00,000; wages 3,00,000 + outstanding 20,000; plant 2,00,000 (value at year-end 1,50,000, so depreciation 50,000); overheads 80,000; materials at site 50,000.

Cost of work done = 5,00,000 + 3,20,000 + 50,000 + 80,000 − 50,000 = ₹9,00,000.

Work certified 12,00,000 + uncertified 1,00,000 = 13,00,000 → notional profit ₹4,00,000.

Completion = 12 ÷ 20 = 60% (over half) → profit to P&L = 2/3 × 4,00,000 × 10,80,000/12,00,000 = ₹2,40,000; reserve ₹1,60,000.

Work in progress in the Balance Sheet = 13,00,000 − 1,60,000 − 10,80,000 cash received = ₹60,000.

How to solve

  1. Debit all costs; add outstanding wages; debit plant at cost (or charge depreciation).
  2. Credit materials returned, materials and plant at site at year-end.
  3. Credit work certified and uncertified work → balance is notional profit.
  4. Find the stage of completion = work certified ÷ contract price.
  5. Apply the fraction (nil, 1/3 or 2/3) × cash received ÷ work certified → profit to P&L; rest to reserve.
  6. Show WIP in the Balance Sheet net of reserve and cash received.

Formulas

Notional profit = (Work certified + Uncertified work) − Cost of work done
Profit to P&L (over 50%) = 2/3 × Notional profit × Cash received ÷ Work certified

How to write the answer

Contract account in T-form → working note for profit to P&L → WIP in the Balance Sheet.

Common mistakes

  • Taking the whole notional profit to P&L on an incomplete contract.
