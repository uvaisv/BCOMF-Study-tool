Stock Levels, EOQ & Inventory Control

Moderate Asked in 6 of 8 papers · avg 7 marks

Reorder, minimum, maximum and danger levels, economic order quantity, ABC analysis, inventory turnover and perpetual inventory.

In your IGNOU bookUnit 4 Inventory Control

Key points

  • Stock levels keep enough stock to avoid stoppages without overstocking.
  • ABC analysis: A items — about 10% of items, 70% of value (tight control); B — 20% and 20%; C — 70% of items, 10% of value (simple control).
  • Inventory turnover ratio = materials consumed ÷ average stock; a high ratio means fast-moving stock; it identifies slow-moving and dead stock and helps cut investment.
  • Perpetual inventory: a continuous record of receipts, issues and balances in bin cards and the stores ledger, checked by continuous stock-taking — finds losses quickly and gives up-to-date stock figures.

Worked examples

Example: stock levels

Usage per week: minimum 100, normal 150, maximum 200 units. Reorder period 4 to 6 weeks (normal 5). Reorder quantity 1,000.

Reorder level = 200 × 6 = 1,200 units.

Minimum level = 1,200 − 150 × 5 = 450 units.

Maximum level = 1,200 + 1,000 − 100 × 4 = 1,800 units.

Example: EOQ

Annual usage 12,500 units; cost per order ₹160; price ₹40 and carrying cost 10% → ₹4 per unit a year.

EOQ = √(2 × 12,500 × 160 ÷ 4) = √10,00,000 = 1,000 units, so 12.5 orders a year.

Check: ordering cost 12.5 × 160 = ₹2,000 and carrying cost (1,000 ÷ 2) × 4 = ₹2,000 — equal at the EOQ.

Diagrams

How to solve

  1. Write the given usage and lead-time figures (minimum, normal, maximum).
  2. Reorder level first, then minimum and maximum levels from it.
  3. EOQ: A = annual usage, O = cost per order, C = carrying cost per unit per year (price × %).

Formulas

Reorder level = Maximum usage × Maximum reorder period
Minimum level = Reorder level − (Normal usage × Normal reorder period)
Maximum level = Reorder level + Reorder quantity − (Minimum usage × Minimum reorder period)
EOQ = √(2 × A × O ÷ C)
Inventory turnover ratio = Cost of materials consumed ÷ Average stock
