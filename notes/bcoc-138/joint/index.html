Joint Products & By-Products

High Asked in 6 of 8 papers · avg 7 marks

Joint products and by-products, split-off point, apportionment of joint costs, and accounting for by-products including the reverse cost method.

In your IGNOU bookUnit 16 Joint Products and By-Products

Key points

  • Joint products: two or more products of similar importance from the same process (petrol, diesel and kerosene from crude oil). By-products: products of lower value produced incidentally (molasses from sugar).
  • Split-off point: the stage at which joint products become separately identifiable.
  • Apportioning joint costs: physical units, average unit cost, sales value at split-off, net realisable value, technical estimates.
  • By-products: sales value (less further costs) credited to the main product's process account; or the reverse cost method — sales value minus estimated profit, selling expenses and further processing cost gives the cost credited to the main product.
  • Treatment depends on value: if negligible, income is credited to costing P&L; if significant, it reduces the main product's cost.

Worked examples

Example: joint products — the basis changes the answer

A joint process costing ₹1,20,000 yields 2,000 units of A (worth ₹60 each at split-off, ₹1,20,000) and 1,000 units of B (worth ₹120 each, ₹1,20,000).

Physical units basis (2 : 1): A ₹80,000, B ₹40,000.

Sales value basis (1 : 1): A ₹60,000, B ₹60,000.

Example: by-product by the reverse cost method

By-product sales ₹20,000; expected profit 20% of sales ₹4,000; selling costs ₹2,000; processing after split-off ₹3,000.

Cost credited to the main process = 20,000 − 4,000 − 2,000 − 3,000 = ₹11,000.

If the joint cost is ₹1,00,000 and the main product output is 5,000 units, its cost = (1,00,000 − 11,000) ÷ 5,000 = ₹17.80 a unit.

How to solve

  1. Find joint cost up to split-off (materials + labour + overheads).
  2. Reverse cost method: by-product sales − profit − selling costs − further processing = cost to credit.
  3. Credit that amount to the main process account; cost per unit of main product = net cost ÷ output.
  4. For joint products, apportion joint cost in the ratio asked (units or sales value).

Formulas

By-product cost (reverse method) = Sales value − Profit − Selling and distribution cost − Post-separation cost

How to write the answer

Process account for the main product crediting the by-product's value → cost per unit of the main product.
