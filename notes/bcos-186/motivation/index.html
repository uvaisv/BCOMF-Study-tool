Motivating & Paying Salespeople

Critical Asked in 8 of 9 papers · avg 18 marks

Role of motivation, Maslow's theory for salespeople, financial and non-financial techniques, compensation plans.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 7 Sales Force MotivationBlock 1 · Unit 4 Sales Force Management (4.7–4.8)

Key points

  • Motivation creates an urge in a person to reach a goal; a salesperson's motivation directly affects performance and targets.
  • Salespeople work alone, face rejection and irregular results, so they need more motivation than most staff.
  • Motivation is dynamic — needs change, and a satisfied need no longer motivates.
  • Maslow applied to salespeople: physiological (fair pay), safety (job security, insurance), social (team belonging), esteem (recognition, awards) and self-actualisation (challenging targets, growth).
  • Monetary rewards: salary, commission, bonus, incentives, contests, profit sharing, fringe benefits. Non-monetary: recognition, praise, promotion, training, participation in setting targets, sales meetings.
  • Compensation plans: straight salary (security, less push), straight commission (strong push, insecure) and combination plans (salary plus commission or bonus — most common). The right plan depends on the firm's nature and size and the length of the sales cycle.

Worked examples

Example: comparing compensation plans

A salesperson sells ₹8 lakh in a month.

Straight salary: ₹20,000 whatever the sales — secure, but little push to sell more.

Straight commission at 3%: ₹24,000 this month — strong push, but in a bad month earnings may fall close to nil.

Combination: ₹15,000 salary + 2% commission on sales above ₹5 lakh = 15,000 + 6,000 = ₹21,000 — security plus incentive, which is why it is the most common plan.

Example: Maslow for a sales team

Fair pay and travel allowance (physiological), health insurance and job security (safety), team outings (social), 'Salesperson of the Month' award (esteem), and a chance to open a new territory (self-actualisation).
