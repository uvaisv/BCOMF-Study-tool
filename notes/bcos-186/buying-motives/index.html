Buying Motives

High Asked in 9 of 9 papers · avg 10 marks

Meaning, types and application of buying motives in personal selling.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 5 Buying Motives

Key points

  • A buying motive is the urge or reason that leads a customer to buy.
  • Two main types: product motives (why this product — size, colour, package, quality, design) and patronage motives (why from this seller).
  • Each is either rational (decided logically) or emotional (driven by feelings).
  • Rational product motives: safety or security, economy (low running cost), price, suitability, versatility, durability, convenience or comfort.
  • Emotional product motives: prestige or pride, emulation (copying others), affection, individuality, pleasure or recreation, habit.
  • Patronage motives: convenience, lower prices, credit facilities, wider choice, the shop's reputation and appearance, recommendations, emulation, prestige and habit.
  • Use in selling: find the prospect's dominant motive early, stress the benefit that matches it, and use it when handling objections and closing.

Worked example

Example: rational and emotional motives in one purchase

A family buys a car. Rational product motives: safety (airbags), economy (mileage) and durability. Emotional product motives: prestige (a well-known brand) and emulation (the neighbour has one). Patronage motive: they buy from a dealer they trust because of good after-sales service and easy finance.

A good salesperson finds the dominant motive — here safety, for the parents — and builds the presentation around it.
