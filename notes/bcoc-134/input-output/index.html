Input–Output Analysis

Low Asked in 2 of 11 papers · avg 4 marks

An occasional 20-mark theory question on Leontief's input–output model.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 4 Application of Matrices in Business and Economics

Key points

  • Developed by Wassily Leontief: shows how the output of one industry is an input to others.
  • Transaction table: rows show how each sector's output is used; columns show the inputs each sector uses.
  • Technology (input) coefficient aᵢⱼ = input from sector i per unit output of sector j.
  • Output needed: X = (I − A)⁻¹ D, where D is final demand.
  • Hawkins–Simon conditions ensure the system is workable.
  • Uses: economic planning, forecasting sector output, finding the effect of demand changes.
  • Other matrix uses in the book: showing business data, finding market demand–supply equilibrium and solving a national income model.

Worked example

Example: two-industry Leontief model

Technology matrix A = [0.2 0.3; 0.4 0.1] (column j = inputs per ₹1 of output of industry j). Final demand D = (120, 60).

I − A = [0.8 −0.3; −0.4 0.9]; |I − A| = 0.72 − 0.12 = 0.6.

(I − A)⁻¹ = (1/0.6) × [0.9 0.3; 0.4 0.8].

X = (I − A)⁻¹ D → X₁ = (0.9×120 + 0.3×60)/0.6 = 210; X₂ = (0.4×120 + 0.8×60)/0.6 = 160.

Check: industry 1 output = 0.2×210 + 0.3×160 + 120 = 210 ✓. Hawkins–Simon holds: 0.8 and 0.9 are positive and |I − A| > 0.

Formulas

X = (I − A)⁻¹ D
