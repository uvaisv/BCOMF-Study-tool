Matrices & Determinants

High Asked in 11 of 11 papers · avg 15 marks

Types of matrices (often in Q1), operations, determinants and their properties, business uses.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 1 Introduction to MatricesBlock 1 · Unit 2 Determinants

Key points

  • Matrix: a rectangular arrangement of numbers in rows and columns; order m × n.
  • Types: row, column, square, rectangular, diagonal, scalar, identity/unit, zero, symmetric, skew-symmetric, orthogonal (AAᵀ = I), transpose.
  • Equal matrices: same order and equal corresponding elements.
  • Multiplication AB is possible when columns of A = rows of B; AB ≠ BA in general; (AB)C = A(BC); A(B + C) = AB + AC.
  • Determinant properties: interchanging rows changes the sign; two identical rows → 0; |Aᵀ| = |A|; a common factor can be taken out.
  • Business uses: storing sales/production data, costing orders (quantity × price), solving equations, input–output analysis.

Worked examples

Example: a bill with matrix multiplication

Customer A buys 2 pens, 3 notebooks and 1 file; customer B buys 1 pen, 5 notebooks and 2 files. Prices: pen ₹10, notebook ₹40, file ₹25.

Quantity matrix (2 × 3) × price matrix (3 × 1) = bill matrix (2 × 1):

A: 2×10 + 3×40 + 1×25 = ₹165. B: 1×10 + 5×40 + 2×25 = ₹260.

The product works because the quantity matrix has 3 columns and the price matrix 3 rows.

Example: a 2 × 2 determinant and a property

|3 2; 1 4| = 3×4 − 2×1 = 10. Swap the two rows: |1 4; 3 2| = 2 − 12 = −10 — the sign changes.

How to solve

  1. Construct aᵢⱼ matrix: substitute each i (row) and j (column) in the given rule.
  2. Bill/production problems: write quantities as one matrix, prices as another, multiply.

Formulas

|A| (2×2) = ad − bc

Common mistakes

  • Multiplying matrices element by element.
