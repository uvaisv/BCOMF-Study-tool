Measures of Dispersion

High Asked in 9 of 11 papers · avg 12 marks

Standard deviation, variance, quartile deviation, mean deviation and combined SD.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 4 · Unit 14 Measures of Dispersion

Key points

  • Dispersion: how spread out values are around the average.
  • Absolute measures (range, QD, MD, SD) are in original units; relative measures (coefficients, CV) are pure numbers for comparison.
  • Coefficient of variation CV = SD/Mean × 100 — lower CV means more consistent.
  • Quartile deviation = (Q₃ − Q₁)/2.
  • A good measure of dispersion is rigidly defined, easy to compute and understand, based on all observations, capable of algebraic treatment and not unduly affected by extreme values — standard deviation meets most of these.

Worked examples

Example: standard deviation

Data: 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9. Mean = 40/8 = 5.

Squared deviations: 9, 1, 1, 1, 0, 0, 4, 16 → total 32. σ = √(32/8) = √4 = 2.

Example: which batsman is more consistent?

A: mean 50 runs, σ = 10 → CV = 10/50 × 100 = 20%. B: mean 40 runs, σ = 6 → CV = 6/40 × 100 = 15%.

A scores more on average, but B has the lower CV, so B is more consistent.

Quartile deviation: if Q₁ = 20 and Q₃ = 40, QD = (40 − 20)/2 = 10 and its coefficient = (40 − 20)/(40 + 20) = 0.33.

How to solve

  1. Grouped SD (step deviation): d = (x − A)/i; SD = i × √[Σfd²/N − (Σfd/N)²].
  2. Quartiles: Q₁ at N/4 item, Q₃ at 3N/4 item, then L + (pos − cf)/f × i.
  3. Combined mean = (N₁x̄₁ + N₂x̄₂)/(N₁ + N₂); combined SD uses dᵢ = x̄ᵢ − x̄₁₂.

Formulas

σ = √[Σf(x − x̄)²/N]
σ₁₂ = √{[N₁(σ₁² + d₁²) + N₂(σ₂² + d₂²)] / (N₁ + N₂)}
CV = σ/x̄ × 100

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to multiply by the class width i in step deviation.
  • Using cumulative frequency of the quartile class instead of the class before it.
