Limits & Continuity

Low Asked in 3 of 11 papers · avg 4 marks

Occasional: evaluate limits (factorisation, rationalisation) or find points of discontinuity.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 6 Limit and Continuity

Key points

  • lim x→a f(x) is the value f(x) approaches as x gets close to a.
  • 0/0 form: factorise and cancel, or rationalise, then substitute.
  • Continuity at a: f(a) exists, the limit exists and equals f(a).
  • Standard limits: lim (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) = n·aⁿ⁻¹; lim (eˣ − 1)/x = 1 as x → 0.

Worked examples

Example: 0/0 forms

lim x→3 (x² − 9)/(x − 3) = lim (x − 3)(x + 3)/(x − 3) = 3 + 3 = 6.

lim x→2 (x⁵ − 32)/(x − 2) = 5 × 2⁴ = 80, using lim (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) = n·aⁿ⁻¹.

Rationalise: lim x→0 (√(1 + x) − 1)/x = lim x/[x(√(1 + x) + 1)] = 1/2.

Example: finding k for continuity

f(x) = (x² − 4)/(x − 2) for x ≠ 2, and f(2) = k. The limit as x → 2 is 2 + 2 = 4, so f is continuous at 2 only if k = 4.

How to solve

  1. Substitute first. If you get 0/0, factorise or rationalise, cancel, substitute again.

Formulas

lim (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) = n·aⁿ⁻¹ as x → a
