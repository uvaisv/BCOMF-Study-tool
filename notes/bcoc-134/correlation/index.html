Correlation

Moderate Asked in 6 of 11 papers · avg 9 marks

Karl Pearson's or Spearman's rank correlation numericals, and correlation vs regression theory.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 5 · Unit 15 Simple Linear Correlation

Key points

  • Correlation measures the degree and direction of relationship between two variables; r lies between −1 and +1.
  • Positive (move together), negative (move opposite), zero (no linear relation).
  • r is unaffected by change of origin and scale (only sign may change with a negative scale).
  • Correlation vs regression: degree of relationship vs estimating one variable; r symmetric vs two different lines; no cause-effect vs dependent/independent variables.

Worked examples

Example: Karl Pearson's r

Using the same data (x: 1–5; y: 2, 4, 5, 4, 5): Σdxdy = 6, Σdx² = 10, Σdy² = 6.

r = 6 ÷ √(10 × 6) = 6 ÷ 7.75 = 0.77 — a fairly high positive correlation.

Example: Spearman's rank correlation

Two judges rank five contestants: judge 1 gives 1, 2, 3, 4, 5; judge 2 gives 2, 1, 4, 3, 5.

D = −1, 1, −1, 1, 0 → ΣD² = 4.

ρ = 1 − 6 × 4 / [5 × (25 − 1)] = 1 − 24/120 = 0.8 — the judges largely agree.

Diagram

How to solve

  1. Karl Pearson: table of x, y, dx, dy, dx², dy², dxdy; r = Σdxdy / √(Σdx² × Σdy²) (deviations from actual means).
  2. Spearman: rank both series, D = R₁ − R₂, ρ = 1 − 6ΣD²/[n(n² − 1)].

Formulas

r = Σdxdy / √(Σdx²·Σdy²)
ρ = 1 − 6ΣD² / n(n² − 1)

Common mistakes

  • Ranking in opposite orders for the two series.
