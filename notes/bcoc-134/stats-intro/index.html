Meaning, Functions & Limitations of Statistics

High Asked in 8 of 11 papers · avg 11 marks

Theory for Q1(b) or a full 20-mark Part B question.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 4 · Unit 12 Introduction to Statistics

Key points

  • Statistics (plural): numerical data; (singular): methods of collecting, organising, analysing and interpreting data.
  • Functions: simplifies complex data, enables comparison, helps forecasting, tests hypotheses, helps policy making.
  • Importance in business: market research, quality control, forecasting sales, financial analysis, auditing (sampling).
  • Limitations: studies aggregates not individuals, only quantitative facts, results true on average only, can be misused.
  • Descriptive statistics summarise data; inferential statistics draw conclusions about a population from a sample.
  • Distrust of statistics ('lies, damned lies and statistics') comes from manipulated or incomplete data presented to mislead, and from data that does not show its own quality — the fault lies with the user, not the science.

Worked examples

Example: an average that misleads

Nine households in a lane earn ₹10,000 a month and one earns ₹1,00,000. The mean income is ₹19,000, yet nine families earn about half that. The statistic is correct, but used alone it misleads — statistics describe aggregates, not individuals.

Example: descriptive vs inferential

A company surveys 500 of its 50,000 customers. 'Average rating 4.1 out of 5 in the sample' is descriptive. 'About 80% of all our customers are satisfied, give or take 3%' is inferential — a conclusion about the whole population from a sample.

How to write the answer

Definition (both senses) → 6–8 headed points → one business example each → conclusion.
