Maxima, Minima & Applications of Derivatives

Moderate Asked in 6 of 11 papers · avg 10 marks

Local maxima/minima, increasing/decreasing intervals, marginal revenue and elasticity.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 8 Maxima and Minima of FunctionsBlock 2 · Unit 9 Application of Derivatives

Key points

  • Increasing where f′(x) > 0, decreasing where f′(x) < 0.
  • Stationary points where f′(x) = 0.
  • Second derivative test: f″(x) < 0 → local maximum; f″(x) > 0 → local minimum.
  • Marginal revenue MR = d(TR)/dx; marginal cost MC = d(TC)/dx; profit is maximum where MR = MC.
  • Price elasticity of demand = (% change in quantity) ÷ (% change in price); income elasticity uses income; cross elasticity uses the price of another good.

Worked examples

Example: profit-maximising output

TR = 50x − x² and TC = x² + 10x + 100.

MR = d(TR)/dx = 50 − 2x; MC = d(TC)/dx = 2x + 10. MR = MC → 50 − 2x = 2x + 10 → x = 10.

Profit P = TR − TC = −2x² + 40x − 100; P″ = −4 < 0, so x = 10 gives a maximum.

Maximum profit = −200 + 400 − 100 = ₹100.

Example: price elasticity from a demand function

q = 100 − 2p. At p = 20, q = 60 and dq/dp = −2.

Ed = (dq/dp) × (p/q) = −2 × 20/60 = −0.67. |Ed| < 1, so demand is inelastic at this price: raising the price a little would raise total revenue.

How to solve

  1. Find f′(x) and set it to 0; solve for x.
  2. Find f″(x) and check its sign at each x.
  3. Put each x back into f(x) to get the maximum or minimum value.
  4. For intervals: factor f′(x), mark the roots on a number line, test signs.

Formulas

Ed = (dq/dp) × (p/q)
TR = P × X, MR = d(TR)/dX

Common mistakes

  • Stopping at f′(x) = 0 without the second-derivative test.
  • Not stating the maximum or minimum value itself.
