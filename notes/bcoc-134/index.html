Exam tips

  • Q1 is compulsory: learn one-line definitions and formulas from every topic — matrix types, SI/CI formulas, quartiles, index number, time-series components.
  • Part A: Differentiation ('find dy/dx, any four') and Compound Interest appear almost every session and are pure method — the safest picks.
  • Part B: Index Numbers (Fisher's index and its tests) and Time Series (least squares trend, components) are asked every session.
  • Pick numericals you can finish. A fully worked problem scores more reliably than a long theory answer in this paper.
  • Write the formula first, then substitute, then the answer with units (₹, years, %). Formula lines earn marks by themselves.
  • Show the working table (x, y, x², xy …) for correlation, regression, SD and trend questions — examiners mark the table.
  • For 'any four' dy/dx questions, attempt exactly four and name the rule used (product, quotient, chain).
  • Round only at the last step; keep 2–4 decimals in between.
