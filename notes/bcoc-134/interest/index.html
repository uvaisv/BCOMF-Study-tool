Simple & Compound Interest

Critical Asked in 11 of 11 papers · avg 20 marks

Asked every session: CI numericals (varying rates, half-yearly, doubling time) or theory on SI vs CI, present value, nominal vs effective rate.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 3 · Unit 10 Interest RatesBlock 3 · Unit 11 Compounding and Discounting

Key points

  • Simple interest: interest only on the principal. Compound interest: interest on principal plus accumulated interest.
  • Nominal rate: stated annual rate. Effective rate: actual annual rate when interest compounds more than once a year.
  • Present value: today's value of a future sum, found by discounting.
  • Discount factor = 1 / (1 + r)ⁿ.
  • Equation of value: equates the values of two sets of payments at one date.
  • Continuous compounding: A = Pe^(rt), used when interest is added over infinitely small periods.
  • Simple (bank) discount is interest deducted in advance as a percentage of the maturity amount: D = A × d × t. Present value at a discount rate: P = A(1 − d)ⁿ.

Worked examples

Example: simple vs compound, ₹10,000 at 10% for 2 years

SI = 10,000 × 10 × 2 / 100 = ₹2,000.

Compounded yearly: A = 10,000 × (1.10)² = ₹12,100 → CI = ₹2,100.

Compounded half-yearly: rate 5% for 4 periods → A = 10,000 × (1.05)⁴ = ₹12,155.06 → CI = ₹2,155.06. More frequent compounding gives more interest.

Example: effective rate, present value and doubling time

Effective rate of 12% compounded monthly = (1 + 0.12/12)¹² − 1 = (1.01)¹² − 1 = 12.68%.

Present value of ₹50,000 due in 3 years at 8% = 50,000 ÷ (1.08)³ = ₹39,691.61.

Doubling at 8% a year: 2 = (1.08)ⁿ → n = log 2 ÷ log 1.08 ≈ 9 years.

How to solve

  1. Write P, R, T (and n compounding periods per year).
  2. Half-yearly: rate ÷ 2, periods × 2. Monthly: rate ÷ 12, periods × 12.
  3. Different rates each year: A = P(1 + r₁)(1 + r₂)(1 + r₃).
  4. Doubling time: 2 = (1 + r)ⁿ → n = log 2 ÷ log(1 + r).
  5. CI = A − P.

Formulas

SI = P × R × T / 100
A = P(1 + r/100)ⁿ
Effective rate = (1 + i/m)ᵐ − 1
PV = A / (1 + r)ⁿ

Common mistakes

  • Not adjusting the rate and periods for half-yearly or monthly compounding.
  • Giving the amount when the question asks for compound interest.
