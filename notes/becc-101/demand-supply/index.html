Demand, Supply, Equilibrium & Tax Incidence

High Asked in 10 of 11 papers · avg 15 marks

Equilibrium from equations, shifts, price ceilings and floors, consumer/producer surplus, tax burden and deadweight loss.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 3 Demand and Supply in PracticeBlock 1 · Unit 2 Demand and Supply Analysis (2.2–2.8)

Key points

  • Demand Q = a − bP slopes down; supply Q = c + dP slopes up.
  • Equilibrium where Qd = Qs.
  • Change in price of a substitute moves demand the same way; of a complement, the opposite way.
  • Price ceiling below equilibrium → shortage; price floor above equilibrium → surplus.
  • Tax incidence: the more inelastic side bears more of the tax. Perfectly elastic demand → sellers bear it all; perfectly elastic supply → buyers bear it all.
  • Deadweight loss: the lost surplus from trades that no longer happen after a tax.
  • Change in quantity demanded (movement along the curve) comes from the good's own price; change in demand (shift of the curve) comes from income, tastes, prices of related goods and expectations. The same applies to supply.
  • A tax on land (fixed supply) falls wholly on landowners — the rent paid by users does not change.

Worked examples

Example: equilibrium, surplus and a tax

Qd = 100 − 2P and Qs = −20 + 2P. Setting 100 − 2P = −20 + 2P gives P* = ₹30 and Q* = 40.

Consumer surplus = ½ × (50 − 30) × 40 = 400; producer surplus = ½ × (30 − 10) × 40 = 400.

A ₹4 tax per unit on sellers: new supply Qs = −28 + 2P → P = ₹32, Q = 36. Buyers pay ₹2 more and sellers get ₹2 less (₹28) — shared equally because the two curves are equally steep. Tax revenue = 4 × 36 = 144; deadweight loss = ½ × 4 × 4 = 8.

Example: a price ceiling

With the same curves, a ceiling of ₹25 makes Qd = 50 but Qs = 30 → a shortage of 20 units, leading to queues, rationing or black markets.

How to solve

  1. Set Qd = Qs and solve for P*.
  2. Substitute P* into either equation for Q*.
  3. Consumer surplus = ½ × (max price − P*) × Q*; producer surplus = ½ × (P* − min price) × Q* for straight lines.

Formulas

a − bP = c + dP → P* = (a − c)/(b + d)

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to draw the diagram with the equilibrium marked.
