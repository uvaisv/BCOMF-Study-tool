Elasticity of Demand & Supply

High Asked in 10 of 11 papers · avg 10 marks

Price, income and cross elasticity; point and midpoint methods; determinants; elasticity and marginal revenue.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 2 Demand and Supply Analysis (2.9–2.12)

Key points

  • Price elasticity of demand: % change in quantity ÷ % change in price.
  • Determinants: availability of substitutes, necessity vs luxury, share of income, time period, number of uses.
  • Income elasticity: > 1 luxury, 0–1 necessity, < 0 inferior; 0 means demand does not respond to income.
  • Cross elasticity: positive for substitutes, negative for complements.
  • Point elasticity on a straight-line demand curve = lower segment ÷ upper segment.
  • Elasticity of supply depends on time, spare capacity, ease of storing, and factor mobility.
  • The book measures price elasticity by the point (percentage) method, the total expenditure (outlay) method and the geometric method. Total expenditure method: if spending rises when price falls, e > 1; unchanged, e = 1; falls, e < 1.

Worked examples

Example: point and arc elasticity

Q = 100 − 2P at P = ₹20: Q = 60, so e = 2 × 20/60 = 0.67 (inelastic).

Arc: price rises from ₹10 to ₹12 and quantity falls from 100 to 80. e = (20/90) ÷ (2/11) = 1.22 (elastic). Check with total spending: 10 × 100 = ₹1,000 falls to 12 × 80 = ₹960 when the price rises — spending falls, so demand is elastic.

At P = ₹20 with e = 2, MR = 20 × (1 − 1/2) = ₹10.

Example: income and cross elasticity

Income rises 10%: restaurant meals rise 20% (income elasticity 2 → luxury); rice rises 3% (0.3 → necessity); coarse grain falls 5% (−0.5 → inferior).

Tea's price rises 10% and coffee demand rises 5% → cross elasticity +0.5 (substitutes). Petrol's price rises and demand for cars falls → negative (complements).

How to solve

  1. Point: e = (dQ/dP) × (P/Q).
  2. Midpoint (arc): e = [(Q₂ − Q₁)/((Q₂ + Q₁)/2)] ÷ [(P₂ − P₁)/((P₂ + P₁)/2)].
  3. From total revenue R(Q): MR = dR/dQ, then use MR = P(1 − 1/e).

Formulas

MR = P (1 − 1/e)
e = (dQ/dP)·(P/Q)

Common mistakes

  • Dropping the minus sign without saying you use absolute values.
