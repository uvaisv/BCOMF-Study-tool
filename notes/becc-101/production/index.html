Production: Stages, Isoquants & Returns to Scale

Critical Asked in 11 of 11 papers · avg 28 marks

Law of variable proportions (three stages), isoquants and ridge lines, least-cost combination, expansion path, returns to scale, economies of scale.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 3 · Unit 6 Production with One Variable InputBlock 3 · Unit 7 Production with Two and More Variable Inputs

Key points

  • Law of variable proportions (short run, one input variable): Stage I — AP rises (increasing returns); Stage II — AP and MP fall, MP > 0 (rational stage); Stage III — MP negative. A rational producer operates in Stage II.
  • TP rises at an increasing rate while MP rises, at a decreasing rate when MP falls, and is maximum when MP = 0; MP cuts AP at AP's maximum.
  • Isoquant: combinations of two inputs giving the same output; slopes down, convex to origin; slope = MRTS.
  • Ridge lines join points where isoquants turn upward; the area between them is the economic region of production.
  • Least-cost combination: tangency of isoquant and isocost line — MPL/MPK = w/r (MPL/w = MPK/r).
  • Expansion path: line joining tangency points as outlay rises; a straight line from the origin for a homogeneous production function.
  • Returns to scale (long run, all inputs change): increasing, constant, decreasing — seen from isoquant spacing. For Q = AL^a K^b: a + b > 1 IRS, = 1 CRS, < 1 DRS.
  • Economies of scale: internal (technical, managerial, marketing, financial) and external (industry growth); diseconomies raise LAC.
  • A production function shows the maximum output from given inputs with given technology; the short run has at least one fixed input, the long run none.

Worked examples

Example: the three stages from a table

Workers (L) 1 to 7 on a fixed plot give total product 10, 24, 36, 44, 48, 48, 45.

MP: 10, 14, 12, 8, 4, 0, −3. AP: 10, 12, 12, 11, 9.6, 8, 6.4.

Stage I (AP rising) runs up to 3 workers, where AP peaks at 12. Stage II (AP and MP falling, MP positive) runs from 3 to 6 workers; TP is maximum (48) where MP = 0. Stage III (MP negative) starts after 6 workers. A rational farmer employs between 3 and 6 workers.

Example: returns to scale and least-cost choice

Q = 10L^0.5 K^0.5: exponents add to 1 → constant returns — doubling L and K doubles output. Q = L^0.6 K^0.6: they add to 1.2 → increasing returns.

Least cost: MPL = 20, MPK = 30, wage ₹10, rent ₹20 → MPL/w = 2 but MPK/r = 1.5. The last rupee buys more output through labour, so the firm should use more labour and less capital until the ratios are equal.

How to write the answer

Common mistakes

  • Confusing returns to a factor (short run) with returns to scale (long run).
  • Drawing isoquants that cross.
