Monopoly & Price Discrimination

Moderate Asked in 9 of 11 papers · avg 8 marks

Monopoly equilibrium, no supply curve, deadweight loss, price discrimination, public monopoly pricing, mark-up pricing.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 4 · Unit 10 Monopoly: Price and Output Decisions

Key points

  • Monopoly: single seller, no close substitutes, barriers to entry; downward-sloping AR with MR below it.
  • Equilibrium MR = MC; a monopolist never produces on the inelastic part of demand (MR would be negative).
  • No supply curve: the same price can go with different quantities depending on demand.
  • Deadweight loss: output lower and price higher than under perfect competition.
  • Price discrimination: 1st degree (each unit at maximum price), 2nd degree (by quantity blocks), 3rd degree (different markets; higher price where demand is less elastic).
  • Public monopoly: marginal-cost pricing (efficient but may cause loss) vs average-cost pricing (no loss, less efficient).
  • Mark-up pricing: price = AVC + percentage mark-up.
  • Compared with perfect competition, monopoly gives a higher price, lower output and P > MC — hence allocative inefficiency and a deadweight loss.

Worked examples

Example: monopoly vs competition

Demand P = 100 − Q; MC = ₹20 (constant).

Monopoly: MR = 100 − 2Q = 20 → Q = 40, P = ₹60, profit (60 − 20) × 40 = ₹1,600.

Perfect competition would give P = MC = ₹20 and Q = 80.

Deadweight loss of monopoly = ½ × (60 − 20) × (80 − 40) = ₹800.

Example: third-degree price discrimination

MC ₹10. In market A demand elasticity is 2; in market B it is 4. Using MR = P(1 − 1/e) = MC: P_A = 10 ÷ 0.5 = ₹20; P_B = 10 ÷ 0.75 = ₹13.33. The higher price is charged where demand is less elastic — like higher fares for business travellers.
