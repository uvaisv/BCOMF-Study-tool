Cardinal Utility & Consumer Surplus

High Asked in 10 of 11 papers · avg 11 marks

Law of diminishing marginal utility, equi-marginal utility, consumer equilibrium, deriving demand, consumer surplus.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 4 Consumer Behaviour: Cardinal Approach

Key points

  • Cardinal approach: utility measurable in units (utils); assumptions include constant marginal utility of money and independent utilities.
  • Law of diminishing marginal utility: each extra unit gives less satisfaction; exceptions include money, hobbies, addictive goods.
  • One-good equilibrium: MUx = Px (in money terms). Two goods: MUx/Px = MUy/Py (equi-marginal utility).
  • Demand curve from DMU: as price falls, more units satisfy MU = P.
  • Consumer surplus (Marshall): what the consumer is willing to pay minus what they actually pay — area under demand above price.
  • Criticism: utility is not measurable; MU of money is not constant; ignores income effect; cannot explain Giffen goods.

Worked example

Example: how many mangoes, and the consumer surplus

MU of successive mangoes (in ₹): 20, 16, 12, 8, 4. Price ₹10 each.

Buy while MU ≥ price → 3 mangoes (the 4th is worth only ₹8).

Consumer surplus = what they would pay (20 + 16 + 12 = 48) − what they pay (3 × 10 = 30) = ₹18.

If the price falls to ₹8, they buy 4 — this is how the downward-sloping demand curve follows from diminishing marginal utility.

How to solve

  1. From total utility, find MU for each unit; buy up to the unit where MU ≥ price.

Formulas

Equilibrium: MUx/Px = MUy/Py
