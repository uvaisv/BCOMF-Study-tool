Cost Curves (Short & Long Run)

Moderate Asked in 8 of 11 papers · avg 8 marks

LAC as the envelope of SAC curves, LMC vs SMC, economies of scale and the shape of LAC, economic vs accounting profit.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 3 · Unit 8 The Cost of Production

Key points

  • Short run: fixed and variable costs; AFC falls continuously; AVC, ATC and MC are U-shaped; MC cuts AVC and ATC at their minima.
  • LAC is the envelope of SAC curves — tangent to each, U-shaped due to economies then diseconomies of scale.
  • LMC cuts LAC at its minimum; at each output, LMC equals the SMC of the plant tangent there.
  • Fixed costs do not affect the profit-maximising output (MC = MR) but do affect total profit.
  • Accounting profit = revenue − explicit costs; economic profit = revenue − explicit − implicit (opportunity) costs.
  • Costs can be explicit (paid out) or implicit (opportunity cost of own resources); private cost vs social cost matters when there are externalities.

Worked examples

Example: short-run cost table

Fixed cost ₹100; variable cost for output 1 to 5: 40, 70, 90, 120, 170.

TC: 140, 170, 190, 220, 270. MC: 40, 30, 20, 30, 50.

AVC: 40, 35, 30, 30, 34 — lowest (30) at 3–4 units, where MC also equals 30.

ATC: 140, 85, 63.3, 55, 54 — still falling because MC (50) is below ATC.

AFC: 100, 50, 33.3, 25, 20 — falls continuously.

Example: accounting vs economic profit

A shop's revenue is ₹10 lakh and its explicit costs are ₹6 lakh → accounting profit ₹4 lakh. The owner could earn ₹3 lakh in a job and ₹50,000 interest on the capital invested (implicit costs) → economic profit only ₹50,000.

How to write the answer

Statement → diagram with SAC curves and LAC envelope (or LMC/SMC) → explanation → conclusion.

Common mistakes

  • Drawing LAC through the minimum of every SAC (it touches only the minimum of the optimal plant).
