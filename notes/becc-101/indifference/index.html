Indifference Curve Analysis

Moderate Asked in 8 of 11 papers · avg 8 marks

Indifference curves and budget line, consumer equilibrium, PCC/ICC/Engel curve, corner solutions.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 5 Consumer Behaviour: Ordinal Approach (5.2–5.8)

Key points

  • Indifference curve: bundles giving equal satisfaction; slopes down, convex to origin (diminishing MRS), never intersect, higher is better.
  • Budget line: Px·X + Py·Y = M; slope = −Px/Py.
  • Equilibrium: tangency, MRSxy = Px/Py, and the IC must be convex — otherwise tangency is not optimal.
  • Corner solution: only one good bought (e.g. concave ICs or perfect substitutes).
  • Price consumption curve (PCC) traces equilibria as price changes → demand curve. Income consumption curve (ICC) as income changes → Engel curve.
  • Perfect substitutes → straight-line ICs; perfect complements → L-shaped ICs.
  • The ordinal approach only ranks bundles (first, second…) instead of measuring utility; the indifference map shows the consumer's whole scale of preferences.

Worked example

Example: consumer equilibrium

Income ₹100; Px = ₹10, Py = ₹5. Budget line: 10X + 5Y = 100 (at most 10 X or 20 Y); slope −2.

With U = XY, MRS = Y/X. Equilibrium where MRS = Px/Py → Y/X = 2 → Y = 2X. Then 10X + 10X = 100 → X = 5, Y = 10.

If Px falls to ₹5: Y/X = 1 → X = 10, Y = 10. Joining such points gives the price consumption curve, from which the demand curve for X follows (price 10 → 5 units; price 5 → 10 units).
