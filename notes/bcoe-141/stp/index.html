Segmentation, Targeting & Positioning

Critical Asked in 8 of 9 papers · avg 19 marks

Bases of consumer market segmentation, segmentation vs targeting, targeting strategies, positioning.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 3 Markets and Market Segmentation

Key points

  • Segmentation: dividing a market into groups of buyers with similar needs.
  • Bases: geographic (region, urban/rural, climate), demographic (age, gender, income, occupation, family size), psychographic (lifestyle, personality, values), behavioural (occasion, benefits sought, usage rate, loyalty).
  • Segmentation divides the market; targeting chooses which segments to serve.
  • Targeting strategies: undifferentiated/mass (one offer for all), differentiated (different offers for several segments — Maruti's range), concentrated/niche (one segment — Rolex), micro-marketing (local or individual).
  • Positioning: creating a distinct place for the brand in the customer's mind — by attribute, benefit, price-quality, use, user, competitor (e.g. Volvo = safety).
  • Requirements of effective segments: measurable, accessible, substantial, differentiable, actionable.

Worked example

Example: segmenting, targeting and positioning a toothpaste

Segments: children (demographic), people with sensitive teeth (benefit sought), those who prefer herbal products (psychographic), rural low-income buyers (geographic and income).

Targeting: the company chooses differentiated marketing — a kids' gel, a sensitive-teeth variant and a herbal variant, each with its own ads.

Positioning: the sensitive-teeth variant is positioned on benefit — 'relief from sensitivity in 14 days', recommended by dentists.

How to write the answer

Meaning → bases or strategies as headed points with brand examples → conclusion.
