Channels of Distribution

Critical Asked in 9 of 9 papers · avg 19 marks

Types of channels, functions of channel members and middlemen, factors in channel choice, intensity of distribution, wholesalers.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 4 · Unit 11 Channels of Distribution IBlock 4 · Unit 12 Channels of Distribution IIBlock 4 · Unit 13 Physical Distribution

Key points

  • Channel of distribution: route through which goods pass from producer to consumer.
  • Types: zero-level (direct — Amway, company websites), one-level (producer → retailer → consumer), two-level (producer → wholesaler → retailer → consumer), three-level (with agents).
  • Functions: information, promotion, contact, matching (sorting, grading, packaging), negotiation, physical distribution, financing, risk-taking.
  • Role of middlemen: reduce the number of transactions, provide assortment, storage, credit, market feedback.
  • Factors in channel choice: product (perishability, value, technical nature), market (number and location of buyers, order size), company (resources, control desired), middlemen (availability, cost), environment and competition.
  • Intensity: intensive (convenience goods everywhere — soap), selective (few outlets — electronics), exclusive (one dealer per area — luxury cars).
  • Services of wholesalers: to manufacturers (bulk buying, storage, finance, market information) and to retailers (assortment, credit, quick supply).
  • Types of middlemen: merchant middlemen (wholesalers, retailers — take title) and agent middlemen (brokers, commission agents — do not).
  • Physical distribution moves goods to customers at the right time and place at least total cost. Tasks: order processing, warehousing, inventory control, transportation and information monitoring.

Worked examples

Example: one company, different channels

A biscuit maker sells through a two-level channel (company → wholesaler → retailer → consumer) to reach lakhs of small shops — intensive distribution, because biscuits are convenience goods.

Its premium gift hampers are sold only through its website and a few selected stores — zero-level and selective distribution.

A luxury car maker, by contrast, gives one dealer per city exclusive rights — exclusive distribution.

Example: how middlemen cut the number of contacts

Five manufacturers selling directly to 20 retailers need 5 × 20 = 100 separate contacts. Through one wholesaler they need only 5 + 20 = 25.
