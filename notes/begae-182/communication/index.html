Communication: Process, Functions & Barriers

Critical Asked in 9 of 11 papers · avg 4 marks

Elements and process of communication, its functions, characteristics of good communication, style, and barriers to communication and how to overcome them.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 1 The Process of CommunicationBlock 1 · Unit 2 The Globalization of Communication: A Global Village

Key points

  • Elements: sender, message, encoding, channel, receiver, decoding, feedback, noise and context.
  • Functions: to inform, instruct, warn, persuade, express feelings and build relationships — the hearer works out the meaning from the context.
  • The book's seven macro functions of communication: emotive (feelings — 'Oh no!'), directive (affect others' behaviour — 'Close the door, please'), phatic (open or check the channel — 'Can you hear me?'), poetic (the form itself matters), referential (carry information), metalinguistic (talk about the language itself) and contextual (set up a context — 'Right! Let's start the meeting').
  • Barriers in the book: code (no shared language), vocabulary (e.g. stock-market jargon), concepts, background knowledge and shared assumptions, pronunciation, stress and accent, culture-specific meanings, physical environment (noise, distance) and affective factors (anxiety, attitudes).
  • Globalisation: new communication technology has made the world a 'global village' (McLuhan); each revolution in communication has brought social change.
  • Good communication — the 7 Cs: clear, concise, concrete, correct, coherent, complete, courteous.
  • Overcoming barriers: simple, precise language; the right channel; checking understanding through feedback; active listening; empathy and respect for other cultures.
  • Style: formal or informal register chosen to fit the purpose, audience and situation.

Worked examples

Examples: the seven functions in single sentences

Emotive: 'What a lovely surprise!' Directive: 'Please submit the report by Friday.' Phatic: 'Hello? Can you hear me?' Poetic: 'Rain, rain, go away.' Referential: 'The train leaves at 6.40.' Metalinguistic: 'What does "liquidity" mean?' Contextual: 'Right, let's start the meeting.'

Example: a barrier and how to remove it

A bank officer tells a farmer, 'Your KYC is pending and your account will be frozen.' The farmer does not know 'KYC' (vocabulary barrier) and is anxious (affective barrier). Better: 'We need a copy of your Aadhaar and a photo so your account keeps working. Can you bring them this week?' — then check he has understood.
