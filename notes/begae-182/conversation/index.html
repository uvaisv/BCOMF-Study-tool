Conversation, Speech Events & Discourse

Moderate Asked in 7 of 11 papers · avg 3 marks

Characteristics and conventions of conversation, how it differs from other speech events, introducing oneself, and forms of discourse.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 1 Informal Interpersonal FunctionsBlock 2 · Unit 2 Making Enquiries/Asking Questions

Key points

  • Conversation: an informal, spontaneous, two-way exchange with turn-taking, shared responsibility and no fixed agenda.
  • Conventions: openings and greetings, taking turns, adjacency pairs (question–answer, greeting–greeting), politeness, closings.
  • Speech events: lectures, interviews, meetings, debates, telephone calls, speeches, conversations. A conversation differs because it is unplanned, equal between speakers and has no set purpose, time or order.
  • Introducing yourself: greeting, name, where you are from or your role, one or two personal details, a question back to the other person.
  • Forms of discourse: narration (tells events), description (paints a picture), exposition (explains) and argumentation (persuades). Argumentative discourse gives a claim, reasons and evidence, and answers the counter-argument.
  • Informal interpersonal functions in the book: greeting, introducing, inviting, small talk; making enquiries mixes formal and informal questions.

Worked examples

Model: introducing yourself at a workshop

'Hello, I'm Rahul Menon. I'm doing my B.Com from Thrissur and I work part-time at an accountant's office. I'm here to learn more about GST filing. And you — what brings you here?'

Example: adjacency pairs

Greeting–greeting: 'Good morning.' — 'Good morning.' Question–answer: 'Is this seat free?' — 'Yes, please sit.' Offer–acceptance: 'Would you like some tea?' — 'I'd love some, thanks.' Each first part expects a matching second part, which keeps a conversation moving.

How to write the answer

Meaning → features as headings → a short example exchange → conclusion.
