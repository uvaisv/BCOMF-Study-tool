Verbal & Non-verbal Communication

High Asked in 8 of 11 papers · avg 3 marks

Oral and written modes of verbal communication, non-verbal communication and its advantages, and stress and intonation.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 3 Verbal and Non-Verbal Communication

Key points

  • Verbal communication uses words, spoken (oral) or written.
  • Oral: quick, immediate feedback, personal, uses tone — suited to meetings, interviews, phone calls and presentations. Written: a permanent record, precise, formal, legal proof — suited to letters, reports, contracts, notices and instructions. Both together: a meeting followed by written minutes; an interview followed by an offer letter.
  • Non-verbal communication: body language (gestures, facial expression, eye contact, posture), space (proxemics), touch, use of time, appearance and paralanguage (tone, pitch, pace, pauses).
  • Advantages of non-verbal communication: supports or replaces words, shows feelings and attitudes, crosses language barriers, gives quick feedback, adds credibility.
  • Stress: the extra force on a syllable or word (PHOtograph, phoTOgraphy; stressing a word changes the sense). Intonation: the rise and fall of the voice (a rising tone for yes/no questions, falling for statements).

Worked examples

Example: words and body language

In an interview a candidate says, 'I'm very confident about this role,' while avoiding eye contact, slouching and speaking very softly. The non-verbal signals contradict the words, and the interviewer believes the body language. Sitting upright, meeting their eyes and speaking at a steady pace would support the message.

Examples: stress and intonation

Stress moves with the word: PHOtograph, phoTOgraphy, photoGRAphic.

Stress changes the sense: 'I didn't take the book' (someone else did) vs 'I didn't take the BOOK' (I took something else).

Intonation: 'You're coming?' with a rising tone is a question; 'You're coming.' with a falling tone is a statement.
