Reading Skills & Comprehension

Critical Asked in 10 of 11 papers · avg 5 marks

Levels of reading comprehension, literal, interpretive and critical reading, reading as a cognitive skill, schemata, faulty reading habits, silent reading and subvocalising.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 3 · Unit 1 The Reading Skill

Key points

  • Four levels of comprehension: literal (what the text says), interpretive or inferential (what it means — reading between the lines), critical or evaluative (judging its truth, purpose and bias), creative or applied (using ideas beyond the text).
  • Interpretive reading: working out implied meaning, the writer's attitude and the reasons behind statements. Critical reading: questioning evidence, argument and bias.
  • Reading involves complex cognitive skills: recognising words, knowing vocabulary, predicting, inferring, linking to background knowledge and monitoring your own understanding.
  • Schemata: the background knowledge structures a reader brings; they help the reader predict and make sense of a new text.
  • Faulty reading habits: word-by-word reading, subvocalising (saying words in your head), vocalising, finger-pointing, moving the head, regression (going back again and again).
  • Silent reading is faster and better for understanding; reading aloud helps pronunciation and sharing a text.

Worked example

Example: four levels of comprehension

Text: 'The shop's shutters stayed down all week, and a "To Let" board appeared on Friday.'

Literal: the shop was closed all week and a 'To Let' board was put up.

Interpretive: the shop has probably shut down for good.

Critical: is the writer suggesting that small shops are struggling? Is one shop enough evidence?

Creative: what could the owner have done differently — for example, selling online?

How to write the answer

Meaning → levels or habits as headings with a short example → conclusion.
