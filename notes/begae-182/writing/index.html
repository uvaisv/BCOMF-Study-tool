Dialogue, Essay & Paragraph Writing

Critical Asked in 6 of 11 papers · avg 5 marks

Writing a dialogue in ten turns, a well-structured essay, completing or writing a descriptive paragraph.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 3 · Unit 3 The Writing Skill: Some Basic Guidelines

Key points

  • Dialogue in ten turns: set the scene in one line; label speakers; alternate turns; greet, move to the purpose, close politely. Use natural, polite spoken English (Could you…, I'd like to…, That sounds good).
  • Essay: an introduction that defines the topic and states your view; three or four body paragraphs, each with a topic sentence and an example; a conclusion that sums up and looks ahead.
  • Paragraph completion: continue the idea of the given opening; one clear line of thought; use linking words (on the one hand, also, however, not only… but also, ultimately).
  • Descriptive paragraph (e.g. your city for a travel magazine): location, layout, landmarks, history, food and culture, best time to visit — vivid but factual.
  • Common essay topics: education and online learning, technology, climate change, globalisation, tourism, careers for young Indians.

Worked examples

Model: a ten-turn dialogue — returning a faulty phone

Customer: Good morning. I bought this phone here last week, and it keeps switching off.

Assistant: I'm sorry to hear that. May I see the bill, please?

Customer: Here it is.

Assistant: Thank you. Did it fall or get wet?

Customer: No, it has been in its cover all the time.

Assistant: Then it is covered by the warranty. Would you like a repair or a replacement?

Customer: I'd prefer a replacement, if possible.

Assistant: Certainly. We have the same model in stock. I'll just transfer your data.

Customer: That's very kind of you. How long will it take?

Assistant: About twenty minutes. Please have a seat.

Model: completing a paragraph

Given: 'Tourism brings money to a region, but…'

Continuation: '…it also brings problems if it is not planned. On the one hand, hotels, guides and local crafts gain income. On the other hand, crowds, plastic waste and rising prices can harm the very places visitors come to see. Ultimately, tourism helps most when local people share its benefits and limits are set to protect the environment.'

How to write the answer

Plan for 2 minutes: list your points, then write in paragraphs with linking words.
