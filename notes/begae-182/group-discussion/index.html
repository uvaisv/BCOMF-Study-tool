Group Discussion

Critical Asked in 10 of 11 papers · avg 8 marks

Writing out a group discussion among five students, and the essentials and rules of a good group discussion.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 3 Group Discussions and Meetings (Group discussions)

Key points

  • Format: name the speakers (A–E, or names); one opens by defining the topic; each speaker gives a point with a reason or example; speakers respond to each other; one summarises at the end.
  • Cover both sides of the topic: at least two points for and two against, then a balanced conclusion.
  • Useful phrases: 'I'd like to begin by…', 'I agree with you, and I'd add…', 'I see your point, but…', 'Could we also consider…', 'To sum up…'.
  • Essentials and rules: listen actively; stay on the topic; give everyone a chance and don't interrupt; disagree politely with reasons; support points with facts; don't dominate; keep to time; summarise.
  • Give each speaker at least three turns when the question asks for them.

Worked example

Model: a short group discussion — 'Should online classes replace classrooms?'

A: I'd like to begin by defining the question: are online classes good enough to replace classroom teaching completely?

B: I think they help working students — they can study at night and replay lectures.

C: I see your point, but many students in villages still lack a steady internet connection.

D: I agree with C, and I'd add that labs and group work need physical presence.

B: That's true, but blended learning could give the best of both.

E: To sum up, online classes widen access and save time, but they cannot fully replace classrooms; a blended model seems the most practical.

How to write the answer

Speaker names + opening → points for and against in turns → summary. Aim for 15–20 turns.

Common mistakes

  • Writing an essay with names in front — a GD needs people responding to each other.
