Capacity to Contract (Minor, Unsound Mind)

High Asked in 7 of 11 papers · avg 9 marks

Competent parties, minor's agreements, necessaries, persons of unsound mind, pardanashin women.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 3 Capacity of Parties

Key points

  • Competent (Sec 11): of the age of majority, of sound mind, not disqualified by law.
  • Minor's agreement is void ab initio (Mohori Bibee v Dharmodas Ghose); no ratification on attaining majority; no estoppel; minor can be a promisee or beneficiary and can be an agent.
  • Necessaries (Sec 68): supplier of necessaries suited to the minor's condition in life can recover from the minor's property, not personally.
  • Unsound mind (Sec 12): cannot understand the contract and judge its effect on his interests; a person usually of unsound mind can contract during lucid intervals.
  • Disqualified: alien enemies, foreign sovereigns, convicts, insolvents.
  • Pardanashin woman: undue influence is presumed; the other party must prove she understood the contract.

Worked examples

Example: minor's agreement (Mohori Bibee v Dharmodas Ghose)

A minor mortgaged his house to a moneylender for a loan. The Privy Council held the mortgage void from the start, and the lender could not even get back the money he had advanced.

Example: necessaries

A 17-year-old college student buys textbooks worth ₹3,000 on credit. The bookseller can recover the price from the student's property, but the student is not personally liable. A gold watch bought on credit would not be a necessary for a student.

Example: sound mind

A patient in a mental hospital who is at intervals of sound mind may make a contract during those lucid intervals. A sane person who is delirious with fever, or drunk, cannot make a contract while unable to understand it.
