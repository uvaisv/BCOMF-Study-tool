Partnership & LLP

High Asked in 11 of 11 papers · avg 17 marks

Partnership and its tests, kinds of partners, registration, rights of partners, dissolution, LLP.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 4 · Unit 12 Definition and Registration of PartnershipBlock 4 · Unit 13 Rights, Duties and Liabilities of PartnersBlock 4 · Unit 14 Dissolution of Partnership FirmBlock 4 · Unit 15 Limited Liability Partnership

Key points

  • Partnership (Sec 4, 1932 Act): relation between persons who agree to share profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all. Mutual agency is the true test; profit sharing alone is not conclusive.
  • Kinds of partners: active, sleeping, nominal, partner by estoppel/holding out, minor admitted to benefits, secret, sub-partner, incoming and outgoing partners.
  • Registration (Sec 58–59): application to the Registrar with firm name, place, partners' names and addresses, date of joining, duration. Non-registration: firm cannot sue third parties or partners; partners cannot sue the firm — third parties can still sue the firm.
  • Implied authority of a partner: acts in the usual course of business (buy and sell goods, receive payments, engage employees) — not to submit to arbitration, compromise claims, open a bank account in own name.
  • Dissolution of partnership (change in relations; firm may continue) vs dissolution of firm (business ends). Modes: by agreement, compulsory, on contingencies, by notice, by court.
  • LLP (LLP Act 2008): body corporate with separate legal entity, perpetual succession, limited liability, minimum 2 partners, 2 designated partners. Differs from a partnership (liability, entity) and from a company (flexible internal management, no share capital).
  • Partnership differs from co-ownership (no business or mutual agency needed) and from a joint Hindu family business (arises by birth, the karta manages, no agreement).
  • On dissolution, accounts are settled in order: losses (including capital deficiencies) are paid first out of profits, then capital, then partners personally; assets go to third-party debts, then partners' advances, then capital, and any surplus is shared in the profit ratio (Sec 48).

Worked examples

Example: implied authority

A, B and C run a textile firm. A buys cloth on credit for the firm in the usual way → the firm is bound, even if B and C did not know.

A signs an agreement referring a firm dispute to arbitration, or opens a bank account in his own name for the firm → outside implied authority; the firm is not bound unless the others agree or usage allows it.

Example: settling accounts on dissolution (Section 48)

Assets realise ₹5,00,000. Outside creditors ₹3,00,000; a loan from partner A ₹50,000; capitals A ₹1,00,000 and B ₹1,00,000; profits shared equally.

Pay creditors 3,00,000 → left 2,00,000. Repay A's loan 50,000 → left 1,50,000. Capitals total 2,00,000, so the loss is 50,000, borne 25,000 each.

A and B each get 1,00,000 − 25,000 = ₹75,000. Check: 3,00,000 + 50,000 + 75,000 + 75,000 = 5,00,000.
