Consideration & Privity

Moderate Asked in 9 of 11 papers · avg 10 marks

Consideration and its legal rules, 'no consideration no contract' and its exceptions, stranger to a contract.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 5 Consideration and Legality of Object (5.2–5.6)

Key points

  • Consideration (Sec 2(d)): at the desire of the promisor, the promisee or any other person has done or abstained, or does or promises to do or abstain.
  • Rules: at the desire of the promisor; may move from the promisee or any other person; past, present or future; need not be adequate but must have some value in law; must be real and lawful; not an existing legal duty.
  • No consideration, no contract (Sec 25) — exceptions: written registered agreement made out of natural love and affection, compensation for past voluntary service, promise to pay a time-barred debt (written and signed), completed gifts, agency.
  • Privity: a stranger to a contract cannot sue (Tweddle v Atkinson) — exceptions: trust or charge, marriage settlements, acknowledgement or estoppel, assignment, covenants running with land, agency.

Worked examples

Example: consideration from a third person (Chinnaya v Ramayya)

An old woman gifted her property to her daughter, on condition that the daughter pay a yearly sum to the old woman's brother; the daughter promised him so. She later refused to pay. The brother could sue: consideration moved from the old woman, and in India consideration may come from 'any other person'.

Example: inadequate consideration, and the exceptions

A freely agrees to sell a horse worth ₹10,000 for ₹1,000 → a valid contract; inadequacy alone does not make it void, but it may be evidence that consent was not free.

A signs a written promise to pay a time-barred debt of ₹20,000 → valid without fresh consideration (Section 25(3)).

Example: privity (Tweddle v Atkinson)

The fathers of a bride and groom agreed with each other to pay the groom a sum; the groom sued when one of them did not pay. He failed — he was not a party to the contract.
