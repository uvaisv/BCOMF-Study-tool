Bailment, Pledge & Finder of Goods

High Asked in 10 of 11 papers · avg 17 marks

Bailment and its kinds, rights and duties of bailee, lien, finder of goods, pledge and pledge by non-owners.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 3 · Unit 10 Bailment and Pledge

Key points

  • Bailment (Sec 148): delivery of goods for a purpose on the contract that they will be returned or disposed of as directed. Essentials: delivery of goods (movable), for a purpose, return of the same goods.
  • Kinds: gratuitous or non-gratuitous; for the benefit of bailor, bailee or both.
  • Duties of bailee: reasonable care, not use goods for unauthorised purposes, not mix goods, return goods and any increase. Rights: compensation, reimbursement of expenses, lien, sue third parties.
  • Lien: right to retain goods until dues are paid. Particular lien (goods on which labour was spent) vs general lien (bankers, factors, wharfingers, attorneys, policy brokers for any balance).
  • Finder of goods: has the rights and duties of a bailee; may retain goods until compensated; may sell if goods are perishable or charges reach two-thirds of their value.
  • Pledge (Sec 172): bailment of goods as security for a debt. Pledge by non-owners is valid in some cases: mercantile agent, person with a voidable title, seller or buyer in possession, co-owner in possession.

Worked examples

Example: bailment and lien

You give cloth to a tailor to stitch a suit — a bailment for the benefit of both. The tailor must take reasonable care of it and return the stitched suit. If you do not pay the stitching charges, the tailor may keep the suit until you pay — a particular lien on the goods on which labour was spent.

Example: finder of goods

A finds a gold chain in a bus, advertises it and spends ₹500 on the advertisement. A must take care of it like a bailee and may keep it until the owner pays that expense. A may sell it only if the owner cannot be found with reasonable care or refuses to pay, and the chain is in danger of perishing or losing most of its value, or the lawful charges reach two-thirds of its value.

Example: pledge

A farmer pawns gold ornaments with a bank for a ₹50,000 loan. This is a pledge — a bailment as security for a debt. If the loan is not repaid, the bank may sue on the debt and keep the gold as security, or sell it after reasonable notice.

How to write the answer

Definition with section → essentials → rights and duties in two lists → conclusion.
