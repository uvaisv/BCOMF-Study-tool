Sale of Goods Act

Critical Asked in 11 of 11 papers · avg 27 marks

Contract of sale, goods, conditions and warranties, caveat emptor, transfer of ownership, delivery, sale by non-owners, unpaid seller.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 5 · Unit 16 Nature of Contract of SaleBlock 5 · Unit 17 Conditions and WarrantiesBlock 5 · Unit 18 Transfer of Ownership and DeliveryBlock 5 · Unit 19 Rights of an Unpaid Seller

Key points

  • Contract of sale (Sec 4): seller transfers or agrees to transfer property in goods to the buyer for a price. Sale (property passes now) vs agreement to sell (passes later/on conditions); risk follows ownership.
  • Goods: every kind of movable property except actionable claims and money. Types: existing (specific, ascertained, unascertained), future, contingent.
  • Condition: stipulation essential to the main purpose — breach allows rejection. Warranty: collateral — breach allows only damages.
  • Implied conditions: title, sale by description, sample, fitness for purpose, merchantable quality, wholesomeness. Implied warranties: quiet possession, freedom from encumbrance.
  • Caveat emptor (let the buyer beware); exceptions: fitness for purpose relied on seller's skill, merchantable quality, sale by sample/description, usage of trade, fraud.
  • Transfer of ownership: specific goods in deliverable state — at contract; unascertained goods — on appropriation; sale or return — on approval.
  • Delivery: actual, symbolic or constructive; rules on place, time, expenses, short or excess delivery, instalments.
  • Sale by non-owners: generally no better title (nemo dat), except mercantile agent, joint owner, voidable contract, seller/buyer in possession, estoppel.
  • Unpaid seller: not fully paid. Rights against goods: lien, stoppage in transit, resale. Against buyer: suit for price, damages. Lien is lost on delivery; stoppage in transit applies while goods are with a carrier and the buyer is insolvent.
  • Sale vs hire-purchase: in a sale ownership passes at once or on the agreed date; in hire-purchase the hirer becomes owner only after the last instalment and may return the goods before that.
  • Destruction of goods: if specific goods perish without the seller's knowledge before the contract, the agreement is void (Sec 7); if they perish before risk passes, without fault of either party, an agreement to sell becomes void (Sec 8).

Worked examples

Example: condition or warranty?

A buyer tells a dealer he needs a pump that can lift water to a second-floor tank, and relies on the dealer's choice. The pump cannot do it → breach of the implied condition of fitness for purpose: he may reject it and get his money back.

The dealer promised a free cover with the pump but did not send it → breach of warranty: the buyer keeps the pump and claims only damages.

Example: rights of an unpaid seller

A seller in Kochi sells 100 bags of rice on credit to a buyer in Chennai and sends them by rail.

While the goods are still with the railway, the buyer becomes insolvent → the seller can stop the goods in transit and take them back.

Had the goods still been in the seller's godown and unpaid for, the seller could keep them (lien). If the buyer does not pay after notice, or the goods are perishable, the seller may resell them and claim any loss.

Example: risk follows ownership

A buyer picks out a specific sofa in a showroom and agrees the price; it is ready for delivery and is to be collected next week. Ownership passes at once when the contract is made. If the showroom burns down that night without the seller's fault, the loss falls on the buyer, who still owes the price — unless they agreed otherwise.

How to write the answer

Definition with section → rules/exceptions as headed points with an illustration → conclusion.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing up lien (goods still with seller) and stoppage in transit (goods with carrier).
