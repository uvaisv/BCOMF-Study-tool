Free Consent (Coercion, Undue Influence, Fraud)

High Asked in 9 of 11 papers · avg 10 marks

Consent and free consent, coercion, undue influence, fraud, misrepresentation, mistake.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 4 Free Consent

Key points

  • Consent (Sec 13): agreeing on the same thing in the same sense. Free consent (Sec 14): not caused by coercion, undue influence, fraud, misrepresentation or mistake.
  • Coercion (Sec 15): committing or threatening an act forbidden by the IPC, or unlawful detention of property, to make a person agree.
  • Undue influence (Sec 16): a person in a position to dominate another's will uses it to get an unfair advantage (doctor–patient, guardian–ward). Pardanashin women get special protection.
  • Coercion vs undue influence: physical threat vs mental pressure; any person vs a party in a relationship; criminal act vs not criminal.
  • Fraud (Sec 17): false statement knowingly made, active concealment, a promise made without intention to perform — with intent to deceive and the other party actually deceived and suffering loss. Mere silence is not fraud unless there is a duty to speak or silence is itself deceptive.
  • Misrepresentation (Sec 18): false statement made innocently. Effect: contract voidable at the option of the aggrieved party; fraud also allows damages.
  • Mistake: of law or of fact. Mistake of Indian law is no excuse — the contract stays valid (Sec 21); mistake of foreign law is treated as a mistake of fact. A bilateral mistake of an essential fact makes the agreement void (Sec 20); a unilateral mistake generally does not (Sec 22).

Worked examples

Example: coercion

A widow's relatives would not let her husband's body be taken for cremation until she adopted a boy they chose (Ranganayakamma v Alwar Setti). The adoption was set aside — her consent was caused by coercion, so it was voidable at her option.

Example: fraud, misrepresentation or silence?

A seller knows his horse is unsound and says it is sound → fraud: the buyer may rescind and claim damages.

A seller honestly but wrongly believes his car's engine was rebuilt and says so → misrepresentation: the buyer may rescind, but gets no damages for fraud.

A seller says nothing about a defect the buyer never asked about → not fraud, unless there was a duty to speak (for example a relation of trust between them).

Example: mistake

A agrees to buy B's horse; unknown to both, it had died the day before. Both were mistaken about a fact essential to the agreement, so it is void (Section 20).
