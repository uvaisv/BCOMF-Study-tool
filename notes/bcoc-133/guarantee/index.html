Indemnity & Guarantee

Moderate Asked in 8 of 11 papers · avg 12 marks

Contract of indemnity vs guarantee, surety's rights against the debtor, creditor and co-sureties, continuing guarantee, discharge of surety.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 3 · Unit 9 Indemnity and Guarantee

Key points

  • Indemnity (Sec 124): one party promises to save the other from loss — two parties, primary liability.
  • Guarantee (Sec 126): to perform the promise or discharge the liability of a third person on default — three parties (creditor, principal debtor, surety); surety's liability is secondary and co-extensive.
  • Rights of surety: against principal debtor — subrogation, indemnity; against creditor — securities held by the creditor; against co-sureties — contribution in equal shares.
  • Continuing guarantee (Sec 129): extends to a series of transactions; revoked by notice (future transactions) or by the surety's death.
  • Surety discharged by: variance in terms without consent, release or discharge of the principal debtor, creditor compounding or giving time, creditor's act impairing surety's remedy, loss of security, revocation.

Worked examples

Example: indemnity or guarantee?

A says to a shopkeeper, 'Give B goods on credit; if B does not pay, I will.' Three parties, and A's liability arises only on B's default → a guarantee; A is the surety.

A asks B to file a suit on A's behalf and promises to make good any costs B suffers → an indemnity: two parties, and A is primarily liable.

Example: rights and discharge of a surety

A bank lends ₹90,000 to D; three friends are co-sureties. D defaults and one surety pays the whole amount. He can recover ₹30,000 from each of the other two (equal shares), and claim the full ₹90,000 from D, taking over any security the bank held (subrogation).

If the bank had given D extra time to repay without the sureties' consent, the sureties would have been discharged (Section 135).

Example: continuing guarantee

A guarantees payment for groceries a shop supplies to B every month, up to ₹20,000. It covers a series of transactions, so it is a continuing guarantee. A can end it for future supplies by notice to the shop.

How to write the answer

Definitions → comparison table (indemnity vs guarantee) → rights under three headings → conclusion.
