Agency

High Asked in 11 of 11 papers · avg 14 marks

Agent and principal, creation of agency (incl. ratification), irrevocable agency, rights and duties of agent, sub-agent, termination.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 3 · Unit 11 Contract of Agency

Key points

  • Agent (Sec 182): person employed to do any act for another or represent another; principal: the person represented. Any person may be an agent — even a minor (the principal is liable, not the minor).
  • Creation: express, implied (e.g. a wife living with her husband can pledge his credit for necessaries; not if living separately without allowance), by necessity, by estoppel, by ratification.
  • Ratification: principal later adopts an act done without authority. Requisites: agent acted for the principal, principal existed and was competent then and now, full knowledge, whole act ratified, within reasonable time, act must be lawful.
  • Irrevocable agency: agency coupled with interest; or after the agent has partly exercised authority incurring liability.
  • Duties of agent: follow instructions/custom, reasonable skill, render accounts, not deal on own account, pay sums received, not delegate. Rights: remuneration, retainer, lien, indemnity, compensation for injury.
  • Sub-agent: appointed by the agent — agent is responsible; substituted agent: named by the agent on the principal's authority — directly responsible to the principal.
  • Classification: general, special, universal; mercantile agents (factor, broker, auctioneer, del credere agent).
  • Termination: by act of parties (revocation, renunciation), by operation of law (completion, expiry, death, insanity, insolvency, destruction of subject matter).

Worked examples

Example: ratification

Without authority, B buys a lorry-load of sugar for A. A later learns all the facts and accepts the deal. The purchase binds A as if B had authority from the start. A cannot accept the sugar and reject the payment terms — the whole act must be ratified.

Example: sub-agent or substituted agent?

A tells his agent B to sell his estate by auction and to employ an auctioneer. B names C as auctioneer. C is a substituted agent — responsible directly to A — because B was authorised to appoint him. Had B hired a helper on his own, the helper would be a sub-agent, and B would answer to A for the helper's work.

Example: agency that cannot be revoked

A owes B money and authorises B to sell A's land and repay himself from the price. The agency is coupled with B's interest, so A cannot revoke it, and it does not end on A's death.
