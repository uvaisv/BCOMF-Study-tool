Offer & Acceptance

High Asked in 10 of 11 papers · avg 13 marks

Offer and its legal rules, types of offer, invitation to offer, acceptance, communication, revocation and lapse.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 2 Offer and Acceptance

Key points

  • Offer/proposal (Sec 2(a)): willingness to do or abstain, to obtain the other's assent.
  • Rules: must intend legal relations, terms certain, communicated, not a mere statement of intention, not an invitation to offer, may be conditional, cannot make silence acceptance.
  • Types: express, implied, general (to the public — Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co.), specific, cross offers (identical offers crossing — no contract until one accepts), standing/continuing offer, counter-offer (rejects the original).
  • Invitation to offer: price lists, goods on display, advertisements, tenders — not offers.
  • Acceptance (Sec 2(b)): absolute and unqualified, communicated in the prescribed or usual manner, by the offeree, while the offer is open.
  • Communication: offer complete when it reaches the offeree; acceptance complete against the proposer when posted, against the acceptor when it reaches the proposer.
  • Revocation: offer can be revoked before acceptance is complete against the proposer; acceptance before it reaches the proposer.
  • Lapse: after the time fixed or reasonable time, death or insanity of offeror, rejection or counter-offer, failure of a condition.

Worked examples

Example: general offer (Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co.)

A company advertised a reward to anyone who used its smoke ball as directed and still caught influenza. Mrs Carlill did so and fell ill. She could claim: a general offer to the public is accepted by anyone who performs its conditions, without telling the offeror first.

Example: acceptance by post

A in Delhi posts an offer on 1 June; it reaches B in Kochi on 3 June — the offer is complete then. B posts an acceptance on 4 June; it reaches A on 6 June.

The acceptance is complete against A on 4 June (when posted) and against B on 6 June (when it reaches A). A may revoke the offer before 4 June; B may revoke the acceptance before 6 June, by a quicker means such as a phone call.

Example: counter-offer ends the offer

A offers to sell land for ₹10 lakh. B replies, 'I will pay ₹9.5 lakh.' A refuses. B then says, 'All right, I accept ₹10 lakh.' There is no contract — the counter-offer rejected the original offer, which is no longer open to accept.
