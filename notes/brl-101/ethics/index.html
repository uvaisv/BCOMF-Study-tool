Ethics in Retailing

Critical Asked in 7 of 8 papers · avg 16 marks

Business ethics in retail, ethical and non-ethical behaviour, benefits of managing ethics, the carry-bag charge issue.

In your IGNOU bookUnit 11 Ethics in Retailing

Key points

  • Business ethics: moral principles guiding retail decisions.
  • Areas: with customers (honest pricing, fair weights, no misleading ads), suppliers (fair terms, timely payment), employees (fair wages, safety), competitors (fair competition), society and environment.
  • Non-ethical behaviour: misleading discounts, selling expired goods, hidden charges, bait-and-switch, poor treatment of staff, data misuse.
  • Benefits of managing ethics: customer trust and loyalty, reputation, employee morale, fewer legal problems, long-term profit.
  • Carry-bag charges: consumer forums have held that charging for branded carry bags without prior notice is an unfair trade practice — retailers should inform customers, offer free or eco-friendly bags, or allow customers to bring their own.
  • Ethical grey areas the book discusses: the carry-bag charge (settled in the courts), frisking customers at the exit, giving sweets instead of exact change, pushing impulse buys, and promoting offers that are already 'sold out'. Shoplifting shows the retailer is not always the one at fault.

Worked example

Example: ethical and unethical practices

Unethical: raising the 'MRP' before announcing a 50% discount; selling goods near expiry without disclosure; charging for a branded carry bag without telling the customer first (held an unfair trade practice by consumer forums). Ethical: clear prices, honest weights, prompt payment to small suppliers, and fair wages and safety for staff — building trust that brings customers back.

How to write the answer

Meaning → areas or behaviours as headed points with examples → benefits → conclusion.
