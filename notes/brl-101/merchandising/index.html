Merchandising & Visual Merchandising

Moderate Asked in 4 of 8 papers · avg 11 marks

Merchandising objectives and budget, merchandise mix, cross merchandising, visual merchandising, private vs national brands.

In your IGNOU bookUnit 4 Sourcing and MerchandisingUnit 5 Store Design and Visual Merchandising

Key points

  • Merchandising: planning and managing the right product, at the right place, time, quantity and price.
  • Objectives: meet customer demand, maximise sales and margin, optimise stock, support store image.
  • Merchandise budget: planned sales, stock levels, reductions (markdowns, shrinkage), purchases, gross margin; constrained by funds, space, supplier terms, season.
  • Merchandise mix: width (number of categories), depth (items per category), consistency. Factors: target customers, store format, competition, space, season, margin.
  • Cross merchandising: displaying related products together (pasta with sauce) to raise basket size.
  • Visual merchandising: presenting products attractively — window displays, fixtures, lighting, colour, signage, mannequins, planograms.
  • Private brands (retailer's own, e.g. DMart's Premia) vs national brands (manufacturer's): higher margin and control vs strong pull and trust.

Worked example

Example: a merchandise budget

Planned sales for October ₹10 lakh; planned reductions (markdowns and shrinkage) ₹1 lakh; desired closing stock ₹6 lakh; opening stock ₹5 lakh.

Planned purchases = 10 + 1 + 6 − 5 = ₹12 lakh.

Cross merchandising: pasta displayed with sauce and cheese raises the average bill; visual merchandising puts winter wear on mannequins at the entrance in November.
