Retail Formats (Store, Non-store, Franchise, E-tailing)

Critical Asked in 7 of 8 papers · avg 22 marks

Classification of formats, modern vs traditional, non-store retailing, franchising, e-tailing, cash and carry.

In your IGNOU bookUnit 3 Retail FormatsUnit 9 Rural Retailing and E-Tailing (E-tailing and franchising)Unit 10 Emerging Trends and Careers in Retail Industry (Cash and carry)

Key points

  • Traditional formats: kirana/mom-and-pop stores, haats and melas, street vendors, general stores.
  • Modern store formats: department store (Shoppers Stop), supermarket, hypermarket (Big Bazaar), convenience store, speciality store (Tanishq), discount store (DMart), category killer, factory outlet, malls, chain stores.
  • Modern vs traditional: organised, large, self-service, wide assortment, technology, standard prices vs small, owner-run, credit and personal service.
  • Non-store retailing: direct selling (Amway), catalogue shops, telemarketing, vending machines, TV shopping, e-tailing.
  • Cash and carry: wholesale self-service for registered businesses (Metro, Walmart Best Price).
  • Brick and mortar: physical stores; click and mortar: both physical and online.
  • Franchising: franchisor gives the brand and system, franchisee pays a fee/royalty. Types: product/trade-name, business-format, manufacturer–retailer. Merits: fast expansion, low capital for franchisor, proven model for franchisee. Limits: less control, royalty cost, dependence.
  • E-tailing: selling online (Amazon, Flipkart). Advantages: 24×7, wide reach, low cost, convenience. Limitations: no touch and feel, delivery and return issues, security concerns.
  • Choice of format follows location: department stores suit scarce central city space, hypermarkets need large plots on the outskirts, neighbourhoods suit food and daily-needs stores, high streets suit brand stores, and malls offer everything under one roof.
  • Emerging trends in the book: cash-and-carry, growth through mergers and acquisitions, and the rise of private brands changing the manufacturer–retailer relationship.

Worked example

Examples: matching formats

The kirana shop on the corner (traditional, credit and personal service); a supermarket with self-service aisles; a hypermarket on the highway with groceries plus electronics; a jewellery speciality store; a discount store with everyday low prices; Amway distributors (direct selling, non-store); a vending machine at a station; a cash-and-carry wholesale store that only serves registered businesses.

How to write the answer

Meaning → classification chart (store / non-store) → each format with an Indian example → merits → conclusion.
