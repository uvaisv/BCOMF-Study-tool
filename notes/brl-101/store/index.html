Store Location, Layout, Display & Space

Critical Asked in 8 of 8 papers · avg 18 marks

Store location factors, layouts, display, atmosphere, internal/external environment, space management and performance measures.

In your IGNOU bookUnit 5 Store Design and Visual Merchandising

Key points

  • Location factors: target population and income, footfall and accessibility, competition, parking, rent and cost, visibility, zoning laws.
  • Layouts: grid (supermarkets), free-form (boutiques), racetrack/loop (department stores), spine, herringbone.
  • Display: window display, interior display, point-of-purchase displays, fixtures, signage, lighting.
  • Store atmosphere: lighting, colour, music, scent, temperature, cleanliness — shapes mood and time spent.
  • Internal environment: layout, display, ambience, staff. External: storefront, signage, parking, surroundings.
  • Space performance measures: sales per square foot, gross margin per square foot, GMROF, sales per linear foot of shelf, space productivity index.
  • Store space management: allocating space to categories by their sales and profit contribution.
  • Atmospherics has four parts in the book: exterior atmospherics (entrance, signboard, window display, parking), interior atmospherics (flooring, design, cleanliness), store layout (free-flow, grid, boutique) and visual merchandising.

Worked example

Example: layout and display decisions

A supermarket uses a grid layout with milk and bread at the back, so shoppers pass other aisles. A boutique uses a free-form layout to encourage browsing. A department store uses a racetrack loop leading past every department.

Impulse items such as chocolates sit at the billing counter (point-of-purchase display); soft lighting, music and a pleasant scent make customers stay longer.

How to write the answer

Meaning → factors or types with a small layout sketch → examples → conclusion.
