Logistics Management Cycle

Critical Asked in 7 of 9 papers · avg 7 marks

The logistics management cycle and how its key activities are interrelated.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 4 Logistics Management Cycle

Key points

  • Logistics management cycle: the repeating set of activities that keeps goods moving to users — serving customers, product selection, forecasting or quantification of needs, procurement, inventory management (storage and distribution) and use, with reverse logistics at the end.
  • At the centre sits the logistics management information system (LMIS), which links every activity.
  • Management support surrounds the cycle: organisation and staffing, budget, supervision, monitoring and evaluation, and quality assurance, all within government policy.
  • Why the activities are interrelated: each activity's output is the next one's input — wrong forecasts lead to wrong procurement, which causes stock-outs or overstock; poor storage damages goods; data from use feeds the next forecast.
  • A failure at any point breaks the whole chain, so the cycle is managed as one system rather than separate departments.

Worked example

Example: one weak link breaks the cycle

A district health store under-forecasts demand for ORS packets before the monsoon. Procurement buys too little; stock runs out in clinics just as diarrhoea cases rise. Even good storage and transport cannot fix a wrong forecast — which is why the cycle (selection, forecasting, procurement, storage, distribution, use) is managed as one system, linked by its information system.

How to write the answer

Draw the cycle as a circle with LMIS in the middle → explain each activity in two lines → show two links between activities → conclusion.

Common mistakes

  • Explaining the activities one by one without showing how they connect — the question asks why they are interrelated.
