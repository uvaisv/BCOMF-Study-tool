Green Logistics & the Environment

Critical Asked in 9 of 9 papers · avg 11 marks

Green logistics: meaning, strategies and protocols, challenges, environmental impacts of logistics, regulatory issues, pollution and environmental management systems.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 3 · Unit 11 Green Logistics

Key points

  • Green logistics: planning and running logistics so that harm to the environment (emissions, pollution, noise, waste, congestion) is as low as possible while service and cost stay acceptable.
  • Environmental impacts: greenhouse-gas emissions from fuel, air pollution (NOx, particulates), water pollution (oil and chemical spills, ballast water), noise, packaging waste, land used by warehouses and roads, congestion and accidents.
  • Strategies: shift freight to rail and water; consolidate loads and optimise routes; cleaner fuels and electric vehicles; eco-driving; green warehouses (solar power, LED lighting, better insulation); less and reusable packaging; reverse logistics and recycling; shared or collaborative logistics; measuring and offsetting carbon; green procurement.
  • Protocols for implementing it: a written environmental policy with targets; measuring the carbon footprint (GHG Protocol); an environmental management system such as ISO 14001; standards for suppliers and carriers; staff training; monitoring and public reporting; compliance with law.
  • Challenges: higher upfront cost, missing infrastructure (charging points, rail sidings), trade-offs with speed and cost, difficulty measuring emissions, low awareness, a fragmented transport industry, weak enforcement.
  • Regulatory and environmental issues: vehicle emission norms (Bharat Stage VI), vehicle scrappage policy, rules for hazardous goods, Plastic Waste Management Rules and extended producer responsibility, e-waste rules, pollution control boards, workplace safety.
  • Environmental management system (EMS): a framework (ISO 14001) to manage environmental effects — environmental policy, planning, implementation, checking and corrective action, management review (plan–do–check–act).

Worked example

Example: greening a distribution network

A FMCG company moves long-haul freight from trucks to rail, consolidates half-empty loads into full ones, switches city delivery to electric vans, and puts solar panels and LED lights on its warehouse roof. It measures its carbon footprint each year using the GHG Protocol and sets a target to cut emissions per tonne-km by 20% in five years.

How to write the answer

Meaning → headed points (strategies or challenges) with one example each → conclusion on balancing economy and environment.
