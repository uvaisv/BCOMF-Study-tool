Procurement & Inventory Control

Critical Asked in 7 of 9 papers · avg 7 marks

Methods of procurement, the procurement process and cycle, the conceptual framework and techniques of inventory control, VED analysis and automation.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 5 Procurement of Material and Inventory Control

Key points

  • Procurement: getting goods and services of the right quality and quantity, at the right time and price, from the right source.
  • Methods: open (public) tender, limited tender, single-source or proprietary purchase, spot purchase, rate contract, negotiation, reverse auction, e-procurement (the Government e-Marketplace, GeM, in India).
  • Procurement process and cycle: identify the need → write specifications → find and shortlist suppliers → invite quotations or tenders → evaluate and negotiate → place the purchase order or contract → follow up → receive and inspect → approve the invoice and pay → keep records and rate suppliers.
  • Inventory control — conceptual framework: stock is held as a buffer against uncertain demand and supply, but it ties up money and space. Balance three costs: ordering, carrying and stock-out. Key decisions: how much to order (EOQ = √(2DS/H)), when to order (reorder level = lead-time demand + safety stock) and how much safety stock to keep.
  • Techniques: ABC (by value), VED — Vital, Essential, Desirable (by how critical the item is; used for spare parts and medicines), FSN (fast, slow, non-moving), HML (high, medium, low price), SDE (scarce, difficult, easy to obtain), EOQ, JIT, minimum–maximum levels, the two-bin system and perpetual inventory.
  • Automation: e-procurement portals, barcodes and RFID, ERP inventory modules with automatic reorder, vendor-managed inventory.
  • Inventory control techniques described in the book: ABC analysis, economic order quantity, materials requirements planning (MRP) and VED analysis.

Worked example

Example: ABC and VED in a hospital store

ABC by value: 10% of items (cancer drugs, stents) take 70% of spending → strict control and frequent review (A). Cotton and gloves are many items of low value (C) → simple, bulk ordering.

VED by criticality: oxygen and anaesthesia drugs are Vital — they must never run out, even if cheap; a particular brand of soap is Desirable. Combining both, a cheap but vital drug gets high safety stock.

How to write the answer

Meaning → steps as a flow chart or methods as headings → one technique explained with an example → conclusion.
