Supply Chain Management

Critical Asked in 5 of 9 papers · avg 6 marks

Supply chain management, its challenges, supply chain management tools and their applications, and business process planning.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 3 Logistics and Supply Chain Management: Inter-relationshipBlock 3 · Unit 13 Effective Logistics Management: Challenges (13.5–13.6)

Key points

  • Supply chain management (SCM): managing the flow of goods, information and money from suppliers through manufacturers, distributors and retailers to the final customer, so the whole chain works as one.
  • Logistics vs SCM: logistics is the movement and storage part; SCM also covers sourcing, production planning, partner coordination and customer demand.
  • Challenges: uncertain demand and the bullwhip effect, supply disruptions (pandemics, conflicts, natural disasters), rising costs, poor visibility and data sharing, weak coordination between partners, too much or too little inventory, infrastructure gaps, sustainability demands, cyber risks, shortage of skilled staff.
  • Tools and application areas: ERP, supply chain planning software, WMS and TMS, barcodes and RFID, EDI, GPS tracking, demand forecasting and analytics, vendor-managed inventory, blockchain. They are applied in procurement, demand planning, inventory, order management, transport, warehousing and supplier and customer relationships.
  • Business process planning: mapping how work flows (for example order to delivery), removing delays and waste, setting standards and redesigning processes (business process reengineering).

Worked example

Example: the bullwhip effect

Customer demand for a biscuit rises 10% during a festival. The retailer, fearing shortage, raises its orders 20%; the distributor, seeing that jump, orders 40% more; the factory schedules 60% more production. After the festival, everyone is overstocked. Sharing real point-of-sale data along the chain (and vendor-managed inventory) dampens these swings.
