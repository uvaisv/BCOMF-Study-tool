Logistics Information System

Critical Asked in 9 of 9 papers · avg 15 marks

What a logistics information system is, its components and requirements, its role, information flows, internal and external information, information processing and technology applications.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 9 Logistics Information SystemBlock 2 · Unit 8 Information Monitoring

Key points

  • Logistics information system (LIS): people, procedures, hardware and software that collect, process, store and report logistics data (orders, stock, shipments, costs) so managers can plan and control the flow of goods.
  • Components: data capture (stock cards, receipts, barcodes), data processing and storage (database, software), reporting and decision support, communication network, and users. Sub-systems: order management, procurement, inventory, warehouse management (WMS) and transportation management (TMS).
  • Requirements of good logistics information: available, accurate, timely, exception-based (flags problems), flexible, and in a usable format.
  • Role: links every activity of the logistics cycle; gives visibility of stock and shipments; supports forecasting and planning; speeds order processing; controls cost; measures performance; improves customer service.
  • Information flows: planning and coordination flows (strategic objectives, capacity limits, logistics requirements, forecasts, procurement and production plans) and operational flows (order management, order processing, distribution, inventory, transport and shipping, procurement). Features: two-way, continuous, integrated across partners, increasingly real-time.
  • Internal information: sales and orders, stock levels, production schedules, costs, staff and capacity. External information: customer orders and feedback, supplier lead times and prices, carrier and shipment status, market demand, government rules.
  • Information processing: capturing, checking, storing, analysing and communicating data. It integrates all areas of logistics because one order triggers stock allocation, picking, transport, billing and after-sales records.
  • Technology applications: barcodes and RFID, EDI, GPS and GIS vehicle tracking, ERP, WMS and TMS software, cloud platforms, IoT sensors for cold chains, analytics and AI for demand forecasting and route planning, drones and robotics in warehouses, blockchain for traceability. In India: FASTag, e-way bill, Unified Logistics Interface Platform (ULIP).
  • The book names three flows in logistics: product flow (goods from supplier to customer, plus returns), information flow (orders and delivery status) and finance flow (payment schedules, credit terms, title). Information links all the others.

Worked example

Example: one order through a logistics information system

A shop orders 200 cartons online. The order-management module checks stock in the WMS and reserves it; the warehouse prints a pick list and scans barcodes as cartons are packed; the TMS books a truck and shares a GPS tracking link; the invoice is raised automatically in the ERP. The manager's dashboard flags only exceptions — say, a delivery running late — so attention goes where it is needed.

How to write the answer

Meaning → components or features as headings → role with examples (a courier's tracking system) → conclusion.

Common mistakes

  • Listing technologies without saying what each does for logistics.
