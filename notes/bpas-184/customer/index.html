Customer Service, Satisfaction & Success

Critical Asked in 7 of 9 papers · avg 8 marks

Customer service in logistics, customer satisfaction and expectations, customer focus, customer success and customer management.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 3 · Unit 10 Customer SatisfactionBlock 3 · Unit 13 Effective Logistics Management: Challenges (13.4 Customer management)

Key points

  • Customer service in logistics: making the product available when and where the customer wants it — measured by availability (stock), operational performance (speed, consistency, flexibility, recovery from failure) and reliability.
  • Elements: pre-transaction (policies, service standards), transaction (order cycle time, stock availability, order accuracy) and post-transaction (delivery, installation, returns, complaints, warranty).
  • Customer satisfaction: the degree to which customer expectations are met. Expectations cover reliability, responsiveness, access, communication, credibility, security, courtesy, competence, tangibles and understanding the customer.
  • Customer focus: organising the whole logistics system around what customers need. Customer satisfaction information (surveys, complaints, feedback, returns data) shows where to improve products and services.
  • Customer success: going beyond satisfaction — helping customers achieve their own goals. Aspects: understanding the customer's objectives, smooth onboarding, proactive support, a dedicated relationship manager, value-added services, measuring outcomes (retention, repeat orders, Net Promoter Score).
  • Customer management: segmenting customers, managing relationships (CRM), handling complaints quickly, keeping loyal customers.

Worked example

Example: the three stages of customer service

Pre-transaction: an online seller publishes a clear return policy and a promise of delivery in 3 days. Transaction: the item is in stock, the order is accurate and arrives on time. Post-transaction: it handles a damaged-item complaint with a quick replacement. Customer success goes further — a B2B supplier helps a client restaurant plan its weekly orders so it never runs short.

How to write the answer

Meaning → elements or ways as headed points → example (an e-commerce delivery promise) → conclusion.
