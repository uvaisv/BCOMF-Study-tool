Voluntary Consumer Organisations & NGOs

Critical Asked in 12 of 12 papers · avg 21 marks

Voluntary consumer organisations (VCOs) and NGOs — nature, characteristics, functions and activities, role under the Consumer Protection Act, selected organisations, management in NGOs, consumer clubs, and do's and don'ts for consumer activists.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 7 Role of Voluntary Consumer Organisations (VCOs)

Key points

  • VCOs and NGOs: voluntary, non-profit, independent bodies formed by citizens to protect and promote consumer interests.
  • Characteristics of NGOs: voluntary membership, non-profit, independent of government, service-oriented, flexible and close to people, dependent on donations and grants.
  • Functions and activities: consumer education and awareness, complaint handling and legal help, filing cases on behalf of consumers, product testing and comparative reports, publications, representation on government bodies, advocacy for better laws.
  • Role under the Consumer Protection Act: a recognised consumer association can file complaints; VCOs sit on consumer protection councils and help consumers approach consumer commissions.
  • Selected organisations: Consumer Guidance Society of India (Mumbai, 1966 — the oldest), Consumer Education and Research Centre (CERC, Ahmedabad), CUTS International (Jaipur), VOICE (Delhi), Consumer Coordination Council (a national federation of VCOs; ran the CORE centre).
  • Management in NGOs: planning, finance, people and accountability are often weak because of small size, reliance on volunteers and irregular funds; good management needs clear goals, trained staff, transparent accounts and evaluation.
  • Consumer clubs: clubs in schools and colleges, supported by the Department of Consumer Affairs, to build awareness among students.
  • Do's for a consumer activist: know the law, collect facts and documents, act through proper channels, be fair and courteous, keep records. Don'ts: make false allegations, seek personal gain, use threats or violence, publicise unverified claims.

Worked example

Example: what a voluntary consumer organisation does

A city consumer group tests ten brands of packaged drinking water in a laboratory and publishes a comparison; two brands fail purity standards. It educates residents through newsletters, helps a group of buyers file a complaint against a builder, and — as a recognised consumer association — files a complaint on their behalf before the Consumer Commission.

How to write the answer

Meaning → functions or features as headings → one Indian organisation as example → conclusion.
