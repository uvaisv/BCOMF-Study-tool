ADR: Mediation, Conciliation, Arbitration & Lok Adalats

Critical Asked in 12 of 12 papers · avg 13 marks

Mediation (types, advantages, stages), conciliation, arbitration and the law on it, Lok Adalats, and how they differ.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 3 · Unit 10 Arbitration, Mediation, Conciliation and Other Mechanisms

Key points

  • Mediation: a neutral mediator helps the parties reach their own settlement; the mediator does not impose a decision. Types: court-referred, private, community, online; consumer mediation cells exist under CPA 2019; the Mediation Act 2023 governs it.
  • Stages of mediation: opening statement by the mediator → joint session (each side explains) → separate sessions (caucus) → negotiation and options → settlement agreement → closure.
  • Advantages: quick, cheap, confidential, voluntary, preserves relationships, flexible solutions.
  • Conciliation (Arbitration and Conciliation Act 1996, Part III): the conciliator helps the parties and may propose terms of settlement; a signed settlement agreement is binding like an arbitral award. Suitable for commercial and civil disputes; not for criminal matters.
  • Conciliation vs mediation: a conciliator may suggest solutions, a mediator only facilitates; conciliation has a statutory settlement under the 1996 Act.
  • Arbitration: disputes decided by an arbitrator chosen by the parties (or appointed by the court, Sec 11); the award is binding and enforceable like a decree, with limited grounds to challenge.
  • Lok Adalats (Legal Services Authorities Act 1987): people's courts that settle disputes by compromise; no court fee, fee refunded if pending in court; the award is final with no appeal. Permanent Lok Adalats handle public utility services. Lok Adalat vs mediation: a Lok Adalat award is a decree of a civil court; mediation ends in a settlement agreement.
  • Grievances against government departments can be lodged online on the Public Grievances (CPGRAMS) portal.

Worked example

Example: mediation vs arbitration vs Lok Adalat

A dispute over a delayed flat: in mediation, a neutral mediator helps the buyer and builder agree on compensation themselves; nothing is imposed. In conciliation, the conciliator may also suggest settlement terms. In arbitration, under a clause in their agreement, an arbitrator hears both sides and gives a binding award. At a Lok Adalat, the matter is settled by compromise; the award is final, with no appeal, and any court fee paid is refunded.
