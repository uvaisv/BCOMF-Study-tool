Advertising, Media & Misleading Advertisements

Critical Asked in 12 of 12 papers · avg 19 marks

Objectives and impact of advertisements, misleading advertisements, laws governing advertising, ASCI and its code, and the role of media in society and consumer protection.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 1 · Unit 1 Role of Media and its Impact on ConsumersBlock 1 · Unit 2 Misleading Advertisement: Regulatory Mechanism

Key points

  • Objectives of advertising: inform (new products, features, prices), persuade (build preference), remind (keep the brand in mind).
  • Positive impact: information and choice, lower prices through mass production, supports media, promotes competition. Negative impact: misleading claims, creates artificial wants and materialism, targets children, stereotypes, raises costs, harmful products glamorised.
  • Misleading advertisement (CPA 2019, Sec 2(28)): falsely describes a product or service, gives a false guarantee, conveys a representation amounting to an unfair trade practice, or deliberately hides important information.
  • Action under CPA 2019: the Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) can order withdrawal or correction and impose penalties on manufacturers and endorsers (up to ₹10 lakh, ₹50 lakh for repeat offences; endorsers can be barred); false or misleading ads can also bring imprisonment. CCPA issued guidelines on misleading ads and endorsements (2022). The Department of Consumer Affairs also runs the GAMA portal for complaints.
  • Other laws: Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act 1954, Cable Television Networks (Regulation) Act 1995 and its Advertising Code, Indecent Representation of Women (Prohibition) Act 1986, COTPA 2003 (tobacco), FSSAI advertising and claims regulations 2018, Emblems and Names Act 1950.
  • ASCI (Advertising Standards Council of India, 1985): the industry's self-regulatory body. Code: ads must be honest and truthful, not offensive to public decency, not promote harmful products or situations, and be fair in competition. Its Consumer Complaints Council decides complaints.
  • Media and society: media informs, educates, shapes opinion and acts as a watchdog; businesses use it to reach and persuade consumers; its enhanced role today comes from the spread of TV, internet and social media, and its power to expose unfair practices and raise consumer awareness.
  • Complaints about an advert can go to ASCI's Consumer Complaints Council, which decides whether it breaks the ASCI Code and asks the advertiser to change or withdraw it.
  • The Press Council of India (a statutory body under the Press Council Act, 1978) sets norms of journalistic conduct and hears complaints against newspapers, including on objectionable advertisements.

Worked example

Example: a misleading advertisement and what can be done

A hair-oil advertisement claims it 'regrows hair in 7 days, clinically proven', but there is no such study. This is a misleading advertisement under the CPA 2019. A consumer or a consumer group can complain to the Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA), which can order the ad withdrawn or corrected and fine the manufacturer and the celebrity endorser — up to ₹10 lakh for a first offence. An endorser who did not take due care to check the claim can also be barred from endorsing for a period.
