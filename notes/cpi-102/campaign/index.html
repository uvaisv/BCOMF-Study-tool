Campaign & Advocacy Programmes

High Asked in 9 of 12 papers · avg 12 marks

Meaning of campaign and advocacy, public and private advocacy, requisites, strategies, techniques (information and publicity), steps of an effective programme, and written protests.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 4 · Unit 11 Strategies (Campaign and Advocacy)

Key points

  • Campaign: an organised, time-bound effort to create awareness or change behaviour. Advocacy: persuading decision-makers to change laws, policies or practices.
  • Types of advocacy: public advocacy (open campaigns, media, rallies, petitions), private advocacy (meetings and representations to officials), self-advocacy, legal advocacy, media advocacy.
  • Requisites: clear goal, research and facts, target audience, credible message, allies and networks, resources, timing, evaluation.
  • Strategies and techniques: information and publicity (leaflets, press releases, social media), education, lobbying, coalitions, legal action, demonstrations and written protests.
  • Steps: identify the issue → research → set objectives → identify targets and allies → frame the message → choose methods → act → monitor and evaluate.
  • Written protests: letters, memoranda and petitions to companies or authorities — a peaceful, documented way to press consumer demands.

Worked example

Example: an advocacy campaign step by step

Issue: schools selling sugary drinks in canteens. Research: survey 20 schools and gather health data. Objective: a state rule banning such drinks near schools. Targets: the education department; allies: parents' associations and doctors. Message: 'Healthy canteens, healthy children.' Methods: petitions, press releases, meetings with officials (private advocacy) and a social-media campaign (public advocacy). Evaluate: was the rule issued, and are canteens following it?

How to write the answer

Meaning → types or steps as headings → a consumer example → conclusion.
