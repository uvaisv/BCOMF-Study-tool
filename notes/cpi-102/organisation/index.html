Setting Up & Running a Consumer Organisation

High Asked in 9 of 12 papers · avg 11 marks

Basic requirements for a consumer organisation, objectives, membership and registration, managerial processes and the alternative view, dynamics and structure, making it viable, and guidelines for public meetings.

In your IGNOU bookBlock 2 · Unit 6 Establishing a Consumer OrganisationBlock 4 · Unit 12 Managing an Organisation

Key points

  • Basic requirements: clear objectives, committed members, a written constitution, registration (Societies Registration Act 1860, a trust, or a Sec 8 company), leadership, funds, an office, staff or volunteers, records and accounts.
  • Objectives: protect consumer rights, educate and inform, help with complaints, represent consumers, test products, work with government.
  • Managerial processes: planning, organising, staffing, directing, coordinating and controlling. The alternative view stresses participation, flexibility, teamwork and networking rather than rigid hierarchy.
  • Dynamics and structurisation: as an organisation grows it needs clearer roles, committees and procedures without losing its voluntary spirit.
  • Making it viable and effective: diverse funding, trained volunteers, credibility through accurate work, networking with other groups, use of media, regular evaluation.
  • Guidelines for public meetings: clear purpose and agenda, permission where needed, suitable venue and time, publicity, expert speakers, orderly conduct, record and follow-up.

Worked example

Example: starting a consumer club in a town

Five committed residents write objectives (educate consumers, help with complaints), adopt a constitution and register as a society under the Societies Registration Act 1860. They elect office-bearers, set up committees for complaints and awareness, raise funds from membership and small grants, train volunteers, and partner with the local newspaper. As they grow they add clearer roles and procedures while keeping their voluntary spirit.
